# The lexicon of Biblical Aramaic

The vocabulary of the Aramaic chapters is small: 698 distinct lemmas (647 of them nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs, proper names included) in 7 549 words (MACULA units, counted 2026-10-17). Most of it is common Aramaic; a striking share comes from **Akkadian** (via the Assyrian and Babylonian administration) and **Old Persian** (via the Achaemenid chancery), with a handful of **Greek** words in Daniel 3. This file gives the core vocabulary by frequency, the loanwords by source, the Hebrew–Aramaic cognate pairs a reader needs, and the Aramaic words that entered Hebrew.

Sources: BDB 1906, Aramaic part (Sefaria `BDB_Aramaic_Dictionary.jsonl`, public domain; cited as BDB-Aram s.v.); Kaufman 1974, *The Akkadian Influences on Aramaic*; the Iranian etymologies of Andreas and Scheftelowitz as cited inside BDB-Aram; Hinz 1975, *Altiranisches Sprachgut der Nebenüberlieferungen*; Vogt 2011, *A Lexicon of Biblical Aramaic* (English edition of *Lexicon linguae aramaicae Veteris Testamenti*, 1971); Koehler and Baumgartner, *HALOT* vol. 5 (Aramaic). Counts: MACULA, 2026-10-17.

## 2. Hebrew and Aramaic: cognates and false friends

A reader who knows Hebrew recognises about half of Biblical Aramaic through the regular sound correspondences ([script-phonology.md](script-phonology.md) §4). The rest is either a different word for the same thing or the same word with another meaning.

## 3. Loanwords

### 3.1 From Akkadian

Akkadian, the language of Assyria and Babylonia, gave Aramaic administrative, architectural and religious words in the Neo-Assyrian and Neo-Babylonian periods. The standard study is Kaufman 1974; BDB-Aram marks most of these as "loan-word from Assyrian".

| Aramaic | meaning | Akkadian | source (BDB-Aram s.v.; Kaufman 1974) |
|---|---|---|---|
| אִגְּרָה | letter | *egirtu* | BDB-Aram s.v.; Kaufman 1974 |
| אַתּוּן | furnace | *utūnu* | BDB-Aram: "prob. loan-word from Assyrian utûnu" |
| הֵיכַל | palace, temple | *ekallu* (from Sumerian É.GAL "big house") | BDB-Aram: "Assyrian loan-word" |
| פֶּחָה | governor | *bēl pīḫati* | BDB-Aram: "Assyrian loan-word" |
| סְגַן | prefect | *šaknu* | BDB-Aram: "Assyrian loan-word" |
| מִנְדָּה, מִדָּה | tribute | *mandattu* | BDB-Aram: "Assyrian loan-word" |
| בְּלוֹ | tribute, tax | *biltu* | BDB-Aram: "from Assyrian biltu, according to most" |
| הֲלָךְ | toll, a tax | *ilku* (feudal duty) | Kaufman 1974 |
| אֹשׁ | foundation | *uššu* | BDB-Aram: "prob. loan-word from Assyrian uššû" |
| אָשַׁף | conjurer | *āšipu* | BDB-Aram: "prob. Bab. loan-word" |

Checked 2026-10-18 (second pass) against Marti 1896, whose glossary carries the Iranian notes of F. C. Andreas (from p. 51*): סְגַן is Assyrian *šaknu* (p. 73*) and כַּרְבְּלָא is linked to Assyrian *karballatu* (glossary s.v.); the month name אֲדָר is Assyrian (p. 51*). Disagreement: Marti derives אִגְּרָה from Middle Iranian *hangert* "report" (with Greek ἄγγαρος "courier"; p. 51*), not from Akkadian *egirtu*; and he explains סָפְרָא in Ezra 4:8 as a rendering of the Persian title *dipīr* "scribe" (p. 74*), not as a loan of Akkadian *šāpiru*. The other Akkadian etymologies rest on BDB-Aram and Kaufman 1974 and were not found in a grammar on disk (unchecked).

### 3.3 From Greek

Only in Daniel 3, the list of musical instruments played before the golden image (Dan 3:5, 7, 10, 15). These are among the arguments for a Hellenistic date of the tale (Collins 1993; Rosenthal 1961).

| Aramaic | meaning | Greek | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| קַתְרוֹס (Ketiv קיתרוס) | lyre, zither | κίθαρις | BDB-Aram s.v. |
| פְּסַנְתֵּרִין | harp, psaltery | ψαλτήριον | BDB-Aram s.v. |
| סוּמְפֹּנְיָה | bagpipe, double pipe (or "in unison") | συμφωνία | BDB-Aram s.v. |
| כָּרוֹז | herald | κήρυξ (some derive it from Old Persian) | BDB-Aram s.v. (with doubt) |

Two other instrument names are Semitic: קַרְנָא "horn" and מַשְׁרוֹקִיתָא "pipe, whistle" (√ שׁרק "whistle"); שַׂבְּכָא "trigon" may itself have given Greek σαμβύκη (BDB-Aram s.v.). The WLC writes it with ס in Dan 3:5 (סַבְּכָא) and with שׂ in 3:7, 10, 15.

Checked 2026-10-18 (second pass): Krauss 1898–1899 has the three Greek instruments and the herald: קַתְרוֹס = κίθαρις, "already Dan 3:5, 7, 10", Qere קַתְרוֹס, Ketiv קיתרוס (II, p. 573); פְּסַנְתֵּרִין = ψαλτήριον, with the spelling פְּסַנְטֵרִין of Dan 3:7 (II, p. 473); סוּמְפֹּנְיָה = συμφωνία "bagpipe", Dan 3:5, 10, 15 (II, p. 376); כָּרוֹזָא = κῆρυξ, "already Dan 3:4" (II, p. 296, where a bracketed note records Hoffmann's doubt about the Greek origin). Marti 1896 §88, p. 92, gives the same four as Greek, with the variants סוּפֹּנְיָה and סיפניה (the Ketiv of Dan 3:10). Disagreement on שַׂבְּכָא: Marti (§88 and glossary: = σαμβύκη, a kind of harp) and Krauss (I, p. 6) count it as a Greek loan, where BDB-Aram takes the Greek word to come from the Semitic one.


### 3.4 Hebrew words in Biblical Aramaic

Some words are Hebrew rather than Aramaic, by origin or by Masoretic vocalisation: מַלְכִים (Ezra 4:13, Hebrew plural), נְבִיָּא "prophet" (Ezra 5:1), כָּהֲנַיָּא "the priests" (Ezra 7:24), לֵוָיֵא "the Levites", נְתִינַיָּא "the temple servants" (Ezra 7:24, from Hebrew נְתִינִים; BDB-Aram s.v.), אֵלֶּה "these" (Jer 10:11), and religious terms like קָרְבָּן, עֲלָוָן "burnt offerings" (Ezra 6:9).

## 4. Aramaic in Hebrew

The influence also went the other way. Late Biblical Hebrew (Esther, Chronicles, Ezra–Nehemiah, Daniel's Hebrew, Ecclesiastes) and Mishnaic Hebrew took many Aramaic words and forms (Kautzsch 1902, *Die Aramaismen im Alten Testament*; Wagner 1966, *Die lexikalischen und grammatikalischen Aramaismen im alttestamentlichen Hebräisch*):

| Hebrew | from Aramaic | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| שֶׁ־ (relative) | (parallel to) דִּי | that, which — not a loan, but it spreads with the Aramaic model |
| אִגֶּרֶת | אִגְּרָה | letter |
| זְמָן | זְמַן | appointed time |
| פִּתְגָם | פִּתְגָם | decree, sentence |
| קִבֵּל | קַבֵּל (pael) | receive |
| סוֹף | סוֹף | end |
| מַלְכוּת | מַלְכוּ | kingdom |
| -וּת abstracts | -וּ, -וּת | abstract nouns |
| רָז | רָז | secret (Mishnaic and modern Hebrew) |
| אַבָּא, אִמָּא | determined forms | father, mother (Mishnaic, modern) |
| סְלִק → סָלַק | סְלִק | go up, remove |
| טַעַם (Jon 3:7, "decree") | טְעֵם | reason, decree |

Checked 2026-10-18 against Kautzsch 1902, *Übersicht* of the certain Aramaisms (pp. 93–98) and the list of doubtful ones (pp. 105–111): זְמָן (p. 94: Koh 3:1; Est 9:27, 31; Neh 2:6), טַעַם "decree" (p. 94: Jon 3:7), קִבֵּל (p. 97: Prov 19:20; Job 2:10; Est 4:4 and more), סוֹף (p. 96: Joel 2:20; Koh 3:11 and more) and סָלַק (p. 96: Ps 139:8) are among the certain Aramaisms; Kautzsch also lists the relative שֶׁ only for particular constructions (p. 98). Corrected: the טַעַם row had the Hebrew and Aramaic forms swapped. אִגֶּרֶת, פִּתְגָם, מַלְכוּת and the ־וּת abstracts are in neither list of Kautzsch 1902 (unchecked: their status as Aramaisms rests on Wagner 1966, not on disk; no work of the second pass covers Hebrew Aramaisms); רָז, אַבָּא, אִמָּא are Mishnaic and outside his scope.

Through Mishnaic Hebrew many of these words are part of Modern Hebrew (`he`), which is why a Hebrew model will meet Aramaic forms (אַבָּא, סַבָּא, אֲגַב, בְּדִיעֲבַד, הֶקֵּשׁ) in ordinary modern text ([../tmr/lexicon.md](../tmr/lexicon.md) §6).
