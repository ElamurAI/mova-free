# The noun in Biblical Aramaic

Nouns and adjectives inflect for **gender** (masculine, feminine), **number** (singular, plural, a few duals) and **state** (absolute, construct, determined). There is no case and no prefixed article. This file describes Biblical Aramaic (`arc`); the later varieties change mostly the use of the states and the plural endings — see the table in [family.md](family.md) §5 and the folders of each variety.

References: Rosenthal 1961, *A Grammar of Biblical Aramaic* (the chapter on nouns); Bauer and Leander 1927, *Grammatik des Biblisch-Aramäischen*, § 51 on noun formation and §§ 52–67 on gender, number, state, the inflection classes and the numerals; Kautzsch 1884, *Grammatik des Biblisch-Aramäischen*, part on the noun; Johns 1972, lessons 3–6; Greenspahn 2003, *An Introduction to Aramaic*, ch. 6–9. Counts: MACULA Hebrew, Aramaic words (`lang` = A), 2026-10-17. Checked 2026-10-18 against Strack 1901 §§ 7–10 (pp. 20–24) and Kautzsch 1884 §52 (pp. 86–88): forms in the tables that these grammars mark as not attested, or that the WLC does not have, are marked `*` (regular, not attested in Biblical Aramaic).

## 1. The three states

| state | Aramaic name | form (m. sg.) | meaning | Hebrew counterpart |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **absolute** | status absolutus | bare stem: מֶלֶךְ, גְּבַר | indefinite, or predicative: "a king", "(is) great" | the bare noun מֶלֶךְ |
| **construct** | status constructus | bare stem, often reduced: מֶלֶךְ, גְּבַר, בֵּית | bound to a following noun: "king of …" | the construct מֶלֶךְ, בֵּית |
| **determined** (emphatic) | status determinatus / emphaticus | stem + ־ָא *-ā*: מַלְכָּא, גַּבְרָא | definite: "the king" | הַמֶּלֶךְ |

The **determined state** does the work of the Hebrew article. In Biblical Aramaic it is still fully definite: מַלְכָּא is "the king", never "a king". In Syriac and JBA it lost that meaning and became the normal form of every noun ([../tmr/nouns.md](../tmr/nouns.md)).

In MACULA's tagging of Biblical Aramaic nouns and adjectives (2 149 words with a state; 2026-10-17):

| state | m. sg. | m. pl. | f. sg. | f. pl. | all (incl. words of common or unknown gender) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| determined | 478 | 183 | 138 | 24 | 837 |
| construct | 439 | 129 | 122 | 36 | 729 |
| absolute | 247 | 122 | 118 | 33 | 583 |

The construct is frequent because names of places and titles are written as chains (בֵּית אֱלָהָא "the house of God", מֶלֶךְ מַלְכַיָּא "king of kings"), but the genitive with **דִּי** is the more Aramaic construction (§5).

## 2. Endings

### 2.1 The basic endings

| | masculine | feminine |
|---|---|---|
| sg. absolute | — (מֶלֶךְ) | ־ָה / ־ָא *-ā* (מִלָּה "word"), ־וּ *-ū* (מַלְכוּ "kingdom"), ־ִי (abstracts) |
| sg. construct | — (מֶלֶךְ) | ־ַת *-aṯ* (מִלַּת), ־וּת (מַלְכוּת) |
| sg. determined | ־ָא *-ā* (מַלְכָּא) | ־ְתָא *-tā* (מִלְּתָא), ־וּתָא (מַלְכוּתָא) |
| pl. absolute | ־ִין *-īn* (מַלְכִין) | ־ָן *-ān* (אֶצְבְּעָן "fingers", Dan 5:5), ־וָן (*מַלְכְוָן) |
| pl. construct | ־ֵי *-ē* (מַלְכֵי) | ־ָת *-āṯ* (מַלְכְוָת) |
| pl. determined | ־ַיָּא *-ayyā* (מַלְכַיָּא) | ־ָתָא *-āṯā* (מַלְכְוָתָא) |
| dual absolute | ־ַיִן *-ayin* (יְדַיִן "hands", קַרְנַיִן "horns") | |

Checked 2026-10-18: Kautzsch 1884 §52.3, p. 88 (the overview יוֹם / חֵיוָא through the three states and two numbers); Strack 1901 §7, pp. 20–21, §8d, m, pp. 22–23.

Remarks:

- The masculine plural **־ִין** is the Aramaic counterpart of Hebrew ־ִים. In Ezra 4:13 the Hebrew-like מַלְכִים appears once, and the Ketiv/Qere system shows other Hebraisms (Rosenthal 1961).
- The determined plural **־ַיָּא** comes from *-ay* + *-ā*. It is written ־יא or ־יה: מַלְכַיָּא, עַמְמַיָּא "the peoples" (Dan 3:4), but יְהוּדָיֵא "the Jews" (Ezra 4:12; gentilic, see §4).
- Nouns in final **־ֵה** or **־ָא** that come from roots III-y keep the y in the plural: מָרֵא "lord" (Dan 2:47), plural determined in the form of *-ayyā*.
- Many feminine nouns take the masculine plural ending: מִלָּה "word" → מִלִּין, determined מִלַּיָּא; שְׁנָה "year" → שְׁנִין (Ezra 5:11). This is ordinary in Aramaic, as in Hebrew שָׁנָה → שָׁנִים.

### 2.2 A full paradigm: three model nouns

**מֶלֶךְ "king"** (masc., stem *malk-*):

| | singular | plural |
|---|---|---|
| absolute | מֶלֶךְ *meleḵ* | מַלְכִין *malḵīn* (Dan 2:21) |
| construct | מֶלֶךְ *meleḵ* (מֶלֶךְ־פָּרָס "king of Persia", Ezra 4:24) | מַלְכֵי *malḵē* |
| determined | מַלְכָּא *malkā* | מַלְכַיָּא *malḵayyā* (Dan 2:44; Ezra 7:12) |
| + 3ms suffix | מַלְכֵּהּ *malkēh* | מַלְכוֹהִי *malḵōhī* |

The absolute singular מֶלֶךְ is vocalised like Hebrew; the Aramaic stem *malk-* shows in all the other forms. Bauer and Leander (1927) treat such "segolate" vocalisations as Masoretic adaptations to Hebrew.

| determined | מִלְּתָא *millətā* (Dan 2:5) | מִלַּיָּא *millayyā* |

## 3. Noun patterns

Aramaic, like Hebrew, builds nouns from a consonantal root by fixed vowel patterns and affixes. The most frequent in Biblical Aramaic (Bauer and Leander 1927 § 51; Rosenthal 1961):

| pattern | examples | type of meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *qatl*, *qitl*, *qutl* | מֶלֶךְ (*malk*), טְעֵם (*ṭiʿm*) "decree, taste", צְלֵם (*ṣilm*) "image", קֻשְׁט (*qušṭ*) "truth" | basic nouns |
| *qatal* | דְּהַב "gold", גְּבַר "man", חֲסַף "clay" | basic nouns |
| *qātil* (active participle) | שָׁלִיט "ruler", כָּרוֹז (loan) | agent |
| *qaṭṭīl* | חַכִּים "wise", קַדִּישׁ "holy", תַּקִּיף "strong", יַצִּיב "certain" | adjectives of quality |
| *qaṭṭāl* | דַּבָּח? (rare); כַּשְׂדָּי is a gentilic, not this pattern | profession |
| *qĕṭīl* (passive participle) | כְּתִיב "written", יְדִיעַ "known" (Ezra 4:12), רְשִׁים "inscribed" (Dan 5:25) | result, state |
| *qəṭal* + ־וּ | מַלְכוּ "kingdom", רְבוּ "greatness" | abstract |
| *maqṭal*, *miqṭal* | מַנְדַּע "knowledge", מִשְׁכַּב "bed", מַעֲבָד "deed" (Dan 4:34) | place, instrument, abstract |
| *qəṭāl* (infinitive-like) | עֲבִידָה "work" (Ezra 4:24), שְׁלָם "peace" | action |
| reduplicated | רַבְרְבָן "great ones, nobles", רַבְרְבָנִין (Dan 5:1) | plural of רַב |
| gentilic ־ָי | כַּשְׂדָּי "Chaldean", יְהוּדָי "Jew", פַּרְסָי "Persian" (Dan 6:29) | origin |

## 5. The genitive: construct versus דִּי

Biblical Aramaic expresses "X of Y" in three ways:

1. **construct chain**, as in Hebrew: בֵּית אֱלָהָא "the house of God" (Ezra 4:24); מֶלֶךְ מַלְכַיָּא "king of kings" (Ezra 7:12).
2. **determined noun + דִּי + noun**: צֶלֶם דַּהֲבָא דִּי הֲקֵים "the golden image that he set up" (relative); and as a pure genitive: לְגֻבָּא דִּי אַרְיָוָתָא "into the den of the lions" (Dan 6:17); כְּתַל הֵיכְלָא דִּי מַלְכָּא "the wall of the king's palace" (Dan 5:5).
3. **anticipatory suffix + דִּי**: the possessed noun carries a pronoun suffix pointing forward to the possessor: שְׁמֵהּ דִּי־אֱלָהָא "his name, (that) of God" = "God's name" (Dan 2:20); בְּיוֹמֵיהוֹן דִּי מַלְכַיָּא "in their days, of the kings" = "in the days of the kings" (Dan 2:44). This construction is typical of Aramaic and becomes very frequent in JBA (שְׁמֵיהּ דְּ־, בְּרֵיהּ דְּרַב "Rav's son").

The share of the three changes over time: (1) dominates in Ezra's official documents, (2) and (3) grow in Daniel and dominate in the later dialects. For a parser all three should end in the same relation (`nmod:poss` or `nmod`, see [mova.md](mova.md) §4).

```
Aramaic:   בְּיוֹמֵיהוֹן דִּי מַלְכַיָּא אִנּוּן
Translit.: bə-yōmēhōn dī malḵayyā ʾinnūn
Gloss:     in-day.M.PL.CST-3MPL REL king.M.PL.DET DEM.3MPL
English:   in the days of those kings
Source:    Dan 2:44 (WLC, public domain)
```

## 6. Adjectives

Adjectives agree with their noun in gender, number and **state**. An attributive adjective follows its noun; a predicative adjective is usually in the absolute state.

| | attributive (agreeing in state) | predicative |
|---|---|---|
| m. sg. | לְחֶם רַב "a great feast" (Dan 5:1, abs.); אֱלָהּ רַב "a great God" (Dan 2:45, abs.); לְבֵית אֱלָהָא רַבָּא "to the house of the great God" (Ezra 5:8, det. with det.) | מַלְכָּא רַב "the king is great" |
| f. sg. | קִרְיְתָא מָרָדְתָּא וּבִישְׁתָּא "the rebellious and wicked city" (Ezra 4:12, det.); בָּבֶל רַבְּתָא "great Babylon" (Dan 4:27, det.) | |
| m. pl. | מַלְכִין תַּקִּיפִין "mighty kings" (Ezra 4:20) | שְׁנִין שַׂגִּיאָן "many years" (Ezra 5:11) |
| f. pl. | חֵיוָן רַבְרְבָן "great beasts" (Dan 7:3) | |

```
Aramaic:   וּמַלְכִין תַּקִּיפִין הֲווֹ עַל־יְרוּשְׁלֶם
Translit.: ū-malḵīn taqqīp̄īn hăwō ʿal-yərūšlem
Gloss:     and-king.M.PL.ABS mighty.M.PL.ABS be.PEAL.PF.3MPL over-Jerusalem
English:   and there were mighty kings over Jerusalem
Source:    Ezra 4:20 (WLC, public domain)
```

**Comparison** has no special form. The comparative uses מִן "than": שַׂגִּיא … מִן "more than". The superlative is expressed by a construct or by context: מֶלֶךְ מַלְכַיָּא "king of kings" = the highest king; עִלָּאָה "the Most High" (Dan 4:31), the determined form of the adjective עִלַּי "high".

## 7. Numerals
