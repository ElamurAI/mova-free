# The verb in Biblical Aramaic

The Aramaic verb is built like the Hebrew one: a consonantal root (mostly three letters), a **stem** (binyan) that adds meaning (intensive, causative, passive, reflexive), and **conjugations**: the perfect (suffixes), the imperfect (prefixes), the imperative, the infinitive and two participles. This file gives the paradigms of Biblical Aramaic, the weak roots, and how the forms are used. The later varieties keep the system but shift its uses and some endings: see [../arc-x-targum/verbs.md](../arc-x-targum/verbs.md), [../tmr/verbs.md](../tmr/verbs.md), [../jpa/verbs.md](../jpa/verbs.md), [../arc-x-zohar/grammar.md](../arc-x-zohar/grammar.md).

References: Rosenthal 1961 (the verb, with full paradigms at the end of the book); Bauer and Leander 1927 §§ 26–50 (the verb); Kautzsch 1884; Marti 1896, *Kurzgefasste Grammatik der biblisch-aramäischen Sprache*; Johns 1972; Greenspahn 2003; Li 2009, *The Verbal System of the Aramaic of Daniel: Explanation and Pedagogy*; Cook 2019, *Aramaic Ezra and Daniel: A Handbook on the Aramaic Text* (Baylor). Forms are from WLC (public domain); counts from MACULA (CC BY 4.0), 2026-10-17.

**Total verb forms: 1 068.** Peal is 59% of all forms; the causative (haphel + aphel) is 16%.

Two things mark Biblical Aramaic against all later Jewish Aramaic:

1. **haphel** with ה is the normal causative, **aphel** with א is the minority (143 : 24 in MACULA). In Onkelos, JPA and JBA the causative is aphel (אַ־) almost everywhere ([family.md](family.md) §5).
2. **Internal passives** (peil, hophal) are alive. Later Aramaic replaces them with the t-stems (*ithpeel*, *ittaphal*); only the peil participle (כְּתִיב "written", יְדִיעַ "known") survives everywhere.

## 2. Peal of the strong verb

Model root כ־ת־ב "write". Where Biblical Aramaic attests a form of another root, it is cited.

Verbs with *i* in the stem (stative verbs) have כְּתִב-type perfects: יְתִב "he sat" (Dan 7:9), שְׁלֵט "he ruled", דְּחִל "he feared".

Checked 2026-10-18: Strack 1901 §14.3, p. 28, §15d–e, p. 30: all eight forms agree (the 1 sg. of the model root is marked as not attested; אִנְדַּע is).

The theme vowel varies by root: *u* (יִסְגֻּד), *a* (יִשְׁלַח, with gutturals), *i/e* (יִפֵּל "he will fall", Dan 3:6).

**Long and short forms.** The plural (and 2 f. sg.) forms have a long form with final ־ן (*-ūn*, *-īn*) and a short form without it. The short form is a **jussive** (wish, negative command) and is used before object suffixes: יֵאבַדוּ "let them perish" (Jer 10:11; BDB-Aram s.v. אבד notes "not ־וּן"), אַל־יְבַהֲלוּךְ "let (your thoughts) not alarm you" (Dan 5:10). The distinction is a remnant of the Old Aramaic and Proto-Semitic jussive; it disappears in the later dialects (Rosenthal 1961; Li 2009, ch. 4).

Checked 2026-10-18: Strack 1901 §14.2, 4–5, p. 29 (imperative, infinitive, active and passive participle as in the table; no feminine plural imperative is given, so it is marked `*`). Second pass 2026-10-18: Bauer and Leander 1927 §37c, p. 117, state that no stem attests a feminine plural imperative and leave it out of their paradigm; their list of every attested verb form (§50j–l, p. 172) has none, and Marti 1896 (Tab. II, p. 4*) gives only m. sg., f. sg. and m. pl. The shape *כְּתֻבָה is therefore a reconstruction that no grammar on disk prints (still unverified as a shape).

The peal **infinitive** has a prefix מ־ (*miqṭal*), unlike Hebrew (*qəṭōl*). It nearly always comes after ל־: לְמִבְנֵא, לְמֶעְבַּד. The infinitive of the derived stems has the ending ־ָה / ־וּת (§3).

The aphel perfect stays starred: Bauer and Leander give אַכְתִּב as attested only with a suffix (אֲקִימֵהּ, Dan 3:1; p. 121 n. 4) and star the form with ṣere; the infinitive is starred by them as well (only the construct אַחֲוָיַת, Dan 5:12, is attested; p. 121 n. 11). Disagreement: Strack §12g counts the forms with א and a strong pattern as haphel-type (syncopated haphel), Bauer and Leander as aphel. Shaphel: the imperfect and participle of the model are attested only from שׁיזב (יְשֵׁיזִב §50d, מְשֵׁיזִב §50v); Bauer and Leander also count מְסוֹבְלִין (Ezra 6:3) as a shaphel participle (§50x, p. 177); no shaphel imperative is attested (starred on p. 119).

The haphel **keeps the ה after the prefix** in the imperfect and participle (יְהַכְתֵּב, מְהַכְתֵּב), unlike Hebrew, where it drops (יַכְתִּיב, מַכְתִּיב).

## 4. Weak roots

Weak roots are those with a guttural, a glide (w, y), n, ʾ or a doubled consonant in a sensitive position. Biblical Aramaic has the same classes as Hebrew, with Aramaic outcomes.

## 5. Object suffixes

See [pronouns.md](pronouns.md) §4. Imperfects take suffixes with an energic n: תְהוֹדְעֻנַּנִי, יְשֵׁיזְבִנָּךְ, יְשֵׁיזְבִנְכוֹן.

## 6. How the forms are used

The use of the forms in Biblical Aramaic is between Imperial Aramaic and the later dialects: the perfect is the past, the imperfect the future and the modal, and the **participle** is spreading from the present into the narrative (Li 2009; Rosenthal 1961; Cook 2019).

| form | main uses | example |
|---|---|---|
| perfect | past event; completed action; performative in decrees | כְּתַבוּ אִגְּרָה "they wrote a letter" (Ezra 4:8); שָׂמֶת טְעֵם "I (hereby) issue a decree" (Ezra 6:12) |
| imperfect | future; purpose; wish; command (negated); after דִּי, הֵן | יְקִים אֱלָהּ שְׁמַיָּא מַלְכוּ "the God of heaven will set up a kingdom" (Dan 2:44) |
| jussive (short imperfect) | wish, negative command | יֵאבַדוּ "let them perish" (Jer 10:11) |
| active participle | present, habitual, imminent future; **narrative present** in the tales | עָנֵה דָנִיֵּאל וְאָמַר "Daniel answered and said" (Dan 2:20); יָדַע מָה בַחֲשׁוֹכָא "he knows what is in the darkness" (Dan 2:22) |
| הֲוָה + participle | past progressive or habitual | חָזֵה הֲוֵית "I was looking" (Dan 7:9); הֲוָא עָבֵד "he used to do" (Dan 6:11) |
| participle + הֲוָה | same, in the visions | אָתֵה הֲוָה "(one like a son of man) was coming" (Dan 7:13) |
| passive participle | resultative, state; often with לֶהֱוֵא | יְדִיעַ לֶהֱוֵא לְמַלְכָּא "let it be known to the king" (Ezra 4:12, the formula of the letters) |
| infinitive (with ל־) | purpose, complement | לְמִבְנֵא "to build"; יָכִל לְשֵׁיזָבוּתַנָא "he is able to save us" (Dan 3:17) |
| imperative | command | מַלְכָּא לְעָלְמִין חֱיִי "O king, live for ever!" (Dan 2:4) |

MACULA's tagging of the 1 068 verb forms (2026-10-17): perfect 394 (37%), active participle 263 (25%), imperfect 250 (23%), infinitive 82, passive participle 44, imperative 35. A quarter of all verbs are active participles: the participle is already a main tense, as it is in all the later dialects ([../tmr/verbs.md](../tmr/verbs.md) §6).

### 6.1 The narrative formula עָנֵה … וְאָמַר

The tales of Daniel introduce speech with two participles, "answering and saying", where Hebrew has וַיַּעַן … וַיֹּאמֶר:

```
Aramaic:   עָנֵה מַלְכָּא וְאָמַר לְכַשְׂדָּאֵי מִלְּתָא מִנִּי אַזְדָּא
Translit.: ʿānē malkā wə-ʾāmar lə-ḵaśdāʾē millətā minnī ʾazdā
Gloss:     answer.PEAL.PTCP.ACT.MS king.DET and-say.PEAL.PTCP.ACT.MS to-Chaldean.PL word.F.DET from-1SG certain
English:   The king answered and said to the Chaldeans: "The word from me is certain (firm)."
Source:    Dan 2:5 (WLC, public domain; אַזְדָּא is a Persian loan, see lexicon.md)
```

The plural form in Dan 3:16 is a perfect followed by a participle: עֲנוֹ … וְאָמְרִין "they answered … and said".

### 6.2 Decrees and letters

Imperial chancery style, preserved in Ezra and in the king's decrees in Daniel, uses fixed verb formulas:

| formula | meaning | source |
|---|---|---|
| יְדִיעַ לֶהֱוֵא לְמַלְכָּא דִּי … | "Be it known to the king that …" | Ezra 4:12, 13; 5:8 |
| מִנִּי שִׂים טְעֵם דִּי … | "From me a decree is issued that …" | Ezra 7:13; Dan 3:29; 4:3; 6:27 |
| אֲנָה דָרְיָוֶשׁ שָׂמֶת טְעֵם, אָסְפַּרְנָא יִתְעֲבִד | "I, Darius, have made a decree; let it be done with diligence" | Ezra 6:12 |
| וּכְעֶנֶת / וּכְעֶת | "and now" (start of the letter's body) | Ezra 4:10, 11, 17; 7:12 |
| שְׁלָם; שְׁלָמָא כֹלָּא | "greetings"; "all peace" | Ezra 4:17; 5:7 |
