# The verb in Jewish Palestinian Aramaic

JPA has the Aramaic stems and conjugations ([../arc/verbs.md](../arc/verbs.md)), with western forms:

1. the **3 m. pl. perfect in ־ון** (*-ūn* in Stevenson 1924; the Guggenheimer edition points ־וֹן): אַזְלוֹן "they went", אֲתוֹן "they came", הָתִיבוֹן "they objected";
2. the **imperfect with יְ־** (and נְ־ for "we"): יֵיזִיל "he will go", נִימַר "let us say" — never the JBA ל־;
3. the **1 sg. perfect in ־ִית**: חֲמִית "I saw", שְׁמָעִית "I heard", סְלִיקִית "I went up";
4. the participle with a **separate** pronoun (אַתְּ מַיִית), not fused as in JBA;
5. a frequent **ethical dative** (ל־ + a pronoun of the subject): נָחַת לֵיהּ "he went down (for himself)", נִיזוּל וְנִיחוּת לִי "let me go and go down".

References: Dalman 1905 (verb §§ 55–75); Fassberg 1991; Golomb 1985; Sokoloff 2002b. Counts: unpointed tokens in the Guggenheimer Yerushalmi (CC BY), 2026-10-17. Vowels follow the Guggenheimer vocalisation and the grammars, as reconstructions.

**No ל־ prefix.** The JBA forms are virtually absent from the Yerushalmi: לֵימָא 3 (against 903 in the Bavli), לֵיזִיל 0. A text with לֵימָא, לִיתְנֵי, לֶיהֱוֵי is Babylonian, or "Babylonianised" by a copyist ([README.md](README.md) §1).

**אִין תֵּימַר** "if you say", the Yerushalmi's way to introduce a hypothesis to be tested (JBA וְכִי תֵּימָא), is one of its most frequent phrases (481× תימר).

## 5. Participle as present; הֲוָה + participle

The participle with a **separate** pronoun is the present and the near future:

```
Aramaic:   בַּאֲדָר אַתְּ מַיִית וְנִיסָן לֵית אַתְּ חָמֵי
Translit.: ba-ʾăḏār ʾat mayyīṯ wə-nīsān lēṯ ʾat ḥāmē
Gloss:     in-Adar you die.PEAL.PTCP.ACT.MS and-Nisan NEG.EXIST you see.PEAL.PTCP.ACT.MS
English:   In Adar you will die, and Nisan you will not see
Source:    y. Maaser Sheni 4:6 (Guggenheimer edition, Sefaria, CC BY)
```

Note **לֵית** + pronoun + participle as the negated present ("you are not seeing" = "you will not see"), the western counterpart of JBA לָא + participle.

**הֲוָה + participle** is the past progressive or habitual: הֲוָה עֲסִיק "he was occupied" (y. Bava Metzia 2:5); הֲוָה יְתִיב "he was sitting" (y. Maaser Sheni 4:6); and **participle + הֲוָה**: בְּעֵי הֲוָה "he wanted" (y. Bava Metzia 2:5).

## 6. The ethical dative

A ל־ + pronoun referring to the subject itself, after verbs of motion and state, is very common in JPA narrative (Dalman 1905; Sokoloff 2002b s.v. ל־):

| example | literal | meaning | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| נִיזוּל וְנִיחוּת לִי | let me go and go down for me | I shall go back down | y. Berakhot 2:8 |
| נָחַת לֵיהּ כַּהֲנָא | Kahana went down for himself | Kahana went (back) down | y. Berakhot 2:8 |
| לְהֵן יֵיזִיל לֵיהּ | where shall he go for himself | where should he go? | y. Berakhot 2:8 |
| מַה הֲוָה מֵיזַל לֵיהּ | why was he going for himself | why did he leave? | y. Berakhot 2:8 |

For a parser this ל־ + pronoun is not an argument: UD would make it `obl` with a subtype or `expl`; the choice must be consistent ([mova.md](mova.md) §4).
