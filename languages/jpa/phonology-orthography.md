# Phonology and orthography of Jewish Palestinian Aramaic

Script and inherited consonants: [../arc/script-phonology.md](../arc/script-phonology.md). JPA texts differ from Biblical and targumic Aramaic in three ways: **gutturals are weak**, spelling is **very plene**, and endings are written in **more than one way** (־ה / ־א, ־ן / ־ם). Vowels below follow Dalman 1905, Fassberg 1991 and Sokoloff 2002b, and the Guggenheimer vocalisation of the Yerushalmi on Sefaria (CC BY); they are reconstructions.

## 1. Gutturals

Galilean speech weakened the gutturals, and the writing shows it (Kutscher 1976, ch. on phonology; Sokoloff 2002b, introduction):

| process | example | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ʾ lost at the end of a syllable, and often between vowels | רֵישׁ "head" (BA רֵאשׁ) | |
| ʾ (from ʾe-) lost at the start of common names | לִיעֶזֶר for אֱלִיעֶזֶר (y. Yevamot 12:2: דרבי ליעזר) | |
| ʿ and ḥ confused with ʾ and h | manuscript spellings that interchange ע and א, ח and ה in the same word (Kutscher 1976 lists them; Sokoloff 2002b gives the variants under each headword) | |

The rabbis themselves joked about it. b. Eruvin 53b tells of a Galilean in the market asking for **אמר**, and being answered: "Foolish Galilean, do you want *ḥamār* (an ass) to ride, *ḥamr* (wine) to drink, *ʿamar* (wool) to wear, or *ʾimmar* (a lamb) to slaughter?" — because he pronounced ḥ, ʿ and ʾ alike. The anecdote is Babylonian and comic, but it agrees with the spellings in Galilean texts.

## 2. Plene spelling

JPA manuscripts and inscriptions write vowels with ו and י more fully than any other Jewish Aramaic:

| vowel | example | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| short i written י | אִית, לֵית, דִּיהוּדָאֵי | there is, there is not, of the Jews |
| short u written ו | קוֹמֵי "before" (Yerushalmi; targum קֳדָם) | |
| ō, ū written ו | כְּדוֹן "now"; תּוּבָן "again, still" | |
| final -ē written י | מוֹדֵיי "agree (pl.)" (y. Bava Metzia 2:5) | |

## 3. Variable endings

| ending | variants | example |
|---|---|---|
| determined -ā | ־א and ־ה | Yerushalmi and inscriptions both; Neofiti mostly ־א |
| m. pl. absolute | ־ין, and Hebrew-like ־ים in some words | |
| 3 m. sg. suffix | ־יה, ־ה, ־יי | לֵיהּ, לֵה |
| final -n / -m | 3 m. pl. ־הון, 2 m. pl. ־כון; ־הום, ־כום only in the Palestinian targums | |
| "the Rabbi" | רִבִּי (Palestinian vocalisation, vs Babylonian רַבִּי) | (Guggenheimer: רִבִּי throughout) |

- Plene spelling: Dalman §12.1a, pp. 70–72: in the Palestinian Talmud and Midrash ו marks *u, o, ū, ō* and י marks *i, e*, also in closed syllables (איתעביד "was made"); final yod is often doubled. ## 4. Inscriptions

Synagogue mosaics and inscriptions of the 4th–7th c. (Hammat Gader, En Gedi, Rehov, Ḥuseifa, Beth Alpha) are the only JPA texts that come down to us **without copying**. They show: determined ־ה and ־א side by side, the formula דְּכִיר לְטַב "remembered for good" (donor formulas), plene spellings, Greek names and words, and occasional loss of gutturals. Standard edition: Naveh 1978, *On Stone and Mosaic: The Aramaic and Hebrew Inscriptions from Ancient Synagogues* (Hebrew); for the long halakhic inscription of Rehov, Sussmann 1974. We do not quote these texts here; their editions are in copyright, and the stones themselves are public monuments ([texts.md](texts.md) §5).

## 5. Phonology in brief

- **Spirantisation**: as in all Aramaic of the period; not marked in most texts.
- **Pretonic reduction**: as in other Aramaic ([../arc/script-phonology.md](../arc/script-phonology.md) §3).
- **Diphthongs**: *au* becomes *ō*, but *ai* is often kept inside the word, more than in the targums (Galilean עַיְינָא beside עֵינָא; Dalman 1905 §14.7b, p. 90). The construct בֵּית is contracted, as everywhere.
- **Assimilation of n**: as elsewhere (יִפּוֹק); but the t of the t-stems often stays: אִתְקְטִיל, and the prefix is spelled אִית־ / אִת־ (Fassberg 1991).
- **Final unstressed vowels** stay in writing more than in JBA (e.g. the perfect 3 m. pl. ־ון keeps its n).
