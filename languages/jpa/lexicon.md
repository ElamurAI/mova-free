# The lexicon of Jewish Palestinian Aramaic

JPA vocabulary is western Aramaic, shared in large part with Samaritan and Christian Palestinian Aramaic, and full of **Greek** (and some **Latin**) words: the Jews of Roman and Byzantine Palestine lived in a Greek-speaking administration and economy. This file gives the core words, a table of JPA–JBA pairs with counts, and the loanwords.

Sources: Sokoloff 2002b, *A Dictionary of Jewish Palestinian Aramaic of the Byzantine Period* (3rd ed. 2017; in copyright, wanted); Jastrow 1903 (public domain; cited s.v.); Krauss 1898–1899, *Griechische und lateinische Lehnwörter im Talmud, Midrasch und Targum* (public domain, on disk; see sources.md); Sperber 1984, *A Dictionary of Greek and Latin Legal Terms in Rabbinic Literature*. Counts: unpointed tokens, Guggenheimer Yerushalmi (CC BY; "Y") and Wikisource Bavli (CC BY-SA, measurement only; "B"), 2026-10-17.

## 2. Core vocabulary of the stories

From the Yerushalmi passages in [texts.md](texts.md): כִּיתְנָא "flax"; חֲמָר "donkey"; מַרְגְּלִי "pearl"; תּוּבָן "straw"; סוֹגִּין "much" (*sōgīn*); לָעֵי "toil"; זְבַן "buy"; עֲסִיק "occupied"; אַרְפֵּי "let go"; חֶלְמָא "dream" (בְּחֶילְמַאי "in my dream"); קוֹפָּד "meat" (Jastrow s.v. קוּפְדָא); מוֹעֲדָא "festival"; רִגְלִי "festival" (lit. "foot, pilgrimage"); קָלָא "voice, news"; גְּזַר דִּין "decree, sentence"; צוֹם "fast"; עוּלֶם "young man".

II page = PDF page − 412 of the scan on disk): אִיקוֹנִין εἰκόνιον (II 40), פַּלְטִין παλάτιον / *palatium* (II 457), קֵיסָר Καῖσαρ (II 535, with jBerakhot 12d), לִגְיוֹן *legio* (II 304–305), אִיסְטְרַטְיוֹט στρατιώτης, "soldier, also officer" (II 84, with jSheqalim 49a), פְּרַקְלִיט παράκλητος (II 496), קָטֵיגוֹר κατήγορος (II 524–525), סָנֵיגוֹר συνήγορος (II 404), פְּרַגְמַטְיָא πραγματεία (II 478), אַרְכוֹן ἄρχων (II 129), דּוּכָּס δούξ (II 189–190), מַרְגְּלִיתָא μαργαρίτης (II 350–351), אֲוִירָא ἀήρ (II 18): all agree with Jastrow's etymologies. Two differ: for בַּרְבְּרוֹן Krauss separates the genitive plural βαρβάρων of the midrashim (Gen. R., Lev. R., Lam. R. introduction) from the y. Bava Metzia 8c word, which he takes as a singular "barbarian" built by analogy (II 163), as the Guggenheimer reading "was he a barbarian?" also requires; and for פְּרוֹזְבּוּל he derives the word from προσβολή "delivery (of the document)" and records the Talmud's own πρὸς βουλευταῖς (bGittin 36b), while Löw's note in the same entry still allows πρὸς βουλήν (II 482): Jastrow's πρὸς βουλῇ and Krauss's προσβολή stand side by side. סִירְקַאי is not in Krauss (unchecked; it stays uncertain). Kohut's *Aruch completum* was not needed for these words.

Notes: the Greek words are used in Aramaic grammar: with the determined ending (קֵיסָרָא), Aramaic plurals (אִיקוֹנִין), and suffixes. Their spelling varies widely in the manuscripts. A Mova lexicon should keep the Greek source word and the dictionary that proposes it.

## 4. Hebrew in JPA

The Yerushalmi is more Hebrew than the Bavli: many statements of the Palestinian amoraim are in Mishnaic Hebrew, and the Aramaic of its anonymous layer is shorter. In the Guggenheimer text, the Hebrew article and Hebrew function words are very frequent; a per-sentence classifier must expect long Hebrew stretches with short Aramaic links (אֲמַר לֵיהּ, הָדָא אָמְרָה, תַּמָּן תַּנִּינָן). The midrashim (Genesis Rabbah, Leviticus Rabbah) are mostly Hebrew with Aramaic stories.

## 5. JPA and the Zohar

Some formulas of the Zohar are JPA, not Babylonian: **כְּמָה דְּאַתְּ אָמַר** "as you say" for introducing a verse (72× in the Yerushalmi; the Zohar uses it constantly), and the Palestinian targums' expansions (the Cain and Abel dispute) are part of the midrashic background of the Zohar.
