# Jewish Palestinian Aramaic texts, annotated

Passages from the Palestinian targums and the Jerusalem Talmud. Sources and licences:

- **Fragment targum** ("Targum Jerusalem on Torah" in Sefaria's tree): public domain.
- **Pseudo-Jonathan** ("Targum Jonathan on Genesis" in Sefaria's tree): public domain; a mixed text (Onkelos + Palestinian material), see [README.md](README.md) §1.
- **Jerusalem Talmud**, Guggenheimer edition (Berlin: De Gruyter, 1999–2015) on Sefaria: **CC BY** — quoted with attribution; its vocalisation is the editor's, and we follow it in the transliteration.
- Targum Neofiti (Sefaria file "The Vatican Manuscript of the Targum Neofiti", licence unknown → private) is **not quoted**.

## 1. "In wisdom": the Fragment targum on Gen 1:1

```
Aramaic:   בְּחוּכְמָא בְּרָא יְיָ
Translit.: bə-ḥūḵmā bərā yəyā
Gloss:     in-wisdom.DET create.PEAL.PF.3MS LORD
English:   With wisdom the LORD created …
Source:    Fragment targum, Gen 1:1 (Sefaria "Targum Jerusalem on Torah", public domain)
```

Notes: בְּרֵאשִׁית is read through Prov 3:19 ("the LORD founded the earth by wisdom") and the identification of "beginning" with Wisdom (Prov 8:22). Compare Onkelos בְּקַדְמִין "at first" ([../arc-x-targum/texts.md](../arc-x-targum/texts.md) §1). The plene spelling חוּכְמָא (with ו) is typical of Palestinian texts.

## 2. Cain and Abel: Gen 4:8 in the Palestinian tradition

Onkelos leaves the Hebrew gap ("Cain said to Abel …" — said what?). The Palestinian targums fill it with a theological dispute. Fragment targum:

```
Aramaic:   וַאֲמַר קַיִן לְהֶבֶל אָחוּי: אִיתָא וְנִפּוּק לְאַפֵּי בָרָא
Translit.: wa-ʾămar qayin lə-heḇel ʾāḥūy: ʾīṯā wə-nippūq lə-ʾappē ḇārā
Gloss:     and-say.PF.3MS Cain to-Abel brother-3MS: come.IMP and-go.out.IPF.1PL to-face.PL.CST field.DET
English:   Cain said to Abel his brother: "Come, let us go out into the open field."
Source:    Fragment targum, Gen 4:8 (public domain)
```

```
Aramaic:   עָנֵי קַיִן וַאֲמַר לְהֶבֶל אָחוּי: לֵית דִּין וְלֵית דַּיָּין וְלֵית עֲלָם אוֹחֲרָן
Translit.: ʿānē qayin wa-ʾămar lə-heḇel ʾāḥūy: lēṯ dīn wə-lēṯ dayyān wə-lēṯ ʿălām ʾōḥărān
Gloss:     answer.PTCP Cain and-say.PF.3MS to-Abel brother-3MS: NEG.EXIST judgment and-NEG.EXIST judge and-NEG.EXIST world other
English:   Cain answered and said to Abel his brother: "There is no judgment, there is no judge, and there is no other world."
Source:    Fragment targum, Gen 4:8 (public domain)
```

```
Aramaic:   וְלָא לְמִתַּן אֲגָר טַב לְצַדִּיקַיָּא וְלָא לְאִתְפְּרַע מִן רַשִׁיעַיָּא
Translit.: wə-lā lə-mittan ʾăḡar ṭaḇ lə-ṣaddīqayyā wə-lā lə-ʾiṯpəraʿ min rašīʿayyā
Gloss:     and-NEG to-give.PEAL.INF reward good to-righteous.M.PL.DET and-NEG to-be.repaid.ITHP.INF from wicked.M.PL.DET
English:   "… no giving a good reward to the righteous, and no exacting punishment from the wicked."
Source:    Fragment targum, Gen 4:8 (public domain)
```

```
Aramaic:   עָנֵי הֶבֶל וַאֲמַר לְקַיִן: אִית דִּין וְאִית דַּיָּין וְאִית עֲלָם אוֹחֲרָן
Translit.: ʿānē heḇel wa-ʾămar lə-qayin: ʾīṯ dīn wə-ʾīṯ dayyān wə-ʾīṯ ʿălām ʾōḥărān
Gloss:     answer.PTCP Abel and-say.PF.3MS to-Cain: EXIST judgment and-EXIST judge and-EXIST world other
English:   Abel answered and said to Cain: "There is judgment, there is a judge, and there is another world."
Source:    Fragment targum, Gen 4:8 (public domain)
```

```
Aramaic:   וַהֲווֹ תַּרְוֵיהוֹן מְנַצִּין בְּאַפֵּי בָרָא, וְקָם קַיִן עַל הֶבֶל אָחוֹי וְקָטַל יָתֵיהּ
Translit.: wa-hăwō tarwēhōn mənaṣṣīn bə-ʾappē ḇārā, wə-qām qayin ʿal heḇel ʾāḥōy wə-qāṭal yāṯēh
Gloss:     and-be.PF.3MPL two-3MPL quarrel.PAEL.PTCP.MPL in-face.PL.CST field.DET, and-rise.PF.3MS Cain against Abel brother-3MS and-kill.PEAL.PF.3MS ACC-3MS
English:   And the two of them were quarrelling in the open field; and Cain rose up against Abel his brother and killed him.
Source:    Fragment targum, Gen 4:8 (public domain)
```

Notes on the language: אָחוּי / אָחוֹי "his brother" with the western suffix ־וֹי (Onkelos אַחוֹהִי); לֵית / אִית; the object marker יָתֵיהּ (targumic, not Yerushalmi usage); הֲווֹ + participle (past progressive); עֲלָם אוֹחֲרָן "another world" (the world to come). Pseudo-Jonathan has the same dispute with additions, e.g. Cain's words מִסְתַּכֵּל אֲנָא דִבְּרַחֲמִין אִתְבְּרִי עַלְמָא "I perceive that the world was created with mercy …" and the ending וְטָבַע אַבְנָא בְּמִצְחֵיהּ וְקַטְלֵיהּ "he sank a stone into his forehead and killed him" (Sefaria "Targum Jonathan on Genesis", public domain).

Translit.: šimʿōn ben šeṭaḥ hăwā ʿăsīq bə-hāḏā ḵīṯnā
Gloss:     Simeon son.CST Shetah be.PF.3MS occupied.PTCP.PASS in-this.F flax.DET
English:   Simeon ben Shetaḥ was occupied with (trading in) flax.
Source:    y. Bava Metzia 2:5 (8c) (Guggenheimer edition, Sefaria, CC BY)
```

```
Aramaic:   אָמְרִין לֵיהּ תַּלְמִידוֹי: רִבִּי, אַרְפֵּי מִינָּךְ, וַאֲנָן זְבַנִּין לָךְ חָדָא חֲמָר, וְלֵית אַתְּ לָעֵי סוֹגִּין

```

Gloss:     what you.PL think.PTCP.MPL, Simeon ben Shetah barbarian be.PF.3MS
English:   What do you think — was Simeon ben Shetaḥ a barbarian?

Aramaic:   בְּעֵי הֲוָה שִׁמְעוֹן בֶּן שֶׁטַח מִשְׁמַע "בְּרִיךְ אֱלָהֵהוֹן דִּיהוּדָאֵי" מֵאַגַר כָּל הָדֵין עָלְמָא
Translit.: bəʿē hăwā šimʿōn ben šeṭaḥ mišmaʿ "bərīḵ ʾĕlāhēhōn də-yəhūḏāʾē" mē-ʾaḡar kol hāḏēn ʿālmā
Gloss:     want.PTCP be.PF.3MS Simeon ben Shetah hear.INF "blessed god-3MPL REL-Jew.PL" than-reward.CST all this world.DET
English:   Simeon ben Shetaḥ preferred to hear "Blessed is the God of the Jews" to all the reward of this world.
Source:    y. Bava Metzia 2:5 (Guggenheimer edition, CC BY)
```

Notes: every JPA mark is here — 3 m. pl. perfects in ־וֹן (אַזְלוֹן, זְבַנּוֹן, אֲתוֹן), לוֹן "to them", כְּדוֹן "now", the indefinite חָדָא / חַד, לֵית + pronoun + participle, הָדָא / הָדֵין, the suffix ־וֹי (תַּלְמִידוֹי), the Greek loan בַּרְבְּרוֹן, and the infinitive מִשְׁמַע "to hear". ## 6. Synagogue inscriptions

Aramaic dedicatory inscriptions in the mosaic floors of Galilean and Judean synagogues (4th–7th c.) use a fixed formula: the donor is "remembered for good" (דְּכִיר לְטַב / דְּכִירִין לְטַב) for what he gave, often with "amen" and "peace". The long inscription of the **Rehov** synagogue (6th–7th c.) lists, in Hebrew and Aramaic, the rules of tithes and the sabbatical year by place; the **En Gedi** inscription curses whoever "reveals the secret of the town". These are the only JPA texts without manuscript transmission. We describe them rather than quote: the editions (Naveh 1978; Sussmann 1974) are in copyright, and no public-domain transcription is on disk ([sources.md](sources.md), wanted).
