## 2. Studies (cited from the literature; none on disk)

- Scholem, G. 1941. *Major Trends in Jewish Mysticism.* Jerusalem: Schocken (rev. eds. 1946, 1954). Lectures 5–6 on the Zohar, its language and its author. In copyright.
- Scholem, G. 1974. *Kabbalah.* Jerusalem: Keter (collected encyclopedia articles, including "Zohar"). In copyright.
- Tishby, I. 1989. *The Wisdom of the Zohar: An Anthology of Texts.* Transl. D. Goldstein. 3 vols. Oxford: Littman Library (Hebrew original *Mishnat ha-Zohar*, 1949–1961). In copyright.
- Liebes, Y. 1976. *Peraqim be-millon sefer ha-Zohar* (Sections of the Zohar Lexicon). PhD diss., Hebrew University. In copyright.
- Liebes, Y. 1993. *Studies in the Zohar.* Albany: SUNY Press. In copyright.
- Matt, D. C., et al., transl. 2004–2017. *The Zohar: Pritzker Edition.* 12 vols. Stanford: Stanford University Press. In copyright.
- Huss, B. 2016. *The Zohar: Reception and Impact.* Oxford: Littman Library. In copyright.
- Meroz, R. Studies on the composition of the Zohar (various articles; see Huss 2016 for references). In copyright.
- Wikipedia, "Zohar", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zohar — for the publication history (Mantua and Cremona editions); consulted for orientation only.

## 3. Grammars of the source dialects (on disk)

There is no grammar of Zoharic Aramaic on disk. The attributions of Zoharic forms to their source dialects in [grammar.md](grammar.md) were checked on 2026-10-18 in the public-domain grammars of those dialects, read from the page images: Dalman 1905 (Onkelos and Galilean), Stevenson 1924 (Palestinian Talmud and Midrash), Margolis 1910 (JBA), Strack 1901 (Biblical Aramaic). Paths and licences: [../arc/sources.md](../arc/sources.md) §3.

**Classical sources cited:** b. Shabbat 33b (the cave story); Dan 7:9; Song 2:2.
