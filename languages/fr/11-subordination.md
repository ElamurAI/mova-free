# 11. Subordination and the subjunctive

A French subordinate clause is introduced by a word that says what kind of clause it is:
- *que* for a complement clause;
- a relative pronoun for a relative clause;
- *si* for a condition;
- a conjunction for an adverbial clause (*quand, parce que, pour que, bien que* …).

Unlike English, the introducing word is never left out: *Je sais **qu'**il viendra*, never \**Je sais il viendra*; *l'homme **que** j'ai vu*, never \**l'homme j'ai vu*. The mood of the subordinate verb, indicative or subjunctive, is chosen by the word that governs the clause, and the tense follows the tense of the main clause. This chapter gives the four kinds of clause, the subjunctive and when it is required, conditions and the sequence of tenses, and the infinitive clauses that replace a finite clause when the subjects are the same.

## 11.1 Complement clauses with *que*

A clause with *que* is the object or the subject of a verb, or the complement of a noun or an adjective:

> Je pense **qu'**il a raison. — "I think (that) he is right."
> **Qu'il soit parti** m'étonne. — "That he has left surprises me." (formal; speech: *Ça m'étonne qu'il soit parti*)
> l'idée **que** tout est possible — "the idea that everything is possible"

*Que* elides to *qu'* before a vowel. It is repeated before each coordinated clause: *Il dit **qu'**il viendra et **qu'**il restera.*

## 11.2 The subjunctive

The subjunctive is the mood of a clause whose content the governing word does not present as a fact: it is wished, ordered, feared, judged, conceded or simply not asserted. Its forms are in [chapter 6](06-verb.md). In current French only two tenses are used: the present (*qu'il vienne*) and the past (*qu'il soit venu*). The imperfect and pluperfect subjunctive (*qu'il vînt, qu'il fût venu*) survive in literary prose, mostly in the third person singular.

The Latin subjunctive covered more ground (purpose, result, indirect questions, indirect discourse) and kept a strict sequence of tenses; its retreat began in late Latin ([la chapter 10](../la/10-subordination.md), §§2, 11).

**The subjunctive is required after:**

| governing word | examples |
|---|---|
| will, wish, order, preference | *vouloir que, souhaiter que, exiger que, aimer mieux que, préférer que* |
| necessity, possibility, judgement | *il faut que, il est possible que, il est important que, il vaut mieux que, c'est dommage que* |
| emotion | *être content que, regretter que, avoir peur que* (with expletive *ne*: [chapter 8](08-negation.md)), *s'étonner que* |
| doubt and denial | *douter que, nier que*, *ne pas penser que* (see below) |
| conjunctions of purpose, concession, condition, time before | *pour que, afin que, bien que, quoique, à condition que, pourvu que, à moins que, avant que, jusqu'à ce que, sans que* |

> **fr-ud-train_05137** · UD French GSD
> Aujourd'hui, il faut qu'elle travaille sur une autre base qui est celle de la solidarité.
> "Today it must work on another basis, which is solidarity."

> Il viendra **pour que** tu ne sois pas seul. — "He will come so that you are not alone."
> **Bien qu'**il soit tard, il travaille encore. — "Although it is late, he is still working."

**The indicative stays after:**
- verbs of saying, believing and knowing used positively: *dire que, penser que, croire que, savoir que, espérer que* (*espérer* takes the indicative, unlike *souhaiter*);
- conjunctions of time other than *avant que*: *quand, lorsque, pendant que, après que*. The subjunctive after *après que* is common but disapproved by the grammars;
- conjunctions of cause and consequence: *parce que, puisque, si bien que*.

**Belief and its negation.** *Penser* and *croire* take the indicative when positive, and usually the subjunctive when negated or questioned by inversion, when the speaker does not vouch for the content:

> Je **pense** qu'il **a** raison. / Je **ne pense pas** qu'il **ait** raison. / **Penses-tu** qu'il **ait** raison ?

**Relative clauses.** A relative clause takes the subjunctive when its antecedent is sought rather than known, or after a superlative: *Je cherche un livre qui **soit** clair.* — *C'est le meilleur film que j'**aie** vu.*

**When the subjects are the same**, an infinitive replaces a subjunctive clause after most of these words: *Je veux **partir**,* not \**Je veux que je parte*; *pour **partir**, avant de **partir**, sans **partir**.* See 11.6.

On our data (the GSD training set, 2026-10-22), the subjunctive accounts for 343 of the 22 026 finite verbs (1.6%). The most frequent subjunctive lemmas are *être* (116), *avoir* (50) and *pouvoir* (26).

## 11.3 Relative clauses

| relative pronoun | function in the relative clause | example |
|---|---|---|
| *qui* | subject | *l'homme **qui** parle* |
| *que* (*qu'*) | direct object, attribute | *le livre **que** je lis* |
| *dont* | complement with *de* (of a verb, a noun, an adjective) | *le livre **dont** je parle* ("I speak of"), *l'homme **dont** le fils est venu* |
| *où* | place, and time | *la ville **où** j'habite*, *le jour **où** il est parti* |
| preposition + *qui* (persons) | other prepositional complement | *la femme **à qui** je parle* |
| preposition + *lequel, laquelle, lesquels, lesquelles* | other prepositional complement, things (and persons) | *la raison **pour laquelle** je pars*; *à* + *lequel* = *auquel*, *de* + *lequel* = *duquel* |
| preposition + *quoi* | after an indefinite or neuter antecedent | *ce **à quoi** je pense*, *de quoi vivre* |

> **fr-ud-dev_00024** · UD French GSD
> Adam est le berceau de la tribu Al Bu Said, dont est issu l'actuel sultan Qabus ibn Said.
> "Adam is the cradle of the Al Bu Said tribe, from which the present sultan Qaboos bin Said comes." (With stylistic inversion of the subject in the relative clause: [chapter 10](10-clause-structure.md).)

> **fr-ud-dev_00119** · UD French GSD
> Ces institutions prêtent à leur tour l'argent reçu à des habitants du pays dans lequel elles opèrent.
> "These institutions in turn lend the money they receive to inhabitants of the country in which they operate."

**Agreement.** The verb after *qui* agrees with the antecedent (*moi qui **suis***, *vous qui **êtes***). A past participle with *avoir* agrees with a preceding *que* ([chapter 7](07-auxiliaries-participle.md)): *les lettres **que** j'ai écrit**es***.

**Free relatives** without an antecedent take *ce* before them: ***ce qui** me plaît*, ***ce que** je veux*, ***ce dont** j'ai besoin*.

**Speech.** Speech often replaces *dont* and the prepositional relatives by *que*, sometimes with a resumptive pronoun: % *le livre **que** je t'ai parlé*, % *la fille **que** je sors avec*. The written standard rejects both.

## 11.4 Conditions and the sequence of tenses

A condition is introduced by ***si***, and the tense of the *si* clause is fixed by the meaning:

| condition | *si* clause | main clause | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| real, possible | present | present, future, imperative | *Si tu viens, je **serai** content.* |
| unreal in the present, unlikely | imperfect | present conditional | *Si tu venais, je **serais** content.* |
| unreal in the past | pluperfect | past conditional | *Si tu étais venu, j'**aurais été** content.* |

**Neither the future nor the conditional is used after *si* «if»**: \**si tu viendras*, \**si tu viendrais*. (After *si* «whether», in an indirect question, both are normal: *Je me demande s'il viendra.*) Spoken French breaks the rule (% *si j'aurais su*); the standard does not.

*Si* has other uses, and the conditional is not always the point:

> **fr-ud-dev_00831** · UD French GSD
> Si le tableau est exposé à New York, le modèle, en bois polychrome, est resté sur place.
> "While the painting is on show in New York, the model, in polychrome wood, has stayed where it was." (A *si* of contrast, not of condition.)

Other conjunctions of condition take the subjunctive (*à condition que, pourvu que, à moins que*) or the conditional (*au cas où*: *au cas où il **viendrait***).

**The sequence of tenses.** After a main verb in a past tense, a reported present becomes an imperfect, a reported past a pluperfect, and a reported future a conditional:

> Il **dit** qu'il **est** malade / qu'il **a été** malade / qu'il **sera** malade.
> Il **a dit** qu'il **était** malade / qu'il **avait été** malade / qu'il **serait** malade.

A fact still true at the moment of speaking may keep the present: *Il m'a dit que la Terre **est** ronde.*

## 11.5 Adverbial clauses

| meaning | conjunctions | mood |
|---|---|---|
| time | *quand, lorsque, pendant que, depuis que, dès que, après que, tandis que* | indicative |
| | *avant que, jusqu'à ce que, en attendant que* | subjunctive |
| cause | *parce que, puisque, comme* (at the head of the sentence), *étant donné que* | indicative |
| consequence | *si bien que, de sorte que* (result), *si … que, tellement … que* | indicative |
| purpose | *pour que, afin que, de sorte que* (aim) | subjunctive |
| concession | *bien que, quoique, encore que* | subjunctive |
| | *même si* | indicative |
| condition | *si*; *à condition que, pourvu que, à moins que* | indicative; subjunctive |
| comparison | *comme, ainsi que, plus … que* | indicative |

Several of these are built on *que*, and a conjunction repeated in coordination is shortened to *que*: *Quand il arrive et **qu'**il voit …*.

> **fr-ud-train_02243** · UD French GSD
> Si bien que le statut de Nééra dut être clarifié par le tribunal.
> "So that the status of Neaera had to be settled by the court." (A clause of consequence standing as a sentence, with a *passé simple*.)

## 11.6 Infinitive and participle clauses

French prefers an infinitive to a finite clause **when the subject is the same** as in the main clause:

> Je veux **partir**. / *Je veux **que tu partes**.*
> Il est parti **sans dire** au revoir. / *Il est parti **sans que** je le **voie**.*
> **Avant de partir**, il a fermé la porte. / ***Avant qu'**il **parte**, …*

With verbs of perception and *faire, laisser*, the infinitive takes its own subject: *Je **vois** les enfants **jouer**.* — *Il **fait** réparer la voiture.* ([chapter 7](07-auxiliaries-participle.md)).

**The gerund** *en* + present participle has the same subject as the main clause and gives time, manner or means: ***En sortant**, il a vu Marie.* — *Il apprend **en lisant**.* An **absolute participle clause** with its own subject is written French: ***Le travail terminé**, ils sont partis.*

## How Mova handles it

**In the UD trees** (UD French GSD, training set, 2026-10-22):
- **The introducing words.** *Que* and the conjunctions are `SCONJ` attached as `mark` to the verb of their clause (5 914 `mark`). A relative pronoun keeps its role inside the clause (*qui* `nsubj`, *que* `obj`, *dont* `obl` or `nmod`), and the relative clause is `acl:relcl` of its antecedent (2 884).
- **The clauses.** A complement clause is `ccomp` (1 278) and a subject clause `csubj` (172). An adverbial clause is `advcl` (2 913), an infinitive with the same subject `xcomp` (3 971), and a participle or infinitive modifying a noun `acl` (4 332).
- **The mood** is a feature of the verb: `Mood=Sub` (343) and `Mood=Cnd` (383), against 22 026 finite verbs.

**In Mova.** `en xpos-fill` maps a subjunctive and a conditional like any finite verb (`VBP`/`VBZ`/`VBD`). The mood is lost from the tags, but it stays in the FEATS of the trees. The parser learns the clause relations from the French trees. What is missing:
- **Modality in the frames.** Mova's English frames reader marks a clause as not a fact (`irrealis`, `cond`) from English words: *if*, *want to*, *would*, the implicatives. For French it needs:
  - the subjunctive and the conditional read from `Mood`;
  - *si* with the imperfect read as an unreal condition and *si* with the present as a real one;
  - the governing word read as wish, fear, doubt or necessity, from the table of 11.2.
- **The sequence of tenses** (11.4) for time: in *Il a dit qu'il était malade* the illness is at the time of saying, not before it. The English reader handles reported speech; the French tense mapping is still to write.
- ***Dont* and the prepositional relatives** have no French role mapping in the frames yet: the antecedent must fill the role the relative pronoun has inside the clause (*le livre dont je parle* → *parler de livre*).
