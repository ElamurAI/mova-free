# 5. Adjectives and adverbs

The French adjective agrees with its noun in gender and number, wherever it stands in the sentence, and its default place is **after** the noun, against the English default. A short list of common adjectives goes before the noun, and for some of them the position changes the meaning. Adverbs do not agree; the large class in *-ment* is derived from the feminine of the adjective. This chapter covers agreement, position, comparison, and the formation and position of adverbs.

## 1. Agreement

An attributive adjective (*un livre rouge*), a predicate adjective (*La maison est grande*) and an adjective in apposition (*Fatiguée, elle est rentrée*) all agree in gender and number with the noun or pronoun that they qualify. The agreement is a property of the written form: for a very large number of adjectives the feminine and masculine, and the singular and plural, sound the same ([ch. 2](02-noun.md), §§4–5), which is why agreement mistakes are mostly spelling mistakes in French.

An adjective that modifies several nouns is plural; if the nouns differ in gender the adjective is masculine (*des hommes et des femmes contents*), and the masculine is also the rule for a mixed group. An adjective that modifies two singular nouns joined by *ou* may agree with the nearer. Adjectives that are used as adverbs, and adjectives formed from nouns of colour (*des yeux marron*, *des robes orange*) or compound adjectives (*des cheveux bleu clair*), do not agree. *Demi* and *nu* are invariable before the noun (*une demi-heure*, *nu-pieds*) and agree after it (*une heure et demie*, *les pieds nus*). *Tout* as an adverb agrees before a feminine adjective that begins with a consonant (*toute petite*, fr-ud-train_01942, *une toute petite île*, fr-ud-train_07397) and is invariable before a masculine one or a vowel (*tout petit*, fr-ud-train_08155).

## 2. The feminine and plural of adjectives

The rules are those of the noun ([ch. 2](02-noun.md)): *petit* → *petite*; *heureux* → *heureuse*; *actif* → *active*; *premier* → *première*; *beau* → *belle*; *nouveau* → *nouvelle*; *vieux* → *vieille*; *fou* → *folle*; *mou* → *molle*; *long* → *longue*; *blanc* → *blanche*; *frais* → *fraîche*; *doux* → *douce*; *faux* → *fausse*; *gentil* → *gentille*. Four adjectives have a second masculine form before a vowel: *beau/bel*, *nouveau/nouvel*, *vieux/vieil*, *fou/fol* (*un bel arbre*, *un nouvel ami*, *un vieil homme*). The plural is *-s*, *-x* (*beaux*, *nouveaux*), and *-aux* for most adjectives in *-al* (*national* → *nationaux*, *principal* → *principaux*, as in *transporteurs nationaux et internationaux*, fr-ud-dev_00708), with exceptions (*banal* → *banals*, *fatal* → *fatals*, *final* → *finals* or *finaux*).

## 3. Position

**Default: after the noun.** Adjectives of colour, shape, nationality, religion, technical and relational adjectives, and adjectives derived from a participle follow the noun: *une voiture rouge*, *un tapis rond*, *un homme français*, *un problème technique*, *une table cassée*. A modified adjective or an adjective with a complement is also after the noun: *un homme très grand*, *une femme fière de son fils*.

**Before the noun.** A short set of very common adjectives is put in front: *grand*, *petit*, *gros*, *jeune*, *vieux*, *nouveau*, *beau*, *joli*, *bon*, *mauvais*, *meilleur*, *autre*, *même*, *premier*, *dernier*, *haut*, *long*, *court*, *vilain*, *faux*, *vrai*. *Un petit requin* (fr-ud-dev_01053), *un grand plaisir* (fr-ud-dev_01489), *un grand nombre* (fr-ud-dev_01543), *le meilleur restaurant* (fr-ud-dev_00292). Number and the numerals come before the descriptive adjective: *les deux premiers jours*, *ces trois grandes villes*. Two adjectives that are both in the preposed set can both go before (*un joli petit village*), or one before and one after (*un beau jardin fleuri*); a pair after the noun is joined by *et* (*un homme grand et mince*).

**Position changes the sense.** For a group of adjectives the preposed use is figurative or subjective and the postposed use literal or objective:

| before | after |
|---|---|
| *un grand homme* "a great man" | *un homme grand* "a tall man" |
| *un ancien collègue* "a former colleague" | *un meuble ancien* "an old piece of furniture" |
| *un pauvre homme* "a wretched man" | *un homme pauvre* "a man without money" |
| *ma propre maison* "my own house" | *une maison propre* "a clean house" |
| *un cher ami* "a dear friend" | *un livre cher* "an expensive book" |
| *un brave homme* "a decent man" | *un homme brave* "a courageous man" |
| *un seul homme* "a single man" | *un homme seul* "a lonely man" |
| *le même jour* "the same day" | *le jour même* "that very day" |
| *un certain succès* "some success" | *un succès certain* "a sure success" |
| *différents livres* "several books" | *des livres différents* "books that differ" |

The rule of thumb that is given is that a preposed adjective is intrinsic, usual, evaluative or short, and a postposed one is distinguishing, new or long. In literary writing a normally postposed adjective can be preposed for effect (*une magnifique journée*, *de blanches colonnes*, a figure called the epithet).

## 4. Comparison and the superlative

**Comparative.** There are three degrees, formed with *plus* (more), *aussi* (as) and *moins* (less) before the adjective or adverb, and a clause or noun phrase introduced by *que* for the standard: *plus grand que moi*, *aussi accro que les drogues dures* (fr-ud-dev_00056), *un peu moins mortelle que les pires saisons grippales* (fr-ud-test_00182), *moins de temps que prévu*. In the negative and the interrogative *si* may replace *aussi*. The second term is a strong pronoun (*que moi*), never a subject clitic in the standard. After a comparative, *ne* may appear in the *que*-clause (*plus grand qu'il ne pense*; expletive *ne*, [ch. 8](08-negation.md)). The comparative of quantity is *plus de*, *moins de*, *autant de*, *tant de* ([ch. 3](03-determiners.md), §3). *Plus de dix mille bijoux* (fr-ud-dev_00993) uses *plus de* for a numerical bound.

**Irregular comparatives.** *bon* → *meilleur* (never *plus bon*), but *aussi bon*, *moins bon*; *bien* → *mieux*; *mauvais* → *pire* or *plus mauvais*; *mal* → *pis* or *plus mal*; *petit* → *moindre* or *plus petit*; *beaucoup* → *plus*; *peu* → *moins*.

**Superlative.** The relative superlative is the comparative with a definite article or a possessive in front: *le plus grand*, *la moins chère*, *mon meilleur ami*, *le puits le plus profond de France* (fr-ud-dev_00044). When the adjective follows the noun, the article is repeated (*le puits le plus profond*, *la ville la plus ancienne*); when it precedes, it is not (*la plus belle ville*). The domain is introduced by *de* (*de France*) or by *parmi*. The absolute superlative has *très*, *fort*, *bien*, *extrêmement*, *tout à fait* or a prefix *archi-*, *super-*, *ultra-*, and the suffix *-issime* on some adjectives; spoken French has *vachement*, *super*, *trop*, *hyper*.

## 5. Adjectives with complements; adjectives used as nouns and adverbs

An adjective may govern a prepositional complement that is selected by the adjective: *fier de*, *content de*, *capable de*, *désireux de*, *sûr de*, *prêt à*, *habitué à*, *conscient de*, *loin de*, *proche de*, and with *de* + infinitive or a subjunctive clause (*content de partir*, *content que tu viennes*; [ch. 11](11-subordination.md)). Nominalised adjectives follow the determiner (*le beau*, *les riches*, *un ancien*) and the nationality is a noun with a capital letter ([ch. 2](02-noun.md)). Some adjectives are also adverbs, in the masculine singular and with no agreement: *parler fort*, *voir clair*, *coûter cher*, *travailler dur*, *chanter faux*, *sentir bon*, *s'arrêter net*, *tout droit*; *Ils parlent bas*.

## 6. Adverbs: formation

Adverbs are invariable. A large productive class of manner adverbs ends in *-ment* and is built on the **feminine** of the adjective: *lent* → *lentement*, *heureux* → *heureusement*, *doux* → *doucement*, *vrai* → *vraiment*, *poli* → *poliment*. If the masculine ends in a vowel, the *-ment* is added to the masculine instead (*vrai* → *vraiment*, *poli* → *poliment*, *absolu* → *absolument*). Adjectives in *-ant* and *-ent* have *-amment* and *-emment*, both pronounced /amɑ̃/ (*constant* → *constamment*, *prudent* → *prudemment*, *évident* → *évidemment*); the exceptions include *lent* → *lentement* and *présent* → *présentement*. A few take an acute accent (*précis* → *précisément*, *énorme* → *énormément*, *profond* → *profondément*). In the treebank: *structurellement* (fr-ud-dev_00003), *véritablement* (fr-ud-dev_00009). Other adverbs are non-derived: *bien*, *mal*, *vite*, *très*, *trop*, *peu*, *beaucoup*, *assez*, *tant*, *déjà*, *encore*, *toujours*, *souvent*, *ici*, *là*, *ailleurs*; adverbial phrases are made with a preposition (*à peine*, *en vain*, *peu à peu*, *tout à fait*, *d'abord*, *à présent*).

**Degree adverbs** are *très*, *si*, *tellement*, *trop*, *assez*, *fort*, *bien* and the like. *Très* modifies adjectives and adverbs and not nouns or verbs (**Il l'aime très*, but *Il l'aime beaucoup*, *Je l'apprécie beaucoup*); the quantity adverb *beaucoup* does not modify an adjective or an adverb in the positive (**beaucoup grand*; *très grand*) but does in the comparative (*beaucoup plus grand*). *Travail de très grande qualité* (fr-ud-dev_00005).

## 7. Adverbs: position and types

**Position.** The adverb follows a finite verb in a simple tense (*Il parle bien*, *Elle voit souvent ses amis*, *On y mange correctement*, fr-ud-dev_00087), stands between the auxiliary and the participle in a compound tense for short adverbs (*Il a bien parlé*, *Il a déjà fini*, *Le gardien algérien a lui aussi laissé filer le ballon*, fr-ud-dev_00051), and follows the participle for the longer adverbs in *-ment* and the adverbs of place and time (*Il est parti hier*, *Il a parlé longuement*). The adverb stands before an adjective or another adverb that it modifies (*très grand*, *assez bien*). Sentence adverbs are at the head (*Heureusement, il est venu*), within the clause, or in final position, and are set off by commas in the first case. Unlike in English, the adverb does not precede a finite verb (**Il souvent mange du pain*) and may stand between the verb and its direct object (*Il mange souvent du pain*, where English has *He often eats bread*). After the object it is heard when the object is short and the adverb stressed (*Il mange du pain souvent*, less usual).

**Types.** Adverbs of manner (*bien*, *vite*, *lentement*), degree (*très*, *trop*), time (*hier*, *déjà*, *aujourd'hui*, *souvent*), place (*ici*, *là*, *partout*), negation (*ne … pas*; [ch. 8](08-negation.md)), interrogation (*comment*, *où*, *quand*, *pourquoi*, *combien*; [ch. 9](09-questions.md)), and sentence adverbs (*heureusement*, *peut-être*, *sans doute*, *probablement*). After *peut-être*, *sans doute*, *aussi*, *à peine*, *ainsi*, at the start of a sentence, the subject is often inverted in the formal language (*Peut-être viendra-t-il*, *Sans doute a-t-il raison*).

## 8. How Mova handles it

**What is done.** French adjectives and adverbs are read with the English tags `JJ` and `RB` (`WRB` for an interrogative or relative adverb), which the English parser attaches with the same relations as for English: `amod` for a modifier of a noun and `advmod` for a modifier of a verb or adjective. The tag set has no gender and no number for adjectives, so agreement is not visible. The position of the adjective, after the noun by default, is learnt by the parser from the treebank and is not stated as a rule; the French adjective before or after a noun gives a different tree shape from English, and the training data is the only teacher.

**UD conventions** (UD French GSD):

- An attributive adjective is `amod`, a predicate adjective is the root with `cop` or the `xcomp` of a verb (*rend aussi accro que les drogues dures*, fr-ud-dev_00056, with *accro* as `xcomp` of *rend*).
- Adverbs in *-ment* and the others are `advmod`; a degree adverb on an adjective is `advmod` of that adjective.
- **Comparison.** In the treebank the *que* of a comparative is a `case` marker (`SCONJ`) and the second term attaches to the degree word: in *aussi accro que les drogues dures* (fr-ud-dev_00056) *drogues* is `obj` of the adverb *aussi*, and in *un peu moins mortelle que les pires saisons* (fr-ud-test_00182) *saisons* is `obj` of *moins*. This is a convention of the treebank that differs from the one for the English comparative; a model trained on English will not know it. *Un peu* is a fixed expression with `advmod`, `ExtPos=ADV`.
- **Superlative.** *Le plus profond de France* (fr-ud-dev_00044) has the adjective *profond* as `nmod` of the noun *puits*, with *le* as `det` and *plus* as `advmod` of the adjective: the postposed superlative is a nominal modifier and not `amod` in this tree.
- Adjectives used as adverbs keep the tag `ADJ` or `ADV` according to the annotator.

**Measured.** The scores in this chapter are those of the whole parser, LAS 87.00 (own tags) and 89.78 (gold tags) on GSD, UD 2.18, train → dev, 2026-10-22; the tagger's accuracy on words not seen in training is 84.8%, and unknown adjectives and *-ment* adverbs are a main source of them, because French derives adverbs freely from adjectives.

**What is not handled.** Agreement checking, the preposed set and the meaning changes with position, the adjective that selects a complement, *-ment* derivation as a morphological rule (the lexicon would give an unknown *-ment* word its adjective), irregular comparatives, and the *que*-clause of comparison with expletive *ne*. A small gain is available from a suffix rule: a word in *-ment* that is not in the lexicon is very likely an adverb.

**Sources.** The descriptive part follows the reference grammars listed in [sources.md](sources.md); examples and counts are from UD French GSD.
