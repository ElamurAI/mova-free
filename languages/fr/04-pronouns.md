# 4. Pronouns

French personal pronouns come in two series. The **clitic** (weak, conjunctive) pronouns are bound to the verb: they cannot be stressed, coordinated or separated from the verb by anything but another clitic and a negation. The **strong** (disjunctive, tonic) pronouns are free words that stand alone, after a preposition, in coordination and in dislocation. The system of clitics, with its fixed order and the rule that decides which of them can occur together, is the characteristic part of French pronominal grammar. This chapter describes the personal pronouns, the clitic cluster, *y* and *en*, the reflexive, *on*, and then the demonstrative, relative, indefinite and possessive pronouns. The interrogative pronouns are in [chapter 9](09-questions.md).

## 1. The forms

| person | subject clitic | direct object | indirect object | reflexive | strong |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 sg. | *je* (*j'*) | *me* (*m'*) | *me* | *me* | *moi* |
| 2 sg. | *tu* | *te* (*t'*) | *te* | *te* | *toi* |
| 3 sg. m. | *il* | *le* (*l'*) | *lui* | *se* (*s'*) | *lui* |
| 3 sg. f. | *elle* | *la* (*l'*) | *lui* | *se* | *elle* |
| 1 pl. | *nous* | *nous* | *nous* | *nous* | *nous* |
| 2 pl. | *vous* | *vous* | *vous* | *vous* | *vous* |
| 3 pl. m. | *ils* | *les* | *leur* | *se* | *eux* |
| 3 pl. f. | *elles* | *les* | *leur* | *se* | *elles* |

Also *on* (subject, §9), *y* and *en* (§6), and *soi* (the strong reflexive of an indefinite, *Chacun pour soi*). The second person has a familiar singular (*tu*) and a polite or plural form (*vous*); the choice is a matter of social relation and is a feature of the whole conversation ([ch. 13](13-varieties-registers.md)).

## 2. Subject clitics

The subject of a finite verb is a noun phrase or a subject clitic, and **a subject must be present**: French does not drop the subject as Spanish and Italian do. The only exceptions are the imperative, the infinitive and a few fixed phrases. A clitic subject stands immediately before the verb (or before the first clitic of the cluster): *Je ne le lui ai pas dit*. When a noun subject is also expressed, it is dislocated and the clitic remains (*Marie, elle est partie*; [ch. 10](10-clause-structure.md)).

**Impersonal *il*.** An empty *il* is the subject of weather verbs (*Il pleut*), of impersonal constructions (*Il faut partir*, *Il y a un problème*, *Il est important que…*) and of the passive of such verbs (*Il a été décidé que…*). In *Il y a douze espèces reconnues* (fr-ud-train_00231) the *il* is an expletive subject and *y* an expletive clitic that is part of the verb phrase.

**Ce.** *Ce* (*c'*) is the demonstrative subject of *être*: *C'est un livre*, *Ce sont des amis*, *C'était hier*. *C'est* is used before a noun phrase with a determiner, a strong pronoun, a clause, and for a situation or an unspecified referent (*C'est grand*, "it is big", said of a thing in view); *il/elle est* is used with a bare adjective or a bare noun of profession that describes a definite antecedent (*Elle est avocate*, *Il est grand*). The cleft construction is built on *c'est* ([ch. 10](10-clause-structure.md)).

## 3. Object clitics

The direct-object clitics are *me*, *te*, *le*, *la*, *nous*, *vous*, *les*; the indirect-object clitics are *me*, *te*, *lui*, *nous*, *vous*, *leur*. *Le*, *la*, *les* stand for a definite object (*Je le vois*, *Je les ai vus*); *lui* and *leur* stand for an indirect object that is **a person** (*Je lui parle*, *Je leur donne le livre*). In *Je leur rétorque qu'ils l'avaient bien vu quand je le leur ai laissé* (fr-ud-train_12887) both *leur* are indirect objects, the *l'* is the object of *vu*, and the *le* before *leur* in the last clause is the object of *laissé*.

The pronoun *le* is also invariable in two further uses: it stands for a **predicate** (*Elle est heureuse et elle ne l'a pas toujours été*) and for a **clause** (*Je le sais*, *Il me l'a dit*; fr-ud-test_00087 has *Il me l'a dit à la Haye*). Both are traces of the neuter gender.

**Verbs that take à + a strong pronoun.** With some verbs whose complement is *à* plus a person, the clitic *lui* is not used and the strong pronoun appears after the preposition: *Je pense à elle*, *Il tient à moi*, *Elle s'intéresse à eux*, *Fais attention à lui*, *Je songe à toi*, *Il s'est habitué à elle*. For things, *y* is used instead (*J'y pense*). The class includes *penser à*, *songer à*, *rêver à*, *tenir à*, *renoncer à*, *s'habituer à*, *s'attacher à*, *faire attention à*, *être à*.

## 4. The order of the clitics

When there is more than one clitic before the verb, they come in a fixed order:

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *me*, *te*, *se*, *nous*, *vous* | *le*, *la*, *les* | *lui*, *leur* | *y* | *en* |

*Il me le donne*, *Il le lui donne*, *Elle nous en parle*, *Je m'y suis habitué*, *Il y en a*, *Il ne le lui a pas dit*, *Il me l'a dit* (fr-ud-test_00087). Two things follow from the table.

- The clitics of first and second persons precede the third-person direct object (*Il me le donne*); the third-person direct object precedes the dative (*Il le lui donne*). The two orders are *me le* and *le lui*: the order is by person, not by case.
- Not all pairs can occur together. A first or second person direct object cannot be combined with a third-person indirect clitic (**Il me lui présente*, **Il te lui a recommandé*): the speaker uses the strong pronoun (*Il me présente à lui*). Two first-or-second person clitics cannot co-occur either (**Il me te donne*). The combination *lui y* does not occur, and *y* and *en* are the last in the cluster (*Il y en a*, *Il n'y en a point*, fr-ud-test_00097).

**With negation.** *Ne* stands before the whole cluster and *pas* after the finite verb: *Je ne le lui ai pas dit*. See [chapter 8](08-negation.md).

**With compound tenses.** The cluster stands before the auxiliary: *Il me l'a dit*, *Je le leur ai laissé*; the past participle may agree with a preceding direct-object clitic ([ch. 7](07-auxiliaries-participle.md)).

## 5. Clitics with infinitives and with the imperative

**Infinitive.** Clitics belong to the verb of which they are an argument and, in the modern language, stand immediately before that verb: *Je veux le voir*, *Il ne peut pas lui parler*, *Pouvez-vous nous en dire plus ?* (fr-ud-train_02355, with *nous* and *en* before *dire*, the verb of which they are arguments, not before *pouvoir*). The older pattern, "clitic climbing", with the clitics before the modal (*Je le veux voir*), was obligatory until the Middle French period and was lost before the 19th century (olivier-2023); it survives with the verbs of perception and causation (*Je l'ai vu partir*) and in *faire*, *laisser* (*Ses enfants le lui font remarquer*, fr-ud-train_04603, where the clitic *le* and *lui* stand before *font*, the auxiliary-like *faire*, and the infinitive is *remarquer*).

**Affirmative imperative.** The clitics follow the verb, are joined to it by hyphens, and stand in a different order: direct object before indirect, with *moi* and *toi* for *me* and *te*:

| verb | direct (*le, la, les*) | indirect (*moi, toi, lui, nous, vous, leur*) | *y* | *en* |
|---|---|---|---|---|

*Donne-le-moi*, *Donnez-le-lui*, *Dis-le-leur*, *Vas-y*, *Parles-en*, *Donnez-m'en* (*m'* and *t'* before *en*), *Suis-moi* (fr-ud-dev_00823, *suis-moi*). A final *-s* that the *tu* imperative of a verb in *-er* has lost is restored before *y* and *en* to keep the liaison (*Va* but *Vas-y*, *Mange* but *Manges-en*). In the **negative imperative** the ordinary order returns: *Ne me le donne pas*, *N'en parlez pas*.

**Inversion.** In a question by inversion the subject clitic follows the verb, joined by a hyphen, with *-t-* inserted between a verb that ends in a vowel and *il*, *elle*, *on* (*Va-t-il …*, fr-ud-dev_00602; [ch. 9](09-questions.md)).

## 6. *Y* and *en*

*Y* and *en* are clitic pronouns that replace a prepositional complement. They are not personal pronouns and were not in the list of §1.

- ***Y*** replaces a phrase with *à* and a thing (*Je pense à ce problème* → *J'y pense*) and a **place** (*Il va à Paris* → *Il y va*; *On y mange correctement*, fr-ud-dev_00087; *La nourriture y est très bonne*, fr-ud-dev_00666). In the standard it is avoided for persons (§3).
- ***En*** replaces a phrase with *de* and a thing or a person-as-source (*Il parle de ce problème* → *Il en parle*), the origin (*Je viens de Paris* → *J'en viens*), and **a quantity of a noun** that is left unsaid: *J'ai trois frères* → *J'en ai trois*; *On en avait vue une dizaine* (fr-ud-dev_00067, where *en* stands for the thing counted and the numeral stays); *Tsahal a commandé 130 Namers et en a reçu 10* (fr-ud-train_06235).
- **Idioms** with a fossilised *y* or *en*: *il y a*, *s'en aller*, *en vouloir à*, *s'y prendre*, *y compris*, *Je n'en peux plus*, *Voilà où nous en sommes* (fr-ud-train_13769).

## 7. Strong pronouns

The strong pronouns (*moi*, *toi*, *lui*, *elle*, *nous*, *vous*, *eux*, *elles*, *soi*) are used:

- alone, as an answer, or after a conjunction: *Qui est là ? — Moi.*; *plus grand que toi*; *Ni lui ni elle*;
- in coordination with a noun or another pronoun, as subject: *Marie et moi, nous sommes parties*; *Lui et moi*;
- after a preposition: *chez elle* (fr-ud-train_02204), *avec eux*, *pour lui*, *rentrer chez elles* (fr-ud-train_01390);
- after *c'est*: *C'est moi*, *C'est lui qui…* (fr-ud-dev_00665 *C'est lui qui recruta le successeur de Graham*);
- in dislocation, as an emphatic doublet of a clitic: *Moi, je pense que…*, *Lui, il est parti* ([ch. 10](10-clause-structure.md));
- with *-même* (*lui-même*, *elle-même*, *l'autorité judiciaire elle-même*, fr-ud-dev_00594);
- as the subject of a verb in the infinitive or the participle clause, or when the verb is elided: *Lui parti, la maison était vide*, *Lui, jamais !*

*Soi* is the reflexive strong pronoun with an indefinite or generic subject (*Chacun pour soi*, *On est bien chez soi*); with a definite subject *lui*, *elle*, *eux* are used (*Il pense à lui*).

## 8. Reflexive and reciprocal

The reflexive pronoun is *se* (*s'*) for all third persons, singular and plural, and for *on*; the first and second persons use *me*, *te*, *nous*, *vous*, which are the same as the object clitics. A reflexive verb carries the pronoun as an obligatory part (*se souvenir*, *s'évanouir*, *se lever*); it also expresses a reciprocal action (*Ils se parlent*), a passive meaning (*Ces livres se vendent bien*), and a reflexive proper (*Elle se lave*). All of these use the auxiliary *être* in compound tenses; see [chapter 7](07-auxiliaries-participle.md).

## 9. *On*

*On* is an indefinite subject clitic with a third-person singular verb. It is "people in general" (*On dit que…*, *En France, on mange tard*) or, in the spoken language, **"we"**, which has nearly displaced *nous* as a subject: *On est parti de nuit*, *On y est allés* ([ch. 13](13-varieties-registers.md)). With *on* as "we" the predicate adjective or participle may agree in the plural (*On est contents*, *On est partis*) or stay singular; the two are heard, and in ParisStories *euh on est arrivé de nuit* (ParisStories_2020_campBedouin_6) has the singular. The form *l'on* occurs after *que*, *si*, *où*, *et* in the formal register to avoid an unpleasant hiatus.

## 10. Demonstrative pronouns

| | masculine | feminine | neuter |
|---|---|---|---|
| near/far | *celui(-ci/-là)* | *celle(-ci/-là)* | *ceci*, *cela*, *ça* |
| plural | *ceux(-ci/-là)* | *celles(-ci/-là)* | |
| subject of *être* and in clefts | | | *ce* |

*Celui*, *celle*, *ceux*, *celles* need a complement: *celui de Pierre*, *celle qui est partie*, or the particles *-ci* and *-là*: *Celles-ci peuvent à tout moment jeter l'éponge* (fr-ud-train_01390). *Cela* (*ça* in speech) points to a situation or a clause (*Cela ne gâte rien*; *Ça va ?*). *Ce* is used before *qui*, *que*, *dont* and before *être*: *ce qui ne gâte rien* (fr-ud-dev_00270), *C'est ce que nous nommons aujourd'hui le Vieux-Flassans* (fr-ud-dev_00188).

## 11. Relative pronouns

| function | persons | things |
|---|---|---|
| subject | *qui* | *qui* |
| direct object | *que* | *que* |
| after a preposition | *qui* (*à qui*, *avec qui*) | *lequel* and its forms (*auquel*, *duquel*, *laquelle*, *lesquels*) |
| *de* + noun, the whole *de*-phrase | *dont* | *dont* |
| place, time | *où* | *où* |
| headless | *ce qui*, *ce que*, *ce dont*, *ce à quoi*; *quiconque* | |

*Dont* replaces *de* plus the relative (*Adam est le berceau de la tribu Al Bu Said, dont est issu l'actuel sultan*, fr-ud-dev_00024). After a noun, *auquel*, *duquel* and the like carry the preposition and agree with the noun: *date à laquelle Mesa quitte les Postobón* (fr-ud-train_01832), *match au cours duquel il inscrit un but* (fr-ud-train_02217). The syntax of the relative clause is in [chapter 11](11-subordination.md).

## 12. Indefinite and possessive pronouns

**Indefinite pronouns:** *quelqu'un*, *personne*, *rien*, *quelque chose*, *chacun(e)*, *tout*, *tous*, *plusieurs*, *certains*, *aucun(e)*, *l'un… l'autre*, *autre chose*, *n'importe qui*. *Personne* and *rien* are negative when they are subject or object of *ne* (*personne ne sait*, fr-ud-train_03972; *je ne vais rien oublier*, fr-ud-test_00293) and positive without it (*Quelqu'un est venu ?* — *Personne.*); [chapter 8](08-negation.md) treats them. An adjective that qualifies an indefinite pronoun is linked by *de* and is masculine: *quelqu'un de gentil*, *rien de plus* (fr-ud-dev_00961), *rien d'exceptionnel* (fr-ud-train_00599). *Chacun* takes *son* or *leur* depending on the antecedent (*chacun des deux pays*, fr-ud-train_02032).

**Possessive pronouns** consist of the article and the stressed possessive: *le mien*, *la mienne*, *les miens*, *les miennes*; *le tien*; *le sien*; *le nôtre*, *la nôtre*, *les nôtres*; *le vôtre*; *le leur*, *la leur*, *les leurs*. *Prend l'avis de chacun, mais réserve le tien* (fr-ud-train_02615).

## 13. How Mova handles it

**What is done.** The English parser reads a French clitic as a word of the class `PRON` and attaches it as the UD treebank has it. The data show how much work the classification of clitics is: in UD French GSD (UD 2.18, three files, 2026-10-22) there are 17 687 `PRON` tokens; the relations `expl:pv` (1 017 tokens), `expl:subj` (931), `expl:pass` (686) and `expl:comp` (211) are four kinds of expletive or reflexive clitic. The tag mapping of `en xpos-fill` gives a pronoun the English tag `PRP` (`PRP$` for a possessive, `WP` for a relative or interrogative): case, person and the difference between a clitic and a strong form are not in the tag.

**UD conventions** (UD French GSD), with the sentence ids of the trees we checked:

- **Subject clitics** are `nsubj`; impersonal *il* and the demonstrative subject *c'* of a cleft are `expl:subj` (*C'est lui qui…*, fr-ud-dev_00665; *Il y a douze espèces*, fr-ud-train_00231).
- **Object clitics** are `obj` (*le*, *la*, *les*, *me*, *te* as direct objects) and `iobj` (*lui*, *leur*, and *me*, *te*, *nous*, *vous* as indirect objects), and they are attached to the verb of which they are an argument, even when the surface position is before a different verb: in *Pouvez-vous nous en dire plus ?* (fr-ud-train_02355), *nous* is `iobj` and *en* is `obj` of the infinitive *dire*, the `xcomp` of *Pouvez*; in the causative *le lui font remarquer* (fr-ud-train_04603) *le* is `obj` and *lui* is `iobj:agent` of *remarquer*, and the noun subject is `nsubj:caus`.
- ***Y*** is `obl:mod` when it is a place or an oblique (222 tokens), `iobj` (117), `expl:comp` (157) in *il y a* and similar; ***en*** is `iobj` (112), `nmod` (86) when it stands for the possessor or the set that a quantity is taken from (*On en avait vue une dizaine*, fr-ud-dev_00067, where *en* is `nmod` of *dizaine*), `expl:comp` (53), `obj` (15).
- **Reflexive** *se* is split among relations: `obj` for a true reflexive (*qu'ils se sont consultés*, fr-ud-train_06603), `expl:pass` for the reflexive passive and the middle (*Va-t-il se circonscrire à*, fr-ud-dev_00602), `expl:pv` for the inherent reflexive of a pronominal verb (*Johnson s'appuyait sur*, fr-ud-dev_00018). The three are distinguished by the annotators, not by form.
- **Inverted subject** pronouns are split from the verb with the hyphen on the pronoun and are `nsubj` (`-t-il`, `-vous`).
- **Strong pronouns** have `Emph=Yes` in FEATS (*elles*, fr-ud-train_01390) and clitics `Emph=No`; the case feature `Case=Nom|Acc|Dat` is on clitics only.
- **Relative** *qui*, *que*, *dont*, *où*, *lequel* have `PronType=Rel`; the clause is `acl:relcl` of the antecedent (*dont est issu…*, fr-ud-dev_00024, with *dont* as `iobj` of the predicate *issu*); a cleft relative is `advcl:cleft` (fr-ud-dev_00665).
- **Demonstratives** have `PronType=Dem` (*celle*, *celles-ci*), and `ce` as the subject of *être* is `nsubj` or `expl:subj` according to the construction.

**A defect to know.** The lemmas of the personal pronouns in GSD are conflated: *il* (2 032 and, capitalised, 1 580), *elle* (523) and *le* (281) all have the lemma *lui*; *je* and *me* have *moi* (182, 53); *se* has *soi* (1 341). Counts by lemma therefore group distinct words, and a lexicon built from the lemma column would need a correction.

**What is not handled.** The clitic order and its co-occurrence restrictions are not modelled, so an ungrammatical sequence is not rejected; the choice of *lui* or *à elle* by the verb is not known; the *tu/vous* choice is not read; *on* as "we" is not resolved; and clitic climbing is learned only as a tree shape, so a clitic whose verb is not the nearest one depends on the training data. The parser's LAS on GSD (87.00 with its tags, 89.78 with gold tags, UD 2.18, train → dev, 2026-10-22) is an average over all relations; the relations of this chapter (`expl:*`, `iobj`, `obl:mod` for *y*) are the rarest in the treebank and we have not scored them separately.

**Sources.** Olivier, Sevdali and Folli 2023 on clitic climbing (olivier-2023); the descriptive part follows the reference grammars of [sources.md](sources.md).
