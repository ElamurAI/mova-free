# 14. The lexicon

French vocabulary comes from three layers:
- the **inherited** words: Latin as spoken in Gaul, worn down by fifteen centuries of sound change (*eau* < *aqua*, *père* < *patrem*);
- **learned** words, borrowed back from written Latin and Greek from the Middle Ages on and kept close to their source (*aquatique*, *paternel*);
- **borrowings** from the languages French met: Frankish and the other Germanic languages, Italian, Spanish, Arabic, English.

Because English took over a large part of its learned and Norman vocabulary from French, the two languages share tens of thousands of words, with many traps. This chapter gives word formation, the doublets, the borrowings, the false friends and the multiword expressions, and says what Mova would need for a French lexicon.

## 14.1 Word formation

**Suffixes.** The productive suffixes:

| suffix | forms | example |
|---|---|---|
| *-age* | action, collective (m) | *lavage, feuillage* |
| *-ment* | action, result (m) | *changement, enseignement* |
| *-tion, -sion* | action (f) | *création, décision* |
| *-eur, -euse; -teur, -trice* | agent | *chanteur, chanteuse; acteur, actrice* |
| *-ier, -ière* | trade, tree | *boulanger, pommier* |
| *-ité* | quality (f) | *rapidité, liberté* |
| *-esse* | quality (f) | *tristesse, sagesse* |
| *-isme, -iste* | doctrine, follower | *socialisme, socialiste* |
| *-able, -ible* | possibility | *lavable, lisible* |
| *-eux, -euse* | full of | *dangereux, courageuse* |
| *-ette, -et; -on* | diminutive | *maisonnette, garçonnet; chaton* |
| *-ment* (on the feminine adjective) | adverb | *lentement, heureusement* ([chapter 5](05-adjectives-adverbs.md)) |
| *-iser, -ifier* | verb | *moderniser, simplifier* |

**Prefixes:** *re-/ré-* (again: *refaire, réécrire*), *dé-/dés-* (undo: *défaire, désordre*), *in-/im-/il-/ir-* (not: *inutile, impossible, illégal, irréel*), *pré-, pro-, anti-, sur-, sous-, contre-, mi-, mal-*.

**Compounds** are written with a hyphen, with spaces, or as one word: *arc-en-ciel, pomme de terre, portefeuille*. A noun–*de*–noun compound is the French counterpart of the English noun+noun compound: *salle **de** bains*, *chemin **de** fer*. The plural of compounds follows their meaning (*des pommes de terre, des portefeuilles, des arcs-en-ciel*). The 1990 rectifications regularise many of them (*des après-midis*) ([chapter 1](01-sounds-and-spelling.md)).

**Clipping and acronyms** are frequent in the familiar register: *prof, appart, ado, resto, ordi, télé, la fac, le bac; la SNCF, un SDF*.

## 14.2 Doublets: one Latin word, two French words

The layers of the Latin vocabulary itself, and how Latin reached English, are in [la chapter 11](../la/11-numerals-lexicon.md), §7.

A Latin word often gave French two words: one inherited (worn by sound change, everyday) and one learned (borrowed back later, abstract or technical). The pair usually differs in meaning:

| Latin | inherited | learned |
|---|---|---|
| *fragilis* | *frêle* "slender, frail" | *fragile* "breakable" |
| *hospitalem* | *hôtel* "hotel, mansion" | *hôpital* "hospital" |
| *potionem* | *poison* "poison" | *potion* "potion" |
| *strictum* | *étroit* "narrow" | *strict* "strict" |
| *redemptionem* | *rançon* "ransom" | *rédemption* "redemption" |
| *auscultare* | *écouter* "to listen" | *ausculter* "to examine (a patient) by listening" |
| *integrum* | *entier* "whole" | *intègre* "honest" |

Learned adjectives often belong to a noun of the inherited layer: *eau* → *aquatique*, *œil* → *oculaire*, *mère* → *maternel*, *doigt* → *digital*, *cheval* → *équestre*. English works the same way (*water* → *aquatic*), with the learned word often the same as the French one.

## 14.3 Borrowings

- **Frankish and Germanic**, in the early Middle Ages: *guerre* (war), *jardin*, *blanc*, *bleu*, *riche*, *haïr*; the *h aspiré* of *haut, honte, hache* that blocks liaison ([chapter 1](01-sounds-and-spelling.md)).
- **Italian**, in the Renaissance: *balcon, banque, soldat, sonnet, caprice, concert*.
- **Arabic**, through Spanish, Italian and Latin and directly: *algèbre, chiffre, zéro, sucre, café, magasin, amiral*; and, in recent familiar and young speech, *toubib, kif, bled, wesh* ([chapter 13](13-varieties-registers.md)).
- **English**, from the eighteenth century and massively since 1945: *week-end, parking, sandwich, email, start-up, cool*. Some anglicisms are French-made and do not exist in English with that sense: *un footing* (a jog), *un brushing* (a blow-dry), *le pressing* (a dry cleaner), *un smoking* (a dinner jacket), *un parking* (a car park). The official French terminology commissions propose French forms (*courriel* for e-mail, *mot-dièse* for hashtag); Québec uses them far more than France ([chapter 13](13-varieties-registers.md)).

## 14.4 False friends

Words that look alike in French and English but differ in meaning:

| French | means | not |
|---|---|---|
| *actuel, actuellement* | current, currently | actual, actually (*réel, en fait*) |
| *éventuel, éventuellement* | possible, possibly | eventual, eventually (*final, finalement*) |
| *assister à* | to attend | to assist (*aider*) |
| *librairie* | bookshop | library (*bibliothèque*) |
| *location* | rental | location (*emplacement*) |
| *sensible* | sensitive | sensible (*raisonnable*) |
| *rester* | to stay | to rest (*se reposer*) |
| *prétendre* | to claim | to pretend (*faire semblant*) |
| *déception* | disappointment | deception (*tromperie*) |
| *demander* | to ask | to demand (*exiger*) |
| *achever* | to finish | to achieve (*réaliser, accomplir*) |
| *large* | wide | large (*grand*) |
| *journée* | a day (its length) | journey (*voyage*) |
| *coin* | corner | coin (*pièce*) |

Partial false friends share a sense and differ in others: *une conférence* (a lecture, or a conference), *une expérience* (an experience, or an experiment), *contrôler* (to check, less often to control).

## 14.5 Multiword expressions

French uses many fixed and semi-fixed expressions, and a reader of single words misses their meaning:
- **light verb constructions:** *avoir faim, avoir peur, avoir raison, faire attention, prendre une décision, donner rendez-vous*. The noun carries the meaning ([chapter 3](03-determiners.md) gives the bare noun after the verb);
- **complex prepositions and conjunctions:** *à cause de, grâce à, au lieu de, en face de; parce que, bien que, afin que* ([chapter 11](11-subordination.md));
- **idioms:** *poser un lapin* (to stand someone up), *coûter les yeux de la tête* (to cost an arm and a leg), *avoir le cafard* (to feel down), *tomber dans les pommes* (to faint);
- **pragmatic formulas:** *ça va, ça marche, n'importe quoi, tant pis, bon courage*.

## How Mova handles it

**What Mova has.** No French lexicon yet: the tagger learns the forms of the training trees and guesses the rest from their endings. On GSD (2026-10-22) 7.2% of the development tokens are unknown to it, and it tags them right 84.8% of the time, against 96.5% overall. The English side of Mova has a full-form lexicon (`en/data/forms.tsv`, from the AGID inflection database) and a register lexicon. The French side has neither.

**What a French lexicon needs,** in order of what it buys:
1. **Full forms with their lemma and features**, to give the tagger the unknown words and to give the frames reader lemmas. The open candidates the library is gathering are Morphalou, Lefff and GLÀFF. Their licences decide whether they can be built in or are only measured against: [sources.md](sources.md) lists them as they come.
2. **The light verbs and the multiword expressions of 14.5**, as the English side has in its expressions and idioms tables. Without them the frames read *avoir peur* as possession of a fear.
3. **Variety and register labels** for the words of [chapter 13](13-varieties-registers.md), from the French Wiktionary (raw dump on disk; CC BY-SA, so learning and tests only).
4. **A French–English concept map** for the meta-world. The doublets and false friends of 14.2–14.4 show why form alone cannot give it. It is to be learned from the parallel texts (Europarl, the Canadian Hansards, the parallel Bibles) the way `translate` learns the English authors' interlingua.
