# 6. The verb

The French verb inflects for person and number, tense, mood and voice. On paper the system is rich: three groups of regular verbs, about a dozen irregular ones that are used constantly, up to forty forms for a verb counting the compound tenses. In speech it is much poorer, because the written endings of the present of the verbs in *-er* (*parle*, *parles*, *parle*, *parlent*) are all pronounced the same, and the forms of the second and third persons often merge. This chapter describes the groups and their paradigms, the simple tenses, the moods, the passé simple and the literary tenses, the non-finite forms and the verbal periphrases. The auxiliaries, the compound tenses and the agreement of the participle are in [chapter 7](07-auxiliaries-participle.md), the subjunctive's use in [chapter 11](11-subordination.md).

## 1. The groups

The traditional division, used in every school grammar, has three groups by the ending of the infinitive and the shape of the present participle:

| group | infinitive | present participle | size | examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *-er* (except *aller*) | *-ant* | by far the largest group; the only productive one | *parler*, *aimer*, *manger*, *envoyer* |
| 2 | *-ir* with *-issant* | *-issant* | a few hundred verbs | *finir*, *choisir*, *réussir*, *grandir* |
| 3 | all the others | various | a few hundred verbs, many of them very frequent | *partir*, *venir*, *voir*, *pouvoir*, *prendre*, *dire*, *faire*, *être*, *avoir*, *aller* |

New verbs enter the first group (*texter*, *googler*, *liker*), and the second group is productive only for a few formations on adjectives (*verdir*, *rougir*). The third group is not a class: its verbs have several stems, and the learner learns each one with its forms. The auxiliaries *être* and *avoir*, and the verbs *aller*, *faire*, *dire*, *pouvoir*, *vouloir*, *savoir*, *devoir*, *voir*, *prendre*, *venir* are the most frequent verbs of the language and the most irregular.

## 2. The present

| | *parler* (1) | *finir* (2) | *vendre* (3) | *être* | *avoir* | *aller* | *faire* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| je | parle | finis | vends | suis | ai | vais | fais |
| tu | parles | finis | vends | es | as | vas | fais |
| il/elle/on | parle | finit | vend | est | a | va | fait |
| nous | parlons | finissons | vendons | sommes | avons | allons | faisons |
| vous | parlez | finissez | vendez | êtes | avez | allez | faites |
| ils/elles | parlent | finissent | vendent | sont | ont | vont | font |

The endings of the regular verbs are *-e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent* (group 1), *-is, -is, -it, -issons, -issez, -issent* (group 2) and *-s, -s, -t, -ons, -ez, -ent* (group 3, with the *-t* absent after *d*: *il vend*). The endings of the third group are the group-2 endings without *-iss-*. Three verbs have a second plural that does not end in *-ez* (*vous êtes*, *vous dites*, *vous faites*).

**In speech.** For verbs of group 1, the first, second and third singular and the third plural sound alike (/paʁl/): the subject clitic carries the person and the number (the verb has to be preceded by a subject, [ch. 4](04-pronouns.md)). For verbs of groups 2 and 3, the singular and the plural differ audibly in most verbs (*il finit* /fini/, *ils finissent* /finis/; *il part* /paʁ/, *ils partent* /paʁt/; but *il vend* and *ils vendent* /vɑ̃d/, with the plural carrying the consonant that the singular loses, as in *il prend* /pʁɑ̃/, *ils prennent* /pʁɛn/).

## 3. Stem alternations and spelling

**Spelling adjustments** keep the pronunciation of the consonant: *manger* has *-ge-* before *a* and *o* (*mangeons*, *mangeait*); *commencer* has *ç* before *a* and *o* (*commençons*); verbs in *-yer* change *y* to *i* before a mute *e* (*il paie* or *paye*, *il envoie*, *il nettoie*, but *nous payons*); verbs in *-eler*, *-eter* double the consonant or take a grave accent before a mute *e* (*il appelle*, *il jette*, but *il achète*, *il pèle*, and by the 1990 rectification *il ruissèle*, *j'étiquète*; [ch. 1](01-sounds-and-spelling.md), §8); verbs with *é* or *e* in the stem, like *céder*, *lever*, *semer*, change the stem vowel before a mute *e* (*il cède*, *il lève*, *il sème*; the future *il cédera* has the grave accent after the rectification).

**Stem change.** Verbs of the third group have two or three stems: *venir* has *vien-* (*viens*, *vient*, *viennent*), *ven-* (*venons*, *venez*), *vin-* (the passé simple *vins*) and *viendr-* (the future); *prendre* has *prend-/pren-/prenn-*; *boire* has *boi-/buv-/boiv-*; *voir* has *voi-/voy-/verr-*; *pouvoir* has *peu-/pouv-/peuv-/pourr-*; *devoir*, *vouloir*, *savoir* are similar. The learner's job is to know the stems; the endings are regular within the group.

## 4. Imperfect, future, conditional

**Imperfect** (*imparfait*): the stem of the first plural of the present (*parlons* → *parl-*), with the endings *-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient*: *je parlais*, *nous finissions*, *ils vendaient*. The one exception is *être*, with the stem *ét-* (*j'étais*). The singular and the third plural are homophones (/paʁlɛ/); the first singular and the third singular of the conditional (*je parlerais*, *il parlerait*) are homophones too.

**Future** (*futur simple*): the infinitive (without the final *-e* for the verbs in *-re*) with the endings of *avoir* in the present: *-ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont*: *je parlerai*, *il finira*, *nous vendrons*. Irregular stems: *être* → *ser-*, *avoir* → *aur-*, *aller* → *ir-*, *faire* → *fer-*, *voir* → *verr-*, *pouvoir* → *pourr-*, *vouloir* → *voudr-*, *venir* → *viendr-*, *devoir* → *devr-*, *savoir* → *saur-*, *falloir* → *faudr-*, *envoyer* → *enverr-*.

**Conditional** (*conditionnel présent*): the future stem with the endings of the imperfect: *je parlerais*, *il aurait*, *nous irions*. The conditional is a tense and a mood in the traditional grammar; in modern descriptions it is a mood (UD: `Mood=Cnd`). Its uses are the consequence of a hypothetical (*Si j'avais le temps, je viendrais*), politeness (*Je voudrais un café*), the future seen from the past (*Il a dit qu'il viendrait*), and, in journalism, a report that is not confirmed (*Il aurait alors distribué des terres aux Gascons*, fr-ud-dev_00947; *Le renforcement de la présence policière y serait pour beaucoup*, fr-ud-dev_01340).

## 5. The passé simple and the literary tenses

**Passé simple (past historic).** The passé simple expresses a completed past event, and **it is a tense of writing**: it is not used in conversation, where the passé composé takes its place, nor in letters. It is the narrative tense of the novel, of history, of biography and of the Wikipedia article, and of the descriptions of events in the past. In narrative it is used with the imperfect, which supplies the background: *Il pleuvait quand il arriva*. In practice only the third person is frequent: the first and second persons are rare, and the second plural is nearly confined to speeches that imitate the old style.

| group | endings | example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *-ai, -as, -a, -âmes, -âtes, -èrent* | *je parlai, il parla, ils parlèrent* |
| 2 and most of 3 (*-is*) | *-is, -is, -it, -îmes, -îtes, -irent* | *il finit, il prit, ils virent, il dit, il mit, il fit* |
| *-us* verbs | *-us, -us, -ut, -ûmes, -ûtes, -urent* | *il fut, il eut, il put, il dut, il vécut, ils furent* |
| *venir*, *tenir* | *-ins, -ins, -int, -înmes, -întes, -inrent* | *il vint, il tint, ils vinrent* |

The treebank is rich in them, because it is mainly written text from Wikipedia and the press: *Elles furent victimes de la Révolution française* (fr-ud-dev_00058), *le M10 prit le nom d'Achilles* (fr-ud-dev_00287), *qu'il eut à repousser l'attaque* (fr-ud-dev_00650), *C'est lui qui recruta le successeur de Graham* (fr-ud-dev_00665), *L'hôpital fut construit … en 1695* (fr-ud-dev_00121). In UD French GSD the tag `Tense=Past` is on 1 592 finite verbs (of 24 877 finite verbs), which is the passé simple (UD 2.18, 2026-10-22).

**Imperfect and pluperfect subjunctive.** The imperfect subjunctive is built on the passé simple (*que je parlasse*, *qu'il finît*, *qu'il vînt*, *qu'ils fussent*). It occurs in formal writing in the third person singular, mainly in a clause that depends on a verb in a past tense (*ordonna que le château fût immédiatement rendu*, fr-ud-train_01351; *jusqu'à ce qu'un nouveau trolley gratuit fût inauguré*, fr-ud-train_02570; *il était presque naturel qu'il continuât son œuvre*, fr-ud-train_02098). In GSD it has only 10 tokens (`Mood=Sub|Tense=Imp`), against 369 for the present subjunctive. The circumflex of *fût*, *continuât*, *eût* is kept by the 1990 rectification ([ch. 1](01-sounds-and-spelling.md)). The **pluperfect subjunctive** (*qu'il eût aimé*) and the **second form of the conditional past** (*il eût aimé* for *il aurait aimé*) are used in literary style. The **passé antérieur** (*il eut fini*) is the compound tense that goes with the passé simple, used after *quand*, *dès que*, *aussitôt que*, *après que* (*Dès qu'il eut fini, il partit*); in speech the *surcomposé* (*Quand il a eu fini*) takes its place.

## 6. Moods: subjunctive and imperative

**Subjunctive.** The present subjunctive has the endings of the present indicative of group 1 for all verbs (*-e, -es, -e, -ions, -iez, -ent*) and the stem of the third plural (*ils finissent* → *que je finisse*; *ils partent* → *que je parte*), with a second stem for the first and second plural of many verbs (*que je prenne* / *que nous prenions*; *que je vienne* / *que nous venions*). Irregular: *être* (*que je sois, que nous soyons*), *avoir* (*que j'aie, que nous ayons*), *aller* (*que j'aille*), *faire* (*que je fasse*), *pouvoir* (*que je puisse*), *savoir* (*que je sache*), *vouloir* (*que je veuille*), *falloir* (*qu'il faille*); fr-ud-dev_00710 has *Il se peut même que l'Athenæum et la bibliothèque du Capitole ne fasse qu'un* with the present subjunctive *fasse*. The subjunctive is a feature of subordinate clauses, and *que* precedes it: *que tu viennes*. The independent subjunctive expresses a wish or an order in the third person (*Qu'il vienne !*, *Vive la République !*, *Dieu veuille avoir en paix ton âme*, fr-ud-dev_00153). The uses are in [chapter 11](11-subordination.md).

**Imperative.** The imperative has three forms, those of the second singular, the first plural and the second plural of the present indicative, with no subject: *Parle !*, *Parlons !*, *Parlez !* The second singular of the verbs in *-er* (and of *aller*, *ouvrir*, *offrir*, *cueillir*) drops the *-s* (*Parle*, *Va*), and the *-s* returns before *y* and *en* (*Vas-y*, *Manges-en*; [ch. 4](04-pronouns.md)). Four verbs have a subjunctive-based imperative: *être* (*sois, soyons, soyez*), *avoir* (*aie, ayons, ayez*), *savoir* (*sache, sachons, sachez*) and *vouloir* (*veuille, veuillons, veuillez*, only in the formula *veuillez agréer*). The negative imperative uses *ne … pas* around the verb with the clitics before it ([ch. 4](04-pronouns.md), [ch. 8](08-negation.md)). In UD French GSD `Mood=Imp` occurs 137 times, as in *viens … suis-moi* (fr-ud-dev_00823).

## 7. Non-finite forms

**Infinitive.** Present (*parler*, *finir*, *vendre*) and past (*avoir parlé*, *être parti*). The infinitive is used as a noun (*Voir, c'est croire*), after a preposition (*pour échapper*, *sans parler*, *avant de partir*, *Il est interdit de fumer*), after a modal or an aspect verb (*Il peut venir*, *Il va partir*), and as an imperative on signs (*Ne pas déranger*).

**Present participle and gerund.** The present participle ends in *-ant* and is **invariable**: *Le groupe, jouant à Paris, …*. The gerund (*gérondif*) is *en* plus the present participle and denotes a manner or a simultaneous circumstance, with the subject of the main clause: *Wotan est devenu son propre adversaire en accomplissant les vœux de la déesse* (fr-ud-dev_00655); *tout en endossant le rôle* (fr-ud-dev_00683). A participle in *-ant* that agrees with its noun is an adjective (*une histoire passionnante*, *des enfants charmants*); some pairs differ in spelling (*fatiguant* participle / *fatigant* adjective, *différant* / *différent*, *négligeant* / *négligent*). A participle with an explicit subject of its own is an absolute construction (*Le soleil se levant, nous partîmes*) that survives in written style.

**Past participle.** The endings are *-é* (group 1), *-i* (group 2 and many others: *fini*, *parti*, *dormi*), *-u* (*vendu*, *vu*, *voulu*, *pu*), *-s* (*pris*, *mis*, *assis*), *-t* (*dit*, *fait*, *écrit*, *ouvert*), with *été*, *eu*, *né*, *mort*, *venu*. It forms the compound tenses, the passive, and an adjectival modifier; the agreement is in [chapter 7](07-auxiliaries-participle.md).

## 8. Periphrases

French has several verbal periphrases with a finite verb and an infinitive:

- **near future**: *aller* + infinitive, nearly equivalent to the simple future in speech (*Le Soudan va être divisé en deux*, fr-ud-train_04657; *Son livre va avoir l'effet d'une bombe*, fr-ud-train_09490). In writing the simple future is preferred; in speech *aller* + infinitive replaces it in most uses;
- **recent past**: *venir de* + infinitive (*Le groupe vient de perdre deux anciens membres*, fr-ud-train_09520); *venir* + *de* + infinitive must be distinguished from *venir* "to come from" (*Son nom vient de « Ulmus »*, fr-ud-train_00347);
- **progressive**: *être en train de* + infinitive; **imminence**: *être sur le point de*; **inchoative**: *se mettre à*, *commencer à*; **continuation**: *continuer à/de*, *ne pas cesser de*; **result**: *finir par*;
- **modals**: *devoir* (obligation, probability), *pouvoir* (possibility, permission), *vouloir*, *savoir* (ability), *falloir* (*il faut* + infinitive or *que* + subjunctive);
- **causatives**: *faire*, *laisser* and the verbs of perception + infinitive ([ch. 7](07-auxiliaries-participle.md)).

French does not have a progressive form in the English sense, and the imperfect and the present usually take its place (*Il lisait quand je suis entré*, "He was reading when I came in"). The expression *depuis* with the present expresses a state that started in the past and goes on: *Il habite ici depuis dix ans*, "He has lived here for ten years", and with the imperfect, the same in the past (*Il habitait ici depuis dix ans*).

## 9. The tenses in use

- **Present**: now, general truths, the near future (*Je pars demain*), the historical present of narration, and the present with *depuis*.
- **Imperfect**: background, a state or an habitual action in the past (*Il pleuvait*, *Chaque été nous allions à la mer*), a hypothetical in an *si*-clause (*Si je pouvais, …*), and the "narrative imperfect" of journalism.
- **Passé composé**: a completed event in speech and in informal writing (*Il est arrivé hier*), and a present result; it is formed with an auxiliary ([ch. 7](07-auxiliaries-participle.md)). In current French it replaces the passé simple in speech and in much writing.
- **Passé simple**: a completed event in literary and historical writing (§5).
- **Future and conditional**: §4; the future also expresses a supposition (*Ce sera Paul*, "It is probably Paul") and a polite order (*Tu feras la vaisselle*).
- **The sequence of tenses** in subordinate clauses is covered in [chapter 11](11-subordination.md).

## 10. How Mova handles it

**What is done.** French verbs are tagged by `en xpos-fill` and then by the English tagger with English verb tags, derived from the FEATS of the treebank: an infinitive is `VB`, a participle is `VBN`, or `VBG` when the treebank marks `Tense=Pres`, a finite form with `Tense=Past` or `Tense=Imp` is `VBD`, a third-person singular is `VBZ`, and every other finite verb is `VBP`. The consequence is that the passé simple and the imperfect are both `VBD`, and the present, the future, the conditional, the present subjunctive and the imperative are `VBZ` or `VBP` according to the person. A present participle that the treebank leaves without `Tense` becomes `VBN`, like a past participle. Mood and tense are lost in the tag; they are partly recoverable from the surrounding words (*que* + subjunctive, the endings of the word as the tagger's suffix features see them), and the parser's structure does not depend on them. The shape of the tree does not need the mood: the clause is a clause either way.

**Measured.** In UD French GSD (three files, 2026-10-22) the finite verbs by mood and tense are: present indicative 19 456, imperfect 2 027, passé simple (`Tense=Past`) 1 592, future 855, conditional 431, present subjunctive 369, imperative 137, imperfect subjunctive 10; the participles are 13 769 and the infinitives 6 170. The passé simple is thus about 6% of finite verbs: it is a form that a French parser cannot treat as a curiosity, and a lexicon of its endings (*-a*, *-èrent*, *-ut*, *-int*) is among the first things to build. The tagger's accuracy on words unseen in training is 84.8% (2026-10-22), and unseen verb forms, in particular the rarer persons of the passé simple and of the subjunctive, are among them.

**UD conventions** (UD French GSD):

- A finite verb has `Mood`, `Tense`, `Person`, `Number`; the conditional is `Mood=Cnd|Tense=Pres`, the passé simple is `Mood=Ind|Tense=Past`, the imperfect `Mood=Ind|Tense=Imp`, the subjunctive `Mood=Sub`, the imperative `Mood=Imp`; the infinitive and the participle have `VerbForm=Inf` and `VerbForm=Part`, with `Tense=Pres` for the present participle (usually unmarked: *accomplissant* and *jouxtant* have `VerbForm=Part` only) and `Voice=Pass` for a passive.
- A compound tense is an `AUX` with `aux:tense` and the participle is the head ([ch. 7](07-auxiliaries-participle.md)).
- A periphrasis with a verb is **not** an auxiliary construction: *aller*, *venir de*, *devoir*, *pouvoir*, *vouloir* are full verbs with the infinitive as `xcomp`, and *de*, *à* are `mark` on the infinitive: *vient de perdre* (fr-ud-train_09520) has *vient* as root and *perdre* as `xcomp` with *de* as `mark`; *va être divisé* (fr-ud-train_04657) has *va* as root and the passive participle *divisé* as `xcomp`, with *être* as `aux:pass` of *divisé*.
- The gerund is `advcl` with `en` as `mark` and `VerbForm=Part` (*en accomplissant*, fr-ud-dev_00655).
- The verb *être* as a copula is `cop` with the predicate as the head, and as an auxiliary it is `aux:tense` or `aux:pass`.

**What is not handled.** No French morphology: no conjugation tables, no stem alternation, no analysis of an unseen form as a finite verb of a known lemma, no lemmatiser (the lemmas in the treebanks are the only source), no tense or mood in the tag set, no sequence-of-tenses check. The first steps would be a conjugation generator for the three groups and the twelve irregular verbs, built from the paradigms above and checked on the lemma and FEATS columns of the treebanks; this would turn the unseen-word problem for verbs from a guess into a lookup.

**Sources.** The paradigms are the standard ones of the reference grammars; the public-domain manual of conjugation of 1848 (bescherelle-1848) is the open reference for them, and we did not check them line by line against it; the counts and examples are from UD French GSD.
