# The verb in Targumic Aramaic

The system is the one described for Biblical Aramaic in [../arc/verbs.md](../arc/verbs.md): a root, a stem, the perfect, the imperfect, the imperative, the infinitive, two participles. This file gives the targumic forms, the differences from Biblical Aramaic, and how the targum renders the Hebrew verb, which is the key to its syntax.

References: Dalman 1905 (verbs §§ 55–75, with full paradigms of the "Judean" dialect of Onkelos and Jonathan); Berliner 1884 vol. 2; Golomb 1985 (for the Palestinian comparison); Fassberg 2012. Examples are from the public-domain Sefaria texts of Onkelos and Jonathan, quoted as printed; counts are unpointed word tokens in Onkelos (2026-10-17).

Checked 2026-10-18 against the comparative verb tables at the end of Dalman 1905 (*Anhang*, pp. 402–407: strong verb in all stems, hollow, geminate and III-y verbs, Onkelos beside Galilean), read from the page images, and against the pointed Sefaria Onkelos (counts of pointed forms made the same day). Dalman's Onkelos follows the Sabbioneta edition; the Sefaria text is another printed pointing, and the two differ in some vowels (both are given where they do).

His ishtaphal examples (אִשְׁתֵּיזֵיב, p. 259) are from the Palestinian targums and the Talmud, not from Onkelos, so the ishtaphal in this row is not confirmed for Onkelos.

What changed since Biblical Aramaic:

1. **Aphel replaces haphel.** The causative is אַ־; the ה of the prefix is gone in the imperfect and participle too (יַקְטֵיל, מַקְטֵיל, against BA יְהַקְטֵל, מְהַקְטֵל). Exceptions are lexicalised forms like הֵימִין "believe" (Exod 14:31 וְהֵימִינוּ) and הוֹדִי "praise" (Dalman 1905).
2. **The internal passives are gone.** Peil and hophal no longer exist as finite forms; their work is done by ithpeel and ittaphal. Only the peal passive participle survives: כְּתִיב "written", גְּלֵי "revealed" (Gen 22:8 קֳדָם יְיָ גְּלֵי "before the LORD it is revealed").
3. **The reflexive prefix is אִתְ־** (BA הִתְ־), with assimilation of the t to a following ת, ט, ד and metathesis with the sibilants ש, ס, צ, ז (after צ the t becomes ט, after ז it becomes ד): אִיטַּמַּר (Gen 3:8) from *ʾiṯṭammar*, אִשְׁתְּבֵיק, אִסְתְּכֵי (Dalman 1905 §15.4b α, p. 103).

## 2. Peal of the strong verb

Model root קטל "kill". Vocalisation after Dalman 1905 and the printed text; the Babylonian-Yemenite reading differs in details ([phonology-orthography.md](phonology-orthography.md) §2).

Corrected: the 3 f. sg. of Onkelos is קְטַלַת (Dalman כְּתַבַת, i-verbs סְלֵיקַת; the Sefaria text has וַאֲכָלַת, וּנְסֵיבַת); קִטְלַת is the Biblical Aramaic form. The **1 sg. ־ִית** (against BA ־ֵת) is shared with JPA and is one of the clearest dialect markers between Biblical and targumic Aramaic.

### 2.3 Imperative, infinitive, participles

| form | m. sg. | f. sg. | m. pl. | example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| imperative | קְטוֹל | קְטוֹלִי | קְטוֹלוּ | שְׁמַע "hear!" (Deut 6:4); דְּבַר "take!" (Gen 22:2); אִיזֵל "go!" (Gen 12:1; 22:2) |
| infinitive | מִקְטַל | | | לְמֵימַר "saying" (308× למימר); לְמֵיכַל "to eat", לְמִלְבַּשׁ "to wear" (Gen 28:20); לְמִתְּבַר "to break" (Gen 6:6) |
| active participle | קָטֵיל | קָטְלָא | קָטְלִין | אָזֵל "going" (Gen 28:20); אָכְלִין "eating" (Gen 2:17); דְּרָחֵשׁ "that creeps" (Gen 1:26) |
| passive participle | קְטִיל | קְטִילָא | קְטִילִין | גְּלֵי "revealed" (Gen 22:8) |

Checked 2026-10-18: Dalman 1905, *Anhang* VI.1, p. 402 (imperative כְּתוֹב, כְּתוֹבִי, כְּתוֹבוּ, f. pl. כְּתוֹבָא; infinitive מִכְתַּב, also as infinitive absolute; participles כָּתֵיב, כָּתְבָא, כְּתִיב, כְּתִיבָא).

**The infinitive absolute.** Hebrew uses an infinitive absolute before a finite verb for emphasis (מוֹת תָּמוּת "you shall surely die"). Onkelos imitates it with the peal infinitive (*miqṭal*): מֵימַת תְּמוּת (Gen 2:17). Derived stems use their own infinitives in the same way.

## 5. How the targum renders the Hebrew verb

Onkelos maps the Hebrew verbal forms onto Aramaic ones systematically. This mapping is what a projection of Hebrew trees onto the targum needs ([mova.md](mova.md) §4).

| Hebrew | Onkelos | example (Hebrew → Aramaic) | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| *wayyiqtol* (narrative past) | ו + perfect | וַיֹּאמֶר → וַאֲמַר; וַיַּרְא → וַחֲזָא | Gen 1:3–4 |
| *qatal* | perfect | בָּרָא → בְּרָא | Gen 1:1 |
| *weqatal* (future, command) | ו + imperfect | וְאָהַבְתָּ → וְתִרְחַם; וְשִׁנַּנְתָּם → וּתְתָנִנוּן; וְדִבַּרְתָּ → וּתְמַלֵּל | Deut 6:5–7 |
| *yiqtol* | imperfect | יִהְיֶה → יְהֵא | Gen 28:20 |
| jussive | imperfect | יְהִי → יְהֵי | Gen 1:3 |
| cohortative | imperfect | נַעֲשֶׂה → נַעֲבֵיד | Gen 1:26 |
| imperative | imperative | שְׁמַע → שְׁמַע; קַח → דְּבַר | Deut 6:4; Gen 22:2 |
| participle | participle | מְרַחֶפֶת → מְנַשְּׁבָא; הוֹלֵךְ → אָזֵל | Gen 1:2; 28:20 |
| infinitive construct with ב / ל | infinitive with suffix | בְּשִׁבְתְּךָ → בְּמִתְּבָךְ; לֵאמֹר → לְמֵימַר | Deut 6:7; Gen 28:20 |
| infinitive absolute + finite | peal infinitive + finite | מוֹת תָּמוּת → מֵימַת תְּמוּת | Gen 2:17 |
| niphal, pual, hophal, hithpael | ithpeel, ithpaal, ittaphal | וַיִּתְחַבֵּא → וְאִיטַּמַּר | Gen 3:8 |
| hiphil | aphel | וַיַּבְדֵּל → וְאַפְרֵשׁ | Gen 1:4 |
| piel | pael | נִסָּה → נַסִּי | Gen 22:1 |
| verbless clause | verbless clause | אָנֹכִי יְהוָה → אֲנָא יְיָ | Exod 20:2 |

The Hebrew *wayyiqtol* has no Aramaic counterpart; Onkelos writes the perfect after ו, and vocalises the ו as וַ־ before a guttural or shewa like Hebrew (וַאֲמַר, וַחֲזָא, וַהֲוָה). This is a spelling coincidence, not a *waw*-consecutive.

## 6. Tense and aspect

Because Onkelos follows the Hebrew forms, its tense system looks more "Hebrew" than the spoken dialects:

- the **perfect** is the narrative past (Hebrew *wayyiqtol*);
- the **imperfect** is future, modal and the rendering of *weqatal*;
- the **participle** is present (אָזֵל "I am going", Gen 28:20) and, as in BA, the predicate of הוה for durative past;
- the participle as a narrative tense, so common in the Talmuds, is rare in Onkelos.

Jonathan to the Prophets is freer and uses the participle more, especially in its expansions (וּמְקַבְּלִין דֵין מִן דֵין וְאָמְרִין "and they receive (permission) one from another and say", Tg Isa 6:3).
