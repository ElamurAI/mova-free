# The lexicon of the targums

Onkelos is a translation, so its vocabulary is best described **against the Hebrew it translates**: for most Hebrew words there is one regular Aramaic equivalent, and learning the pairs is learning to read the targum. This file gives the frequent words, the regular Hebrew → Aramaic equivalents, the theological and rabbinic terms, and the loanwords.

Sources: Jastrow 1903 (cited s.v., public domain; local JSONL); Levy 1867–1868, *Chaldäisches Wörterbuch über die Targumim* (public domain; scan); Dalman 1905; Cook 2008, *A Glossary of Targum Onkelos* (Brill; in copyright, wanted). Counts are unpointed word tokens in the public-domain Onkelos (Sefaria), made on 2026-10-17; a spelling may cover more than one word (דבר is both "take!" and "word"; עבר "cross" and "side").

## 4. Loanwords

The targums have far fewer Greek and Latin words than the Talmuds and midrashim, because they translate a Hebrew text and keep to a formal register (Dalman 1905, introduction; Alexander 1992). What they have is mostly inherited from Imperial Aramaic or shared with the Bible:

| word | origin | meaning | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| פִּתְגָּמָא | Persian | word, thing | the normal word for Hebrew דָּבָר (77× פתגמא, 50× פתגמיא) |
| הֵיכְלָא | Akkadian (< Sumerian) | palace, temple | Tg Isa 6:1 |
| אִגַּרְתָּא | Akkadian | letter | |
| גִּזְבְּרָא | Persian | treasurer | rare |
| זִיוָא | Akkadian? | splendour | זִיו יְקָרֵיהּ (Tg Isa 6:3) |
| סַפְרָא | Akkadian-Aramaic | scribe | Gen 49:10 (וְסַפְרָא) |
| פְּרָסָא, פַּרְסָה | Persian (*parasang*) | a measure of distance; also "hoof" (Aramaic) | ambiguous spelling |
| קוֹלָסִין, אִיסְטְלָא etc. | Greek | | **not** found in Onkelos (0 hits for the tested forms, 2026-10-17); frequent in JPA ([../jpa/lexicon.md](../jpa/lexicon.md)) |

Checked 2026-10-18 (second pass): Krauss 1898, vol. 1, *Einleitung*, p. XXIII: every rabbinic work, the targums included, has Greek loanwords, but Onkelos is the purest of them; Krauss explains this by the careful language of Onkelos, not by a Babylonian origin. This confirms the opening sentence and the last row. The Persian and Akkadian etymologies of the other rows were not compared with Kohut's *Aruch* (unchecked: the OCR of Kohut has no Hebrew script and the entries were not looked up in the scans).
