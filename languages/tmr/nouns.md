# The noun in Jewish Babylonian Aramaic

JBA keeps the inherited categories — two genders, singular and plural, three states — but **the states no longer mean what they meant**. The determined form in ־ָא has become the ordinary form of the noun, definite or not; the absolute is left for predicates, numbers and fixed phrases; the construct survives in compounds and is otherwise replaced by ד־. The masculine plural has a new ending ־ֵי. Compare [../arc/nouns.md](../arc/nouns.md) for Biblical Aramaic.

References: Margolis 1910 (the noun, §§ 19–23); Epstein 1960; Bar-Asher Siegal 2013 (ch. on nominal morphology); Sokoloff 2002a (headwords and plurals). Counts are unpointed tokens of the Wikisource Bavli (CC BY-SA, measurement only), 2026-10-17; they include any homographs.

## 1. The states

| state | form | use in JBA | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| "determined" (now: **unmarked**) | ־ָא, f. ־ְתָא | the normal form of a noun, definite or indefinite | גַּבְרָא "a man / the man" (622×); מִילְּתָא "a thing / the matter" (743×); עָלְמָא "the world; people in general" (643×) |
| absolute | bare stem | predicates (adjectives, participles), after numerals, in distributive and fixed phrases | חַד גַּבְרָא but שְׁנִין "years" after a number: שַׁבְעִין שְׁנִין "seventy years" (Taanit 23a) |
| construct | bare stem | compounds and a few frequent nouns: בֵּי, בַּר, בַּת, רֵישׁ, מָרֵי | בֵּי רַב "the school of Rav"; רֵישׁ גָּלוּתָא "the Exilarch"; מָרֵי דְּאַרְעָא "the landowner" |

The loss of definiteness is shown by sentences where a ־ָא noun is clearly indefinite:

```
Aramaic:   חַזְיֵיהּ לְהַהוּא גַּבְרָא דַּהֲוָה נָטַע חָרוּבָא
Translit.: ḥazyēh lə-hahū gaḇrā də-hăwā nāṭaʿ ḥārūḇā (reconstr.)
Gloss:     see.PEAL.PF.3MS-3MS to-that man REL-be.PF.3MS plant.PEAL.PTCP.ACT.MS carob
English:   He saw a certain man who was planting a carob tree
Source:    b. Taanit 23a (Wikisource Bavli, CC BY-SA; written חזייה לההוא גברא דהוה נטע חרובא)
```

גַּבְרָא needs the demonstrative הַהוּא "that, a certain" to be identified; חָרוּבָא "a carob tree" is indefinite though it has the ־ָא ending. In Biblical Aramaic the same form would mean "the carob".

**Definiteness** is therefore marked, when it is marked at all, by **demonstratives** (הַאי "this", הַהוּא "that, a certain"), **suffixes**, the **ד־ genitive**, and context ([pronouns.md](pronouns.md) §5). For a model this means: the ־ָא ending is not a definiteness feature in `tmr` (unlike `arc`, `arc-x-targum`): in UD terms, `Definite` should not be set from the ending alone ([mova.md](mova.md) §4).

| construct | — | ־ַת | ־ֵי | ־ָת |

Second pass 2026-10-18: Levias 1900 §§985–990, pp. 227–228, pointed: m. pl. absolute ־ִין (rarely ־ִי), construct ־ֵי, determined ־ַיָּא and ־ֵי (זוּזֵי, Sanh. 29b); f. pl. absolute ־ָן, construct ־ָת, determined ־ָתָא; the absolute and construct are rare and the determined state "has largely lost its determining force" (§987). **Hebrew plurals in ־ִים / ־וֹת** occur in the Bavli in Hebrew passages and in Hebrew words used in Aramaic (שְׁנִים "years" 1 468×, mostly Hebrew contexts), against Aramaic שְׁנִין (109×) and שְׁנֵי (which is also the Hebrew construct "years of"). Plural endings are one of the cues for the Hebrew/Aramaic split ([mova.md](mova.md) §2).

## 3. The construct and the genitive

The construct chain is **alive only in compounds and a few nouns**:

| construct | meaning | example |
|---|---|---|
| בֵּי (from בֵּית) | house of; place of | בֵּי רַב "Rav's school"; בֵּי מִדְרְשָׁא "the house of study"; בֵּי דִּינָא "the court" |
| בַּר / בַּת | son of / daughter of; member of a class | בַּר בְּרֵיהּ "his grandson" (Taanit 23a); בַּר מִינֵּיהּ "one of his kind"; בַּר נָשׁ "a human being" |
| רֵישׁ | head of | רֵישׁ גָּלוּתָא "the Exilarch"; רֵישׁ לָקִישׁ (a name) |
| מָרֵי | master, owner of | מָרֵי דְּבֵיתָא "the owner of the house" (with ד־) |
| אֲבֵי / אַבָּא | father of | |

Everywhere else JBA uses **ד־**, very often with an **anticipatory suffix** on the first noun (the construction already seen in Biblical Aramaic, [../arc/nouns.md](../arc/nouns.md) §5):

| construction | example | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| noun + ד־ + noun | מִילְּתָא דְּרַבָּנַן | a matter of the rabbis |
| noun-SUFFIX + ד־ + noun | בְּרֵיהּ דְּרַב | Rav's son (lit. "his son, of Rav") |
| noun-SUFFIX + ד־ + noun | שְׁמֵיהּ דְּמָר | the master's name |
| noun-SUFFIX + ד־ + noun | בְּרֵיהּ דְּחוֹנִי הַמְעַגֵּל | Honi the circle-drawer's son (Taanit 23a) |

בְּרֵיהּ "his son" (1 401× as a written token בריה) shows how frequent the pattern is.

```
Aramaic:   בְּרֵיהּ דְּחוֹנִי הַמְעַגֵּל מִי קַיָּים
Translit.: bərēh də-ḥōnī ha-mʿaggēl mī qayyām (reconstr.)
Gloss:     son-3MS REL-Honi the-circle.drawer Q alive
English:   Is the son of Honi the circle-drawer alive?
Source:    b. Taanit 23a (Wikisource Bavli, CC BY-SA)
```

Note: הַמְעַגֵּל is a Hebrew participle with the Hebrew article (a name), and מִי here is the Hebrew-like interrogative particle "is …?" used in JBA questions.

## 4. Adjectives

Adjectives follow their noun and agree in gender and number; the "determined" ending is used as the ordinary attributive form, the absolute as the predicate:

| | attributive | predicative |
|---|---|---|
| m. sg. | גַּבְרָא רַבָּה "a great man" | גַּבְרָא רַבָּה הוּא / רַב "he is a great man" |
| f. sg. | מִילְּתָא רַבְּתָא "a great matter" | |
| m. pl. | גַּבְרֵי רַבְרְבֵי | |
| comparison | X … מִן Y "X more than Y"; טְפֵי מִן "more than" | |

Two adjectives are especially frequent as predicates in the argument: פְּשִׁיטָא "it is obvious!" (914×, a passive participle of פשט; [discourse-terms.md](discourse-terms.md)) and קַשְׁיָא "it is difficult" (1 899×).

## 6. Noun formation

JBA builds nouns as other Aramaic does ([../arc/nouns.md](../arc/nouns.md) §3), with some patterns especially productive in the language of argument:

| pattern | example | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *qəṭīl* passive participle as a noun or predicate | פְּשִׁיטָא "simple, obvious"; שְׁמִיעַ "heard" | |
| *qəṭāl-* + ־וּתָא abstract | תְּיוּבְתָּא "refutation"; קוּשְׁיָא "objection" | |
| *maqṭal* | מַתְנִיתָא / מַתְנִיתִין "a Mishnah / our Mishnah" | |
| *qaṭṭāl* (agent) | תַּנָּא "repeater, tanna"; סַפְרָא "scribe" | |
| gentilic ־ָאָה, pl. ־ָאֵי | בַּבְלָאָה "Babylonian", pl. בַּבְלָאֵי; פַּרְסָאָה "Persian"; אָמוֹרָאֵי | |
| diminutive ־וֹן, ־וּנָא | | rare |
