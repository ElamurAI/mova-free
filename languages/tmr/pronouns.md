# Pronouns in Jewish Babylonian Aramaic

JBA has the richest pronoun system of the Jewish Aramaic varieties: independent pronouns in two forms (plain and emphatic), **enclitic** subject pronouns that fuse with participles, **copular** pronouns (נִיהוּ, נִינְהוּ), a full set of independent possessives (דִּידִי …), and demonstratives built on הָ־. Compare [../arc/pronouns.md](../arc/pronouns.md).

References: Margolis 1910 §§ 8–11; Epstein 1960; Bar-Asher Siegal 2013 (pronouns); Wikipedia, "Jewish Babylonian Aramaic" (tables; retrieved 2026-10-17); Jastrow 1903 s.vv. Counts: Wikisource Bavli (CC BY-SA, measurement only), unpointed tokens, 2026-10-17.

Checked 2026-10-18 against Margolis 1910 (§§ 8–11, pp. 16–18; Tab. II, VII, VIII), read from the page images (the OCR has no Hebrew script). Margolis prints JBA unvocalised, so only the consonants are compared; he marks forms of the earlier language (targumic quotations, old formulas) with †, which this file writes as "archaic".

Notes:

- **אֲנַן "we"** replaces אֲנַחְנָא. **אִינְהוּ "they"** comes from *ʾinnūn* + *hū* (Bar-Asher Siegal 2013); the ending ־הוּ reappears in the suffixes (־הוּ "their").
- **אִיהוּ** is an emphatic "he (himself)"; in the Zohar it becomes the general copula ([../arc-x-zohar/grammar.md](../arc-x-zohar/grammar.md)).
- **The copula נִיהוּ / נִינְהוּ** stands at the end of a nominal clause: אֲנָא נִיהוּ "I am he" (Taanit 23a); מַאן נִינְהוּ "who are they?". Jastrow s.v. נִיהוּ: it is אִיהוּ "mostly after an open syllable to avoid a hiatus, or after ן".

```
Aramaic:   אָמַר לְהוּ אֲנָא נִיהוּ לָא הֵימְנוּהוּ
Translit.: ʾāmar ləhū ʾănā nīhū lā hēmnūhū (reconstr.)
Gloss:     say.PEAL.PF.3MS to-3MPL I COP.3MS NEG believe.APH.PF.3MPL-3MS
English:   He said to them, "I am he." They did not believe him.
Source:    b. Taanit 23a (Wikisource Bavli, CC BY-SA; written אמר להו אנא ניהו לא הימנוהו)
```

## 2. Enclitic subject pronouns

The 1st and 2nd person pronouns attach to an active or passive **participle** (and to some adjectives) to form the present tense (Margolis 1910 §8a; Bar-Asher Siegal 2013):

| person | form | example | meaning | count |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| 1 sg. | ptcp. + ־נָא | אָמֵינָא | I say | (הוה אמינא 407×) |
| 1 sg. | | יָדַעְנָא | I know | (ידענא in BM 2a) |
| 2 m. sg. | ptcp. + ־תְּ | אָמְרַתְּ | you say | |
| 1 pl. | ptcp. + ־ינַן | אָמְרִינַן, שָׁמְעִינַן, חָזֵינַן | we say, we hear, we see | חזינן 67 |
| 2 m. pl. | ptcp. + ־יתוּ | אָמְרִיתוּ | you (pl.) say | |
| passive ptcp. | + ־נָא | עֲסִיקְנָא "I am busy"; שְׁמִיעַ לִי "I have heard" (lit. "it is heard to me") | | |

These are the most "Babylonian" forms of all; in unpointed text they look like perfects or nouns (אמינא, ידענא) and must be analysed as participle + pronoun ([mova.md](mova.md) §3).

## 5. Demonstratives

| | near: this, these | far: that, those | "the aforementioned" |
|---|---|---|---|
| m. sg. | **הַאי** (2 246×) | **הַהוּא** (1 382×) | הָאיךְ (13×) |
| f. sg. | **הָא** (also "this" generally; 6 734× including the particle הָא "behold") | **הַהִיא** (404×) | הָךְ (151×) |
| pl. | **הָנֵי** (1 196×) | **הָנְהוּ** (106×) | **הָנָךְ** (221×) |

(Forms after Wikipedia and Jastrow; counts 2026-10-17, הָאיךְ and הָךְ 2026-10-18.)

Checked 2026-10-18: Margolis 1910 §9, pp. 17–18: near הַאי, הָא, הָנֵי (early דין, דא, אילין and הדין, הדא, הלין); far ההוא, ההיא, הנהו and האיך, f. הך, pl. הנך. Corrected: הַךְ is the feminine; the masculine of this series is הָאיךְ. Second pass 2026-10-18: Levias 1900 §§116–117, p. 35: near m. הַאי, f. הָא, f. pl. הָנֵי; far m. הָהוּא (rarer הָאִיךְ), f. הָהִיא, הָךְ, pl. הָנָךְ, אִינָךְ, common אִידָךְ "the other". Corrected: the feminine is pointed הָךְ (qameṣ) in Levias, so the table's הַךְ is changed. Disagreements: Levias points the far forms with qameṣ (הָהוּא, הָהִיא) where the table has pataḥ; and he lists הָנְהוּ among the near masculine plurals (§116), while Margolis has it as far (both given; the table keeps Margolis).

Uses:

- **הַאי** precedes its noun: הַאי גַּבְרָא "this man"; הַאי קְרָא "this verse". It also stands alone: הַאי מַאי "what is this?". It is used for feminine nouns too in later texts (Margolis 1910 §9: "used incorrectly as f.").
- **הַהוּא** "that; a certain" introduces new participants in stories: הַהוּא גַּבְרָא "a certain man" (Taanit 23a; 1 382× in all), הַהִיא אִיתְּתָא "a certain woman". It works like an indefinite article in narrative.
- **הָא … הָא** "this … that" divides cases: הָא בְּ־X הָא בְּ־Y "this one refers to X, that one to Y" — the standard way of resolving a contradiction (לָא קַשְׁיָא, הָא … הָא, [discourse-terms.md](discourse-terms.md)).
- **Adverbs** from demonstratives: הָכָא "here, in this case" (3 063×), הָתָם "there, in that case" (2 411×), הָכִי "thus" (4 285×), הַשְׁתָּא "now", הָאִידָּנָא "now, nowadays".
