# Jewish Babylonian Aramaic (`tmr`)

Jewish Babylonian Aramaic (JBA) is the Aramaic of the Jews of Mesopotamia in the Sasanian and early Islamic periods (3rd–11th c. CE): the language of the **Babylonian Talmud** (the Bavli), of the geonic responsa and halakhic works, of the Aramaic **incantation bowls** written by and for Jews, and of the legal formulas (the *get*, the *ketubbah*) still used today. It is an **Eastern** Aramaic dialect, a close relative of Syriac and especially of Mandaic.

The code is ISO 639-3 `tmr` ("Jewish Babylonian Aramaic (ca. 200–1200 CE)"). Conventions: [../arc/README.md](../arc/README.md). Since vocalised witnesses are few and late, forms here are given in the **broad transcription** with vowels after the grammars (Margolis 1910; Epstein 1960; Bar-Asher Siegal 2013) and the Yemenite reading tradition (Morag 1988), **as reconstructions**.

## 1. What it is

- **The Bavli** is a record of discussions in the academies of Babylonia (Sura, Pumbedita, Nehardea, Maḥoza) from the 3rd to the 5th c. CE, edited and expanded by anonymous editors (the *stammaim*) in the 6th–7th c. Its frame language is JBA; inside it are quotations in Hebrew (the Mishnah, baraitot, many sayings of the sages) and in Palestinian Aramaic (statements of Palestinian sages, some stories). Telling these apart sentence by sentence is the first problem a model meets ([mova.md](mova.md) §2).
- **Not one layer.** Within the Bavli, scholars distinguish (Epstein 1960; Kutscher 1971; Bar-Asher Siegal 2013, ch. 1):
  - the **standard** language of most tractates;
  - the language of the **"irregular" tractates** — Nedarim, Nazir, Me'ila, Keritot, Tamid (and parts of Temura) — with forms closer to Mandaic and to geonic texts (e.g. other forms of the pronouns and the imperfect);
  - the **geonic** language (She'iltot, Halakhot Pesuqot, responsa), later and more standardised;
  - Anan ben David's *Sefer ha-Miṣwot* (8th c.), the Karaite founder's law book in JBA.
- **The incantation bowls**: several thousand clay bowls from 5th–7th c. Mesopotamia, inscribed in JBA (and in Syriac and Mandaic) with spells against demons. They are the only JBA texts that survive as originals, not in medieval copies (Montgomery 1913; Naveh and Shaked 1985, 1993; Shaked, Ford and Bhayro 2013).
- **Reading traditions.** The Bavli was read aloud in study for fifteen centuries; the oral traditions of Yemen (Morag 1988), Iraq and Ashkenaz differ. Early **Eastern manuscripts** (Geniza fragments, the Yemenite manuscripts) preserve older spellings and forms than the printed Vilna edition (Morgenstern 2011, *Studies in Jewish Babylonian Aramaic Based upon Early Eastern Manuscripts*).

## 2. What marks it

Against **all western Jewish Aramaic** (`arc`, `arc-x-targum`, `jpa`):

| feature | JBA | others | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 m. sg. imperfect prefix | **ל־ / נ־** | י־ | לֵימָא "let him say; shall we say?" (903×), נֵימָא "let us say" (400×), לִיתְנֵי "let him teach" |
| m. pl. determined | **־ֵי** *-ē* | ־ַיָּא *-ayyā* | רַבָּנַן "the rabbis" (*-ān*), מִילֵּי "words, things", אִינָשֵׁי "people" |
| the determined state | **unmarked**: no longer definite | definite | גַּבְרָא "a man / the man" |
| "there is / is not" | **אִיכָּא / לֵיכָּא** (2 488 / 381×) | אִית / לֵית | |
| "this, these" | **הַאי, הָא, הָנֵי** | דְּנָה, הָדֵין | הַאי גַּבְרָא "this man" |
| "what?" | **מַאי** (9 108×) | מָה | |
| "now" | **הַשְׁתָּא** (974×) | כְּעַן, כְּדוֹן | |
| durative particle | **קָא** (2 828× as a word, and fused: קָאָמַר, קָתָנֵי) | — | קָא אָמַר "he is saying" |
| "he" (emphatic, copula) | **אִיהוּ**, **נִיהוּ**; pl. **אִינְהוּ**, **נִינְהוּ** | הוּא, אִנּוּן | אֲנָא נִיהוּ "I am he" (Taanit 23a) |
| enclitic subject pronouns | **־נָא, ־תְּ, ־נַן, ־תּוּ** on participles | rare | אָמֵינָא "I say", אָמְרִינַן "we say" |
| object | **ל־**, with **prolepsis**: suffix on the verb + ל־ + noun | יָת (targum) | אַשְׁכְּחֵיהּ רַבִּי נָתָן לְאֵלִיָּהוּ "R. Nathan found Elijah" (BM 59b) |
| object marker יָת | almost absent | frequent (targum) | |
| loss of final consonants | -n, -m, -t often drop | kept | תְּרֵי "two" (BA תְּרֵין); 3 m. pl. suffix ־הוּ (BA ־הוֹן) |
| guttural weakening | ה ~ ח ~ ע ~ א confused in some words | kept apart (targum, BA) | |
| vocabulary | שְׁקַל "take", חֲזָא "see", טוּבָא "much", אָזַל "go", מִשּׁוּם "because" | נְסַב, חֲמָא (JPA), סַגִּי | |

Against **Hebrew**: the whole Aramaic grammar ([../arc/family.md](../arc/family.md) §4); in the Bavli the two languages alternate inside a page, sometimes inside a sentence (Shabbat 31a: a Hebrew baraita whose punchline is in Aramaic, [texts.md](texts.md) §2).

Against **Syriac and Mandaic**, its eastern sisters: JBA shares the plural ־ֵי and the loss of definiteness with Syriac, and the imperfect with ל־ / נ־ with both (Syriac נ־, Mandaic נ־ / ל־); it shares more sound changes and vocabulary with Mandaic (Bar-Asher Siegal 2013, ch. 1; Kutscher 1971).
