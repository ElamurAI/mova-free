# Annex A — Elamur Cosmic License and Elamur API Terms

Status: working draft  
Version: 2026-10-14

## Purpose

This Annex identifies restricted jurisdictions for specific government, security, intelligence, state-controlled, and proxy uses of works under the Elamur Cosmic License and of the Elamur API and tokens (2026-10-15; Mova Extended is postponed).

The list is intentionally narrower than generic "Not Free", "authoritarian", or "autocracy" indexes. A jurisdiction is included only where the current working assessment finds a serious combination of authoritarian rule, systemic political repression, grave rights abuses, aggressive war, or high-risk state surveillance and censorship practices.

Restrictions are directed at prohibited institutions and prohibited uses. They are not based on nationality, ethnicity, citizenship, or residence.

## Annual-review jurisdictions

### Uzbekistan

Remain restricted for now. Review annually. Reforms since 2016 make Uzbekistan more dynamic than the hardest closed regimes, and substantive political liberalization could justify removal in a future revision.

### Bahrain

Remain restricted for now. Review annually. The concern is not monarchy itself, but political repression, elimination of meaningful opposition, political imprisonment, repression of dissent, and surveillance risk.

## Scope principle

The restriction should apply to prohibited institutional users and prohibited uses, including:

- political repression;
- mass or population-scale surveillance for political control;
- censorship enforcement;
- persecution of dissidents;
- unlawful targeting;
- discriminatory population profiling;
- support for torture or enforced disappearance;
- prohibited military or security uses;
- assistance to designated repressive security or intelligence services.

## Nationality is not a prohibited characteristic

The Annex must not be interpreted as a ban on people from listed countries.

Examples of users who should remain eligible under the ordinary license terms include:

- independent researchers;
- students;
- journalists;
- human-rights defenders;
- dissidents;
- civil-society organizations;
- independent developers;
- ordinary private users;
- private organizations not acting for a prohibited institution.

The project restricts dangerous institutional use, not people by passport.
