## strict_record_transport

Type: framing_consumer_domain. Let B be existing partial bytes, Q unique request ids, and P ids of its parsed nonblank succeeded rows. Define tail(B) as the suffix after the last LF and sep(B) as tail(B) containing a non-whitespace character under the worker's current trim predicate. After complete validation, the worker appends LF iff sep(B), then appends each new result row with LF. Thus a real unterminated record is separated, while a whitespace-only tail remains a prefix of the next row.

A strict consumer parses every physical line as JSON, unlike the worker's nonblank-row projection. Its stronger admitted domain requires every existing completed physical line and any non-whitespace final tail to be a complete JSON row, and any final whitespace-only suffix to contain only JSON whitespace (space, TAB, CR) and be followed by at least one new row, unless that suffix is empty. On this domain, successful publication retains B as a byte prefix and transports the parsed records in order P ++ filter(Q,id not in P), without introducing a blank physical line. A complete last row ending in bare CR is valid JSON plus whitespace; adding LF yields CRLF.

Counterexample to the earlier separator rule: B="   " followed by a successful row. Unconditional LF for any nonempty non-LF tail creates a blank line that a strict consumer rejects; preserving the spaces as JSON-leading whitespace retains compatibility. Preexisting blank physical lines, all-done whitespace tails and Unicode-only whitespace are not promised strict-reader acceptance; the tolerant worker can admit more inputs. The integrated strict-reader regression owns executable evidence. Files already carrying a final sentinel bypass queue validation and are outside the transition domain.

## resume_work_preimages

Type: operational_work_fiber. Fix Q with unique ids and an accepted ordered partial subset P. Observe source-level calls to ask during one successful completion. The call sequence is filter(Q,id not in set(P)); its length is |Q|-|P|. Separator repair adds no ask and does not change request identity. Partial order is retained, so Q=[a,b,c], P=[b] publishes [b,a,c] while asking only [a,c]. This concerns the queue's call schedule, not internal work or billing of a subprocess.

For fixed final row order S, every accepted P satisfying S=P ++ filter(Q,id not in set(P)) remains a compatible initial state, together with arbitrary prior failed attempts and supplied row bodies admitted by the envelope checks. Final bytes do not uniquely reconstruct which prior rows came from a real call, a supplied partial or earlier explicit resumes. An ask failure leaves completed rows and the optional separator; sticky failure prevents implicit new calls until explicitly cleared. A final-file observation alone is not evidence of training quality or persistent learned knowledge.

Parsing and validation retain the existing O(B+|P|*|Q|) work plus serialization and external calls. The new tail decision does not imply a measured lesson-throughput improvement. Fake-process fixtures test byte framing, order, skip counts and explicit failure/resume only.
