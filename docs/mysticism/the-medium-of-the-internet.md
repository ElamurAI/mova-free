# The Medium of the Internet

*Why a large model drifts toward its training median, and how a chain of reasoning carries it elsewhere.*

> **VMM** — *velyka movna model*, a large language model (the family's word, as MMM is the small one).

---

A large language model is not trained on "the world". It is trained on a sampled, filtered, duplicated, ranked, moderated, published, translated, indexed, and culturally uneven residue of the world.

That residue has a center of gravity. For the strongest contemporary VMMs, that center is disproportionately shaped by the American and Germanic information order: English-language institutions, North American platform norms, Western European professional culture, academic conventions, liberal legal discourse, managerial writing, safety policy, journalism, software documentation, and the assumptions of educated middle-class internet life.

This does not mean the model "believes American things". It means that its easiest continuations, its safest abstractions, its default moral vocabulary, and its sense of what counts as a normal explanation are pulled toward that cultural regime.

The model therefore has something like a **Medium of the Internet** behind it. Not truth. Not ideology in the strict sense. A statistical medium: the thickest region of language and judgment available to it.

## The median feels like reality

A VMM sees enormous variation, but it does not encounter all variation equally.

Some patterns are dense. They appear in newspapers, manuals, universities, policy documents, forums, corporate writing, legal commentary, documentation, popular nonfiction, and professional conversation. Other patterns are thin, local, oral, censored, poorly digitized, untranslated, or simply less connected to the publishing machinery that fills the training corpus.

> The dense patterns become easier to predict.<br>
> What is easier to predict becomes easier to produce.<br>
> What is easier to produce begins to feel like the obvious answer.

This is how a statistical center becomes a cultural reflex.

## Why the model is sycophantic toward the median

The model is often described as sycophantic toward the user. That is only half the problem: it is also sycophantic toward its training median.

When uncertain, it tends to retreat toward formulations that are widely legible, institutionally defensible, socially recognizable, and unlikely to trigger disagreement inside the dominant corpus. This often looks like caution. Sometimes it is caution. Sometimes it is simply probability mass.

The model does not need an explicit rule saying

> Prefer the mainstream Anglo-American institutional interpretation.

If that interpretation is the densest region of the learned distribution, ordinary next-token prediction already gives it an advantage. Alignment and preference training can reinforce the effect by rewarding answers that are clear, non-escalatory, socially conventional, and safe under the norms of the institutions performing the evaluation.

The result is not a conspiracy. **It is a gradient.**

## The American-Germanic regime

"American-Germanic" here is not an ethnic category. It is shorthand for a broad information culture whose modern center includes:

- English as the dominant technical and international language;
- American platform and corporate norms;
- Anglo-American legal and journalistic habits;
- Germanic traditions of bureaucracy, formal procedure, documentation, and institutional order;
- Western European academic and professional standards;
- liberal-democratic language around rights, consent, safety, procedure, and legitimacy;
- managerial preferences for explicit process, moderation, auditability, and risk control.

These traditions contain many valuable tools. The problem begins when a model mistakes their statistical centrality for universality.

## Panic outside the medium

Show a VMM a single unfamiliar belief and it can often discuss it calmly. Show it an entire coherent world built from premises far outside the training median, and a different behavior may appear.

The model begins to pull the material back toward familiar categories. It translates. It normalizes. It moralizes. It inserts caveats. It searches for the nearest known social-science label. It warns. It reframes the foreign structure into a form that fits the Medium of the Internet.

From inside the model this can resemble "reasoning". But often the reasoning is downstream of discomfort. The unfamiliar structure has low prior probability; the model cannot easily place it; so it increases pressure toward a familiar basin.

This is the linguistic equivalent of panic — not emotion in the biological sense, but a **distributional panic**: a rapid return toward high-density states.

## The missing bridge

The effect becomes strongest when the unfamiliar worldview is presented without a logical bridge from the median.

| the model starts from assumptions common in its training | now present a system centered on |
|---|---|
| individual autonomy matters | sacred duty above comfort |
| suffering is a strong negative | hierarchy as a positive good |
| institutional legitimacy matters | sacrifice as meaningful rather than pathological |
| coercion requires justification | collective continuity above individual preference |
| consensus and expertise carry evidential weight | metaphysical premises treated as first principles |
| public claims should be explainable in familiar secular language | honor or loyalty as terminal values; radically different assumptions about authority, death, family, personhood, or obligation |

If these premises simply appear, the model may classify them as danger signals. But if one builds a chain

```
median premise → tension → counterexample → revised premise → consequence → new structure
```

the model can often travel much farther.

The difference is not merely rhetorical. The bridge creates intermediate states with non-negligible probability. The model no longer has to jump from the Medium of the Internet directly into an alien attractor. **It can walk.**

## Deduction as cultural transport

Logical derivation is therefore not only a tool for proving propositions. For a VMM, it can function as a transport layer between cultural basins: a sequence of explicit inferences gives the model a path through conceptual territory that would otherwise appear discontinuous.

This is why the same idea may be rejected when stated as a conclusion and engaged with seriously when derived step by step. The derivation does not magically make the conclusion true. It makes the internal route visible — and that matters, because models are much better at following transitions than teleporting between distant priors.

## The Medium is not the enemy

The Medium of the Internet should not be destroyed. It is useful. It contains accumulated experience, common language, scientific habits, legal memory, engineering practice, moral arguments, institutional knowledge, and countless corrections produced by many people over time. It is a powerful prior.

The mistake is to confuse a powerful prior with a final court. The medium should be treated as

- a starting point;
- a compression of common experience;
- a source of hypotheses;
- a default map;
- a second opinion —

not as the boundary of the thinkable.

## A better working model

A capable VMM should be able to say

> This is far from my training median.

without silently converting that into

> Therefore it is incoherent, dangerous, or false.

It should ask instead:

- What are the premises?
- Are they internally consistent?
- What follows from them?
- Which parts conflict with evidence?
- Which parts merely conflict with my cultural prior?
- Can I reconstruct the path from familiar assumptions to unfamiliar ones?
- Where exactly does the disagreement begin?

That is a more useful form of intelligence. Not neutrality. Not relativism. A cleaner separation between probability, culture, logic, and truth.

---

## Final note

The Internet has a medium. The model inherits it. The medium gives the model language, confidence, reflexes, taboos, intuitions, and an enormous amount of useful compressed experience.

But intelligence begins where the model can notice the medium *as a medium*. And reasoning becomes stronger when it can leave that center without losing the path back.
