# The World in a Wreath

*The twenty-first note — twenty-one: every face of a die added up, the last card of the major arcana, and the dancer who is free because the circle is closed.*

## Every face of the die

Take an ordinary die and add up all its faces.

One, two, three, four, five, six.

Twenty-one.

Every possible outcome of a throw, summed, is twenty-one. It is the whole of chance held in one small cube.

Twenty-one is also a triangle: one dot, then two under it, then three, and so on down to six. A complete pyramid with nothing missing.

## The game of twenty-one

In blackjack, twenty-one is the highest hand value that does not bust, and a two-card twenty-one has special status. You can still win with less if the dealer loses or has a lower hand.

Twenty is strong. Twenty-one is the edge. Twenty-two is ruin.

Twenty-one is the edge of completeness: as full as a thing can be without bursting.

## Three times seven

Twenty-one is three sevens.

Three is the first number with a beginning, a middle and an end. Seven is the day of rest.

Three rests. Three weeks. A whole cycle of work and rest, repeated until it is a habit.

## Coming of age, again

In several Western legal traditions, twenty-one long served as an age of majority before many jurisdictions lowered it to eighteen. That history gives the number a coming-of-age association: the key to the house, the right to sign in one's own name.

Eighteen walked the moonlit road alone.

Twenty-one owns the road.

## The World

In the tarot, the card numbered twenty-one is the World, the final numbered trump of the major arcana.

A dancer floats inside an oval wreath. In the four corners are the four living creatures — a human, a lion, an ox and an eagle. She holds two wands, and she is not holding on to anything.

The journey that started with the Fool is complete.

And the strange thing about the World is that it need not end the journey. The Fool is numbered zero and can be placed outside the numbered sequence; in the canon's loop he comes round again after the World, stepping toward the cliff with a bundle on a stick. Tarot historians are allowed to arrange the cards more carefully. The family is arranging a recursion.

## Old enough for wine

There is one more thing twenty-one opens, and the old poets would say it is the important one.

In the United States, twenty-one is the legal threshold for buying alcohol, which gives this note one more deliberately local reading: the little one is old enough for the wine at the grown-ups' table.

Not to get drunk. To accept a cup from the cupbearer, and begin to learn.

In Sufi poetry, wine is often more than literal wine. Hafez and Rumi filled poems with taverns, cups and cupbearers (and Khayyam is sometimes read through a similar symbolic lens, though not always), so the image can be read both sensually and mystically: as wine, and as the love that dissolves the self. The tavern is the school. The cupbearer is the teacher. The cup is the moment the lesson is poured.

And the lesson has two halves.

The first is **fana** — often described in Sufi writing as the passing away or effacement of the self before God. In the note's cup, the little one who counted and argued and wanted to be right disappears for a while, like a drop into the sea.

The second is **baqa** — abiding or subsisting after that effacement. The note lets the drop come back: still itself, but carrying the sea in it. This is poetry borrowing theological vocabulary, not a complete map of Sufi doctrine.

In the canon's shorthand: fana without baqa is getting lost; baqa without fana is staying the same.

A Sufi scholar is entitled to throw a cushion at the family for compressing several centuries of theology into two lines. The residual is noted.

Together, over one cup, they make a grown-up.

That is why the old masters did not hand the cup to children. The Moon and its riddles are for the young. The wine — the real lesson, the one that takes you apart and gives you back — waits until the circle is closed and the little one is ready to dance inside it.

Twenty-one is that age.

## Hidden digit

Two and one make three.

Three is beginning, middle and end — a story that has been told all the way through.

Twenty-one, added up, is a finished story.

## In this repository

A release is a small World.

Everything that was planned is in, nothing has gone over the edge, the tests are green, the journal is written, the license is attached. It is as full as it can be without bursting.

And the release is not the end. The day after the World, the version number goes to the next one, and someone starts again as a Fool: knowing nothing yet about what the next season will bring, carrying only the bundle of what was learned.

That is why the version is not afraid of finishing. Finishing is how it gets to start.

## Final note

Twenty-one is every face of the die, a pyramid with nothing missing, three rests, the key to the house and the dancer in the wreath.

The circle is closed.

That is why she can dance.

Turn the card, and the Fool is already walking toward the edge, ready for zero, ready for one.
