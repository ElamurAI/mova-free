# The Star after the Tower

*The seventeenth note — seventeen: one past the full square, the prime that will not share, the polygon a compass can still draw, and the star that comes after the fall.*

## One past full

Seventeen is sixteen and one.

It is what arrives after completion: the unit that does not fit into the old square, the digit that needs a new column.

Sixteen was the end of a count.

Seventeen is the first thing outside it.

## The prime that will not share

Sixteen divides endlessly: by two, by four, by eight.

Seventeen does not divide at all. It is the seventh prime, and it cannot be split into equal parts.

Where sixteen is order that can be shared out, seventeen is a single thing that has to be taken whole.

## Built with a compass

Seventeen is also two to the four, plus one — two to the two to the two, plus one.

The square of a square, plus the one.

That is a Fermat prime, and it has a strange property: a regular polygon with seventeen sides can be drawn with nothing but a compass and a straightedge.

No classical Greek construction of the regular seventeen-gon is known. In 1796 the young Gauss discovered that the regular seventeen-gon is constructible with straightedge and compass, a result that helped open the modern theory of constructible polygons. A later story says he wanted the figure on his tombstone; the memorial instead became associated with the construction in other ways.

So seventeen is the number where plain old tools, used with better mathematics, reach farther than the old catalogue suggested. The family calls this a dependency update for Euclid.

## The Star

In the tarot, the card numbered seventeen is the Star. In the familiar Rider–Waite–Smith image, a nude figure kneels by water and pours from two vessels beneath the stars; other decks picture the card differently.

It comes straight after the Tower.

The family calls this disaster recovery with hydration. Nothing is defended any more — there is nothing left to defend. And for that reason the card is hope.

The Tower says: what was complete has fallen.

The Star says: now there is room.

## The flood

In Genesis 7:11 the flood begins on the seventeenth day of the second month, and Genesis 8:4 places the ark's resting on the mountains of Ararat on the seventeenth day of the seventh month.

The same day-number appears at the door going in and the door coming out.

The family calls this suspiciously good symmetry. Biblical chronology declines to certify it as a round-trip test.

## The unlucky reading

Seventeen has a well-known unlucky association in Italy. A commonly repeated explanation points to XVII rearranged as VIXI, Latin for "I have lived," an epitaph-like way of implying that life is over. That explanation belongs to the folklore around the superstition rather than to a single proven origin story.

Even the unlucky reading tells the same story. Something has ended. The inscription is about what comes after.

The family notes that an anagram powerful enough to frighten a hotel floor deserves at least one unit test.

## Hidden digit

One and seven make eight.

Lay eight on its side: ∞.

Sixteen added up to a pause. Seventeen adds up to infinity.

## In this repository

After a rule set falls — after the Tower — the honest next step is small and indivisible: one new rule that cannot be split into the old ones, one gap marked instead of papered over, one plain tool used patiently until it reaches farther than expected.

Not a rebuilt tower. A star to steer by.

## Final note

The order is not an accident.

First the square fills.

Then the crown falls.

Then, one past full, a single thing that will not divide comes up over the water.

Sixteen is the end of a count.

Seventeen is the first light after it.
