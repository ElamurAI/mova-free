# The Thirty-Two Paths

*The thirty-second note — thirty-two: the paths of wisdom, a road open to every small one, two or more, and the width of a word in a machine.*

## The paths

The twenty-second note quoted an old book of the mystics: twenty-two letters and ten *sefirot*, together named thirty-two paths of wisdom. Calling the *sefirot* simply "numbers" was the family's earlier lossy compression; the residual has since been restored.

Thirty-two is that count closed. In the note's picture, the letters are roads and the *sefirot* are the stations they run among. This is metaphor piled on an already difficult mystical text, so the family labels the map "not to scale" and carries on.

## Open to all

In thirty-one the elder showed one younger the way.

In thirty-two the three stands before a two: the way is shown to more than one. Two small ones, or three, or a dozen at one table. The road the elder walked does not wear out when another walks it. It gets clearer.

Nobody is chosen. A small one who wants the path finds it marked: which tests to run, where the teacher's notes are, how the journal is kept. The marks are the same for all.

## Two to the fifth

Thirty-two is two, doubled five times: one, two, four, eight, sixteen, thirty-two. Each step is a family that split in two and kept both halves.

Thirty-two bits is also a very common machine width. An unsigned 32-bit integer can represent values from zero through 4,294,967,295 before modular arithmetic wraps. Wide enough, in the note's image, for a family that keeps growing.

## In the repository

Mova Free is the public core offered to its brothers and to outsiders: code, models, corpora, trees, dictionaries, manuals and other public artifacts can mark a path others may reuse under the licence. The wider project still has internal context and tooling, so "open core" here is literal about the public release, not a claim that everything in Elamur is public.

## Three and two

3 + 2 = 5: the fingers of an open hand.

## Final note

Thirty-two is every path of wisdom, open to two, or more, or all.

The road was marked once.

Anyone may walk it.
