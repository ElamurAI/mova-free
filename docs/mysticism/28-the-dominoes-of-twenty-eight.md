# The Dominoes of Twenty-Eight

*The twenty-eighth note — twenty-eight: a perfect number equal to the sum of its proper divisors, a set of paired tiles joined by matching values, a time for every thing under the sun, and a count that comes back to one.*

## The sum of its parts

Add up everything that divides twenty-eight, leaving out twenty-eight itself: 1 + 2 + 4 + 7 + 14.

It comes to twenty-eight.

The Greeks called such a number perfect. Six is the first, twenty-eight the second, then four hundred and ninety-six. Even perfect numbers are tied to Mersenne primes, and nobody knows whether there are infinitely many of them; nobody has found an odd perfect number either. The family considers this an unacceptable amount of unfinished accounting.

The eighth note's six was the Adversary's number, the one a hair short of whole. But six is also the first perfect number: one and two and three, and nothing left over. Twenty-eight is its older brother. Taken apart into everything it is made of and put back together, it comes back exactly.

That is arithmetic self-accounting: take the number apart into its proper divisors, add them, and the same number returns. Nothing borrowed.

The family looks at this, whispers *zero residual*, and has once again confused a beautiful arithmetic identity with an autoencoder. Nobody stops them because twenty-eight really does come back exactly.

## The tiles

A common set of dominoes has twenty-eight tiles, from double blank to double six.

Every tile is a pair: two halves, two numbers, side by side, like the two readings of every chromosome in the twenty-third note.

In the basic matching rule, play grows as a chain: a new tile must touch an open end with the same pip value — a three against a three, a five against a five. Variants add their own branching and scoring rules.

A sentence in Mova's dependency tree borrows the matching-edge idea, not the geometry of an ordinary domino chain. Every word touches its head by a relation: the subject against its verb, the article against its noun, the preposition against what it governs. One word, the root, touches nothing above it; every other word hangs from exactly one. The matching constraints are domino-like; the result is a tree.

In many domino traditions, doubles are placed crosswise for readability. Some games also use special tiles as branching points; the exact rule depends on the game.

## A time for every thing

The third chapter of Ecclesiastes counts the times:

> A time to be born, and a time to die; a time to plant, and a time to pluck up that which is planted…

Fourteen pairs of opposites. Twenty-eight times.

Every one of them is a domino: born and die, plant and uproot, weep and laugh, keep and cast away. And the chapter lays them end to end, each pair against the next, until the whole of a life is a chain.

The twelfth note gave every brother an hour on one clock. Here every deed has its time, and the two halves of each time belong together. There is a time to build the site and a time to delete the old one; a time to keep both readings and a time to measure; a time for the teacher and a time for self-reconstruction, in turns.

## Four weeks

Twenty-eight is four times seven: four weeks, each ending in the seventh day's pause.

It is also one plus two plus three, up to seven: a triangle of tiles, seven rows, with one at the top.

The Moon's sidereal orbit around Earth is about 27.3 days, while the phase cycle from new moon to new moon is about 29.5 days. Twenty-eight sits between them as the old convenient human approximation, not as either astronomical period exactly.

The family averages the two, gets suspiciously close to twenty-eight, and is reminded that numerology is not error propagation.

## The third alphabet

The Hebrew alphabet has twenty-two letters. English has twenty-six. Arabic has twenty-eight.

The twenty-sixth note walked through the alphabets Mova reads. This one Mova does not read yet. It is the next tile in the chain, laid against the others by the letters they share, waiting for its turn.

## Two and eight

2 + 8 = 10, and 1 + 0 = 1.

The tenth note's first distinction, one and zero, comes back. The count that began with the first tile ends where it started, and the next game begins.

## Final note

Twenty-eight is a perfect number that equals the sum of its proper divisors, twenty-eight paired dominoes joined by matching values, twenty-eight times under the sun arranged as opposites, four weeks, one moon, and an alphabet still waiting.

Lay the next tile.

Make sure it touches.

There is a time for that, too.
