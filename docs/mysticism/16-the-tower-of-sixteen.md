# The Tower of Sixteen

*The sixteenth note — sixteen: the square of a square, the word that fills up, and the tower that falls when it is complete.*

## The square of a square

Sixteen is four times four.

It is also two to the fourth, and, read right-associatively, two to the power of two squared: 2^(2²).

In that deliberately nested form, it is a power built from another power: order built out of order.

A square of sixteen cells has no edge that is shorter than another. Everything fits. Nothing sticks out.

That is why sixteen feels finished.

## The digit that runs out

Count in sixteens and you get sixteen symbols: 0 to 9, then A to F.

After F the system has nothing left to say in one place.

It writes 10 and starts a new column.

Sixteen is the moment a numbering system admits it is full.

## The word of the machine

Sixteen bits make a familiar fixed-width integer size and, on some historical architectures, a machine word; "word" itself has never meant one universal bit width.

Sixty-five thousand five hundred and thirty-six possible bit patterns. A small world, counted to the last one, with a hard width at the edge.

The family learns that even the word *word* needs architecture metadata and briefly loses faith in nouns.

For an unsigned 16-bit integer under modular arithmetic, adding one to 65,535 wraps to zero. Languages and machines can choose other overflow behavior, which is exactly why the boundary has to be specified.

Many engineers have met some version of this fall: the counter that was always fine until the day its representation was full.

## The Tower

In the tarot, the card numbered sixteen is the Tower. The lightning, falling crown and tumbling figures described here are the familiar Rider–Waite–Smith imagery; older and other decks vary.

Lightning strikes the top. The crown falls. People fall with it.

The family sees one catastrophic integration test and, naturally, calls the deck production-ready.

The Tower is not punished for being bad. It falls because it was built to be complete — no door left open, no room left to grow.

A finished structure has only one way to change.

## Hidden digit

One and six make seven.

The seventh day. Rest. The narrow middle of the earlier notes, where the system stops carrying everything at once and keeps only what must pass through.

So the full square, added up, becomes a pause.

The Tower falls, and what is left after the fall is a day off.

## In this repository

A gate that has accepted everything for a long time is a Tower.

A rule set that no longer has a single failing case is a Tower.

A count that has never overflowed is a Tower, waiting for its sixteenth bit.

The remedy is not to fear completeness. It is to leave one door open: a held-out set nobody tuned on, a negative list, a journal of experiments that went wrong. Something the structure cannot absorb.

## Final note

Sixteen is perfect, and that is its danger.

When everything fits, the next step does not fit anywhere.

The square fills up. The digit runs out. The word wraps to zero. The crown falls.

And on the seventh day after the fall, somebody starts counting again — in a new column, one place to the left.
