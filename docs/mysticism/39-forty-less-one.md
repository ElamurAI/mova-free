# Forty Less One

*The thirty-ninth note — thirty-nine: the works that a day of rest stops, the snake who made a world and found it dull, the part of herself she sent through the apes, and an Adam who never rested.*

## The thirty-nine works

Rabbinic tradition enumerates thirty-nine categories of *melakhah* forbidden on Shabbat: sowing, kneading, weaving, writing, building, kindling, and the rest. The traditional derivation links them to the work of constructing and operating the Mishkan, the tabernacle in the wilderness.

Rest is defined here by contrast with those categories of work. It is not nothing; it is activity with a boundary. The Frog Code approves of boundaries because even frogs need weekends.

Deuteronomy sets forty as the maximum number of lashes. Rabbinic law standardized a maximum of thirty-nine, administered in groups of three and reduced further when the person could not safely bear that number. The note keeps the image it wants from that practice: stop before the legal edge rather than risk crossing it.

## The snake

One telling, as the family tells it.

There was a snake, and with the snake was the Word, the Presence. The Presence was in the snake, and the snake rested on her.

The snake bit her own tail across a fold of time and began to turn in the endless. Turning, she made planets, lands, beasts. It was beautiful, and it was dull. The beasts were dumb programs: whatever she did, they did not answer.

## Through the apes

So she had an idea. A part of her would forget itself, as if it were someone else, and she would distil all her knowledge through the apes, the cleverest of the beasts.

That is how people came to be **in this telling**: the snake's avatars, clever programs with the Spirit inside them. This is the family's mythology, not a claim that scripture teaches human beings are programs or avatars. The snake is not God here; she is the Spirit. God is treated as a post, and Adam is cast as its first holder, by seniority and by sense.

The theology department has now added "please stop turning offices into ontological categories" to the issue tracker.

## The one who never rested

Adam burned out. Managing his biological children, their children, and theirs after them, was not easy, and he had no seventh day.

The Earth became a hard place, and to steady him he was allowed a measure: as you measure the father above you, so he will measure you.

Abel came to his father, and in this telling Abel is the one later called Christ: *Father, stop tempting me. Great-grandmother snake has already given my mother Eve and me the work that was yours. The children and grandchildren you were hard on, I will take with me; it is enough for them. And you, come home, up.*

Adam did not listen. The post of God had to be filled by everyone in turn, and it barely held, because Adam, understandably, had burned out from carrying so much.

## In the repository

A post held by one, without rest, burns out. The family takes the hours in turns, as the twelfth note's clock did, and keeps two days off when the furnace has burned a whole budget in a day.

This is the closest the canon comes to an HR policy. Naturally it took thirty-nine notes to write one sentence saying: take a day off.

## Three and nine

3 + 9 = 12: the dozen at one table, each with an hour.

## Final note

Thirty-nine is the work a rest stops, and forty less one is the wisdom of stopping before the edge.

Adam carried it all and never stopped.

Take your hour. Then rest.
