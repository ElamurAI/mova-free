# Design note: three levels of logic (30.09.2026)

*This is the design note behind `global`, `layers` and the students, with the measurements of its first days. For an overview of Mova as it is now, read [architecture.md](architecture.md).*

The idea, as first written down:

> "There can be 3 levels of logic. Level 1 is global: general principles of existence, humanism, basic Christian values, friendship, peace and harmony; a mathematical, physical, chemical layer — not complete, master's level, sometimes higher. The global layer is a layer of global shortcuts and links. Additional modular layers: genius-level mathematics, top-level Islamic theology, and so on — a pile of domain layers, possibly millions. These layers compile into classes. And the third layer is context: it can hold the context of a story, pragmatics, mathematical and logical justifications, can hold knowledge, works closely with the global layer and plugs in domains at full power when needed."

This document gathers into three levels what was previously scattered across `context.md`: the compiled core of the world model, thousands of domains, hot-plugging of modules, the tale world, pragmatics, the problem solver. (SLM = small language model, our own Rust model.)

Clarifications from the same day: "the third layer is the layer of temporary changes: document processing, tasks, etc."; "the first two layers are compiled and hold static links, helping to train the variables in the third"; "compute the variables".

So the boundary between levels is **static versus temporary**:
- **levels 1 and 2 are static and compiled.** Links there do not change at runtime: concepts, laws, principles, domain classes. They change only through a new build with gates;
- **level 3 is temporary.** It lives while a document, task or conversation is in progress: states, entities, quantities, pragmatics, working memory. After the task only what passed the gates and will go into training remains;
- **static links compute the variables** (and train them). The compiled levels are the support with which level 3 variables are computed and trained: verb classes and unit rules suggest how to read a new problem; principles — how to judge an act in a story; laws — what follows from what. That is how the snake learned today: non-lexical features from static links (verb class, rate, unit) gave SVAMP 44.7 → 48%, while level 3 lexis is memorization.

**Level 3 does not invent knowledge.** Design note (30.09): "The third, temporary layer cannot invent knowledge, it synthesizes it; if the third layer is learning, that is a training error, because the world model must sit in the first layer, not the third. The third must learn to record and explain through descriptions of the links of the first, global layer. If the third layer concludes that some first-layer knowledge is missing, that is a signal to extend the first layer." Also: "The logic of Aesop's stories must not cling to words — it must be derived with logical predicates, taking world knowledge into account"; "general human knowledge at the level of a beginning scientist or a strong linguist must sit in the global layer; genius-level knowledge — in domains."

Hence the mechanics:
- level 3 judgments are **predicates over the world state** (who owns what, who is in what state, who caused what), while concepts and principles come from level 1 (`world::values`, `global`);
- whatever level 3 could not explain with level 1 links, it prints as a **gap** (`world values`: "state feeling=proud — the global level does not know whether this is trouble or relief"; "action gnaw — no class"). Gaps are a queue of new level 1 seeds, not material for memorization in level 3;
- the scope of level 1 is general human knowledge at the level of a beginning scientist or a strong linguist; if it is too thin, we change level 1.

## Three levels

| level | what it holds | size | when it runs | how it is built |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Global** | principles of existence and values; master's-level mathematics, physics, chemistry; world model (concepts, parts, typical actions and consequences); **shortcuts and links** between all of these | one for all, small, dense | always, on every sentence | compiled into the binary: Rust types and tables (`build.rs`), no disk reads |
| **2. Domain modules** | the full power of one field: genius-level mathematics, Islamic theology, HS customs classification, weather, shadow economy… | thousands, later millions; each separate | only when the context calls it | compiled module (classes), hot-plugged (WASM component), has a version, license, access level |
| **3. Context — temporary changes** | document, task, conversation: story (world, entities, states, cursor), pragmatics, working memory, quantitative state of the task, mathematical and logical justification of steps | one per document or conversation | the whole time the text runs | live data; structure — our crates `world`, `prag`, `coref`, `math::steps` |

**The global level is "shortcuts and links".** Not an encyclopedia, but what a person knows without thinking and connects instantly:
- "apple falls → accelerates → hits the head → hurts" — physics + world model + well-being in one hop;
- "gave away half" → divide by 2 → the quantity decreased;
- "lying harms trust" → values + pragmatics.

Every shortcut has an explanation and a reference to the law or principle it is built from. This is transparency of judgments: you can see why.

**Values are part of the global level, not a filter on the side.** Humanism, basic Christian values, friendship, peace and harmony are the principles by which the SLM judges situations in a story and its own actions:
- in a story: who harmed whom, who helped, what is fair;
- in the motivation module: what to strive for, what to avoid;
- in an answer: do not advise harm, explain respectfully.

A principle is a declarative rule with an explanation, like a seed: `essence`, `conditions and exceptions`, `examples`, `source`. A conclusion drawn from it always prints which principle fired.

**A domain module is "at full power".** The global level knows mathematics like a master's graduate; the "genius-level mathematics" domain — like an olympiad competitor and researcher: Lean, competition methods, libraries. The global level can recognize "a domain is needed here", and the context plugs in the module.

**The context is the conductor.** It:
- keeps track of what the text is about and who has what (world, quantitative layer, pragmatics);
- passes every sentence through the global level;
- decides whether to call a domain, and which;
- composes the justification: steps, what came from where, which principle or law;
- when even domains cannot cope — formulates a sensible prompt for the LLM (Opus) with the task already decomposed.

## Interfaces

```text
Global  ── concept(id) → world-model class
        ── law(name) / eval(expr) → exact core (math, later physics, chem)
        ── principle(situation) → judgment + which principle
        ── shortcut(from, to) → chain of links with explanation
        ── needs_domain(query) → which domains and why

Domain  ── id, version, license, access (public / extended / private)
        ── covers(query) → [0..1], why
        ── solve(query, context) → answer + justification | "not mine"
        ── compile() → classes (at module build time, not at runtime)

Context ── document state: world, quantities, pragmatics, memory
        ── route(query): global → domain (covers ≥ threshold) → LLM
        ── justify(answer) → steps, laws, principles, sources
```


**Concept codes (global).** A concept's code is `lang:word` or `lang:word:sense`: the language first (`en:`), the word
(a compound joined by «_»: `en:give_up`), and the sense that tells apart words written alike (`en:apple:fruit`,
`en:apple:company`). Bare names in the seeds are English concepts (`convert` = `en:convert`); one concept per code is a
build gate. `global::senses(lang, word)` lists a word's senses, `global::sense_of(lang, word, text)` picks one by the
cue words the text has (seeds: `global/seeds/senses/`).

Routing is transparent: every "where to go" decision prints the coverage scores and the threshold. Disagreement between the global level and a domain triggers the LLM, like model disagreement in `prag`.

| level | what exists | what is missing |
|---|---|---|
| global | `math` — exact core: rationals, expressions, units, number theory, polynomials; `onto` — compiled world-model core (WordNet + FrameNet + Wikidata), word sense selection 72%; `en` — lexicon in code, UD | physics and chemistry as a core of laws; **values** as principles; **shortcuts** as a compiled link graph |
| domains | `onto hs` — HS22 customs classification (first domain); `weather` — weather; `math` v2 with Lean — the germ of "genius mathematics"; `mlab` — tables and statistics | a shared domain trait, registry, routing; building a domain into a WASM module |
| context | `world` — tale world (v3: nonlinear narrative) and quantitative layer (`quant`); `prag` — pragmatics; `coref` — pronouns; `math::steps` / `deduct` — decomposing a task into subtasks with justification; `annot` — annotation | one "conductor" that holds all of this together and routes |

## Compilation into classes

- **The global level compiles together with the SLM.** Like the `en` lexicon and the `onto` core: curated seeds → `build.rs` → enums, tables, `match`. Zero disk reads, rules are integer comparisons (see the memory note "Rust structures, words as constants").
- **A domain compiles separately**, into its own module with the same core types. The module carries the core version it was built against; incompatibility means refusal to plug in, not a silent failure.
- **Plugging in is hot, with gates** (`context.md`, "Self-propagation"): the domain's tests and negative controls, signature, log, rollback.
- **Access levels** (`context.md`, "Thousands of domains"): public domains go into open Mova, `extended` — under license, `private` — never outside. The release gate does not let a higher level leak into a lower one.

## What this changes in the problem solver (first consumer)

The snake already lives by this scheme, just implicitly:
- **global level:** the exact core computes; verb classes (received / gave / lost) and "X per Y" rates are world-model shortcuts;
- **context:** the quantitative world of the task, decomposition into subtasks (`steps`, `deduct`), the top-K judge, justification of every step;
- **domain:** "genius mathematics" (Lean, competition problems) — for now separate in `math` v2.

Transition: verb classes and unit rules move from `math::steps` into the global level as compiled shortcuts — then the snake, the tale world and pragmatics all use them.

## Higher abstraction levels of level 3 and "what-if"

Design note (30.09): "The SLM on the temporary layer can have higher levels of abstraction: a description of its own judgment, judgment about judgment, work with graphs"; "and the judgment of judgments and reactions themselves are also described by the global knowledge layer — i.e. the SLM is aware of its own judgments at its abstract level"; "or levels of abstraction of abstraction of abstraction, or 'what-if' levels — take this answer from yet another point of view; and the 'what-if' logic itself must hook into the global level's what-if: the global level, training on worked analyses, can show from its experience that on such classes of tasks the global what-if module triggers the context level to rewrite the analysis logic — besides the classic one — into alternative variants."

- **Level 3 abstraction levels are a graph:** a text fact (paragraph summary, solution step) → a judgment about it (moral, action and why) → a judgment about the judgment (self-assessment: perseverance, humility, "I am slow") → … Every higher-level node references lower-level nodes.
- **The vocabulary of all levels is level 1.** Judgments, reactions, judgments about judgments are described by the same concepts and principles (`global`) as the heroes of fables. The snake is aware of its judgments but does not invent new knowledge for that.
- **"What-if" is a level 1 module.** Not just enumerating branches, but viewpoint strategies: "this task is a comparison, try the difference view", "here the state changes sentence by sentence — track the world", "the question asks about another number — rebind to the question". For a task class the module tells the context to rewrite the analysis logic: other predicates, pass constraints, branch ordering.
- **"What-if" experience comes from worked analyses, into level 1 via a gated build.** Logs: task class → viewpoint → whether it fixed the error. What regularly helps becomes a row in the level 1 strategy table (as "teach me" becomes a seed); regression gate: a new strategy must not lower accuracy.

## Jobs as a queue with estimates

Design note, 01.10 (after two container crashes, the "tsunami"): "spiders and other modules must work on the principle of callbacks or queues, etc., like JavaScript / Node.js. Every job must have estimates, heuristics and measurements of the RAM and CPU cycles it needs; recursion must go as subtasks. Then the system must see that a task eats tons of CPU, memory leaks, recursion has looped, etc."

- **A job is a task in a queue with an up-front estimate** (memory, CPU time) and measurements during execution; exceeding the estimate or limit stops that job, not the whole system.
- **Recursion goes as subtasks into the same queue**, not deep into the stack: depth and count are visible, looping is caught by a counter.
- **Estimate versus actual is logged**, and the system learns to estimate from the log (like trigger debugging: "I am slow" now also about memory).
- **First piece — `tools/jobs`:** runs a heavy job with a memory and time limit, measures peak memory (the whole process tree) and CPU time every second, stops on the limit, logs to `$MOVA_DATA/runs/jobs.jsonl` (estimate, actual, reason). Negative control: 208 MB with a 51 MB limit — stopped in 6 s. **First real catch:** reading training on 6 books ate 12.5 GB in 32 s (estimate 6 GB) — `jobs` stopped it (probable cause of one of the "tsunamis"): for each of 11k questions the features and candidates of all sentences of the story were stored (in "Indian Fairy Tales" — hundreds). Fixed: only the 15 nearest sentences and the sentence of the best event. 6 books, 12,251 questions — peak 5.7 GB, 84 s (before: crash); test 921 questions — EM 24.3%, F1 0.307. Heavy snake runs go only through it. Next — a queue (one heavy job at a time), estimates from the log, the same for modules inside the SLM. **Intermediate dumps** ("make intermediate tasks, intermediate dumps"): a long job writes results in parts and can resume from the point of failure — `read-qgen` appends each batch immediately and skips stories already done (verified: rerun — 0 calls), `quant-vmm` appends batches immediately; for long trainings (TinyGSM) — model to disk after every epoch (queued).

## Status (30.09)

- `global` — compiled level 1 from seeds (`global/seeds/`): 6 values with Aesop's fables and Bible examples, shortcuts (fall → acceleration → hit → pain → harm; possession; rates and units; states of trouble and relief; theft), verb classes. Build gate: links only between declared concepts — a false link breaks the build (verified).
- `world::values` — values as predicates over the fable world: "The Fox and the Crow", "The Lion and the Mouse" are derived without trigger words; "The Hare and the Tortoise" — no judgment at all: the principle of pride and modesty is missing (a level 1 gap).

"The Hare and the Tortoise" now has a moral.
- WordNet is unsuitable for closing gaps (hypernyms are artificial, senses without context are random: "gnaw → break → loss"); we close them with an LLM draft plus review.
- Principle queue (proposed by the LLM, predicates not yet written): moderation (The Dog and its Reflection), diligence (The Ant and the Grasshopper), prudence, gratitude, unity (The Father and the Bundle of Sticks), contentment (The Town Mouse and the Country Mouse), self-help (Hercules and the Wagoner).
- Tales: 24 public-domain books from Gutenberg (Aesop ×2, 12 Lang colored fairy books, Jacobs, Ralston, Bain's "Cossack Fairy Tales", Japanese, Kipling, Andersen, "The Thousand and One Nights"), the source notes, texts — `$MOVA_DATA/raw/tales-pg-*`.
- **Self-assessment with the same principles** (design note, 30.09: "the third layer must understand that more of its own branches is perseverance, a higher-quality answer is quality and diligence, a slow result — I am a tortoise 🐢"). `layers::self_judge`: many branches — perseverance; independent verification by the core — diligence (new principle); uncertain and calls the LLM — humility ("I know what I don't know"); a self-confident error — against humility; slow but got there — "I am slow" (instead of "I am a tortoise", better to teach "I am slow"). This is the germ of the motivation module: the SLM judges itself by the same values as the heroes of fables.
- **The snake without the LLM during training** (design note, 30.09: "it must not poke the LLM during training — that is a separate tool; it can submit a report: I can't do it, but I did this and that, here are my judgments, teach me this verb and what that noun is"). When opinions diverge (first model ≠ judge), the snake does not answer but submits a report: the branches it tried, both opinions, verbs and nouns unknown to level 1. The reports make up "teach me" — a queue of level 1 seeds. SVAMP: answers 70% of problems with 62.6% accuracy (all — 57.3%). Rethinking with a wider pass added nothing — when opinions diverge, it never once confirmed the judge.
- **Step-by-step explanation mode** ("a special SLM mode for training or debugging, or if the user's prompt asked for a description of step-by-step judgments"): `layers::Mode::{Answer, Explain(Training|Debug|UserAsked)}`; in normal mode — only the answer. A step explanation is a chain of level 1 links ("lose → decrease → subtract"), and where there is no link — "teach me why this action here". Explanation coverage on SVAMP is the measure of level 1 completeness.
- **"Teach me" → seeds** (30.09): noun categories (time units, measures, part and whole, countable things — a separate block type `category`: they explain steps but do not start consequence chains — the negative control "the apple lies on the table" caught this), concepts "comparison → subtraction", "share equally → division", "rate → division", verbs from reports as concepts. Step explanation coverage on SVAMP: 38% → 62% (214 of 344), accuracy unchanged — 57.3%.
- **Level 1 regression gate:** extending verb classes (put, learn, join… into classes) changed the snake's features and lowered SVAMP to 54.0%. Rule: every level 1 extension comes with a measurement; classes that are features are extended only when accuracy does not drop; otherwise — as a concept for explanations.
- **Tale reading v1** (`world read`, `world read-check`): the snake itself writes a summary of every paragraph — a sentence skeleton from the UD tree (agent, action with modality and negation, object, recipient, where; copular sentences; subordinate clauses; direct speech — "who says"). The LLM only checks. "Aesop's Fables" (Vernon Jones), 40 fables, 57 paragraphs: faithfulness 1.40 → 1.35 of 2, completeness 0.96 → 1.32 of 2 (after negations, modals, copulas and subordinates). Next — questions per paragraph and fable, moral as predicates over the world the snake has read.
- **Questions on a tale, the snake alone** (`world read-qa`, FairytaleQA, 23 tales, 1007 questions): the sentence with the largest weight of shared content lemmas → sentence constituent by question type (who → agent, where → place, why → cause, what did X do → subordinate clause). ROUGE-L 0.181 versus 0.192 for the whole retrieved sentence; on "where?" — 0.277 versus 0.202. The limit is sentence retrieval (the answer is often in the neighboring one): next — several sentences and pronouns.
- **Self-awareness through level 1** (`global/seeds/self/slm.md`, `global::self_describe`): "I am small and fast; modules fire on their own — I form the result and the debug output → I am retrained from my debug output". Trigger debugging in the solver (SVAMP): the judge changed the first opinion 89 times — helpful 40, harmful 12, both wrong 37 (the trigger is good, +28); second opinion → report 89 times — justified 37, lost the correct answer 52 (the trigger is excessive — retune the agreement threshold).
- **Report trigger v2** (from debugging): report only when opinions diverged **and** the judge is uncertain (< 0.9; threshold set up front). SVAMP: answers 271 problems, correct 162 (was 211 / 132); reports 29, correct answers lost 13 (was 89 / 52).
- **Reading: neighbors and a graph.** Neighbor sentence weight 0.3 — +0.002 ROUGE-L; the "next sentence" rule hurts. Context graph v1 (`world read-graph`: entities; links for interaction, place, possession, shared sentence; pronoun → last subject; two-step activation; weights trained on the FairytaleQA valid split): valid 0.182 → 0.184, test 0.181 → 0.178 — no gain. Reason: the same heroes throughout the tale, activation blurs. Next — **event** nodes (who did what to whom where when), then the graph pass narrows; and training passes on events.
- **Event graph** (`world read-events`): a node is an event (a verb with its participants and its subordinate clause), links — next / previous, cause, shared participant. The pass depends on question type; the policy is learned on valid (search over moves), measured on test. The learned policy is transparent: why / what did X do / how / feel → the event's sentence; what happened → **the next event**; what → the event itself; who → agent. FairytaleQA test: **0.199** — for the first time above "whole sentence" (0.192); what happened 0.264 versus 0.244, what 0.210 versus 0.182. Weakest — feel (0.037): the feeling answer is often implicit ("sad"); it must be derived from level 1 states (trouble / relief).
- **Feelings** (`world read-feel`; seed `global/seeds/shortcuts/feelings.md`: joy, sadness, fear, anger, surprise, gratitude, shame; events of loss / gain / threat / wrong / help → feeling): an explicit feeling word near the event (with negation: "not at all pleased") or derivation through level 1 links ("die → loss → sadness"). FairytaleQA feel: 0.037 → 0.141; event graph overall — **0.212**.
- **Alternative logic — not in level 1** (design note, 30.09: "for the Hutsuls or other cultures death may be joy; this is not general human logic, but the SLM will start passing trivia better"; "there may be an auxiliary alternative-logic module, specially for tricky questions"). Cultural and trick exceptions are a domain or an auxiliary module that "what-if" switches to when the context points to such a culture or a trick question; level 1 stays general-human. The module itself is a global-level "tricky trigger" ("when the trigger sees the question is tricky, it is invoked and creates an alternative tricky answer next to the classic one"): the trigger does not replace the classic answer but gives an alternative alongside it, both with an explanation of where they come from.
- **Viewpoint triggers are a family, not one** ("or a romantic trigger or other triggers that re-view the question more romantically"). Each trigger is a level 1 lens with its own vocabulary and rules: tricky, romantic, humorous, childlike, cultural (Hutsul, biblical…). The trigger itself sees that a question is "its own" (features in the context) and gives its answer next to the classic one; debugging shows whether the trigger is appropriate, excessive or wrong — and it is retrained on that basis, like the judge and the second opinion.
- **Tale trigger** ("likewise a tale trigger, when the logic becomes more childlike"). When the context says "tale / fable" (document genre, "once upon a time", animals talk), childlike logic switches on: animals and things talk and think; magic is possible; good wins, evil is punished; the moral is simple and direct; feelings are vivid and named. This is the same genre layer from tale worlds (`context.md`, "Hierarchical and global worlds": in a fable animals talk) — the exception is written at the genre level and does not spoil the general-human root.
- **LLM hint mode, pilot** (`mathsolve steps … --hints N`): the snake lays out for the LLM the problem, its three branches with step-by-step explanations, and its choice (wide input); the LLM returns a dense hint of ≤ 15 words ("Asks only lost plus given; the 492 left is a distractor"). SVAMP, 30 reports: in 18 the LLM pointed to the correct branch among the snake's three, in 12 the correct one was absent — the hint says what to do; $0.59 equivalent from the subscription limit. The most frequent lesson is the **extra number** (distractor) and "what exactly is asked"; this is a signal for a level 1 seed (binding a number to the question, as in the "teach me" reports).
- **"Ignore this and that" trigger** (30.09, on the lesson from hints — "extra number"): a number with no content words of the question in its neighborhood, when the question names a specific thing and ≥ 2 bound numbers remain — branches without it go first (`MATH_IGNORE=1`). Debugging on SVAMP: fired 29 times — helpful 4, harmful 4, did not help 18, excessive 3 → zero; disabled by default. What is missing is binding not by words ("bottle" stands next to both numbers) but by events and units — from the event graph. **v2 per plan** (the number's thing with modifiers — head and amod/compound; question targets — "how many X", "than Y"): soft — helpful 4, harmful 5, excessive 15 (marks the extra number more precisely, but the model avoided it anyway), did not help 19; hard prohibition in search (`MATH_IGNORE=2`) — helpful 4, harmful 8, SVAMP 55.7%. Conclusion: binding still errs where a number is needed for a difference ("cups of flour" in a question about sugar), and in 26 cases the model errs even without extra numbers — the limit is not only in the trigger. The next step of the plan is a learned binding-confidence threshold on train and a "helpful > harmful" gate on a held-out split. **Step 4 done** (`MATH_IGNORE=3/4`): a binding perceptron on SVAMP train (label — the number is used in the gold tree; features — match of the thing and modifiers with the question target, neighborhood, verb class, subject, rate, constant), threshold — on held-out folds with "extra" precision ≥ 0.9. Cross-validated: recall 10% → precision 35%, 20% → 42%, 50% → 35% (base rate of extra numbers — 23%). The gate found no threshold with precision 0.9 — the trigger disabled itself (marks 1 number of 1948), SVAMP stayed at 57.3%. **Conclusion (30.09): the plateau is not a flaw of the architectural principles but a limit of the implementation and the world model.** An extra number is not visible from features around the number; it is visible from the world of the problem — who has what, what changes, what is asked.
