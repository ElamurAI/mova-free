# Reduplication: zigzag, ping-pong, hip-hop, so-so

**Gist.** A small group of English words is formed by repeating a stem: unchanged (*pooh-pooh, goody-goody, so-so, bye-bye*), with vowel change (*zigzag, chit-chat, ping-pong, dilly-dally, shilly-shally, tip-top, sing-song, willy-nilly, riff-raff*) or with rhyme, where the first consonant changes (*hanky-panky, helter-skelter, higgledy-piggledy, hip-hop*). The meaning is onomatopoeia, repeated action, contempt or playfulness. The part of speech of such words varies and is determined by use: *zigzag* is a noun and a verb, *so-so* an adjective and an adverb, *willy-nilly* an adverb.

**Conditions and exceptions.**
- Written solid (*zigzag*) or hyphenated (*ping-pong*); in EWT a hyphenated word is one token.
- The lemma is the whole word (*zigzagged → zigzag*); reduplicated verbs inflect as ordinary regular ones (*shilly-shallied*).
- Repetition in speech for emphasis (*very very good, a big big house*) is not word formation but two separate words.

**Examples.** *The path **zigzags** up the hill* (VERB). *a **so-so** film* (ADJ). *They play **ping-pong*** (NOUN).

**In UD.** Tag and lemma as for an ordinary word of the corresponding part of speech; no special features.
