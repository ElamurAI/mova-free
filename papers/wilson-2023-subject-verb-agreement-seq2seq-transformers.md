# Subject-verb agreement with Seq2Seq transformers: Bigger is better, but still not best

**Authors:** Michael Wilson, Zhenghao Zhou, Robert Frank · **Year:** 2023 · **Venue:** Proceedings of the Society for Computation in Linguistics (SCiL) 2023
**Link:** https://aclanthology.org/2023.scil-1.24/ (ACL Anthology 2023.scil-1.24)
**License of the paper:** CC BY 4.0 (ACL Anthology) — https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/

## Summary
The paper studies what determines whether T5-style sequence-to-sequence transformers get English subject–verb agreement right, where the correct verb form depends on structure rather than on the nearest noun. Rather than comparing token probabilities, models are fine-tuned on a tense-change task: given a past-tense sentence, where number is not visible on the verb, they rewrite it in the present tense. Fine-tuning uses about a thousand sentence pairs from Wikipedia, while test sentences are generated by a PCFG and contain distractor nouns inside prepositional phrases and relative clauses; teacher forcing makes the model choose between the two verb forms. Models of different size and shape, and models pre-trained on corpora of different type and size, are compared. Larger models and larger corpora generally make fewer errors, but no model reproduces the human error pattern: models are affected more by linear than by structural proximity of the distractor. Scale alone therefore does not guarantee hierarchical generalization.

## How Mova uses it
- `ai/en/seeds/errors/plural-subject-singular-verb.md` (rule `en.errors.plural-subject-vbz`) and `ai/en/seeds/errors/singular-subject-plural-verb.md` (rule `en.errors.singular-subject-vbp`): agreement rules of the English grammar expert system (`ai/en/src/expert.rs`). The paper is cited for the observation that linear proximity of a distractor matters more than structure for neural models, and that singular subjects attract the most errors.
- The Mova rules check agreement through the dependency tree (subject relation), i.e. structurally, which is the behaviour the paper finds missing in neural models.
