# Predicative complement of the object: made him king

**Gist.** After *make, call, name, elect, appoint, consider, think, find, keep, leave, paint* the object also gets a "predicate": *They made Victoria **queen***, *I consider him **a fool***, *Custom renders the feelings **blunt***. This is not a second object: *queen* says what Victoria became. Reed & Kellogg call it an objective complement (factitive object).

**Conditions and exceptions.** With *as* (*We consider time **as** a sacred trust*, *regard X as Y*) EWT annotates the phrase as `obl` with `case(as)` (with the verb *being* — `advcl` with `mark(as)`). In the passive the predicative stays: *He was elected **president*** — `xcomp`. Resultatives (*The pond froze **solid***) are also `xcomp`.

**Examples.** *They made Victoria queen* — `obj(made, Victoria)`, `xcomp(made, queen)`. — *He painted the barn red.* — *Many Iraqis regard the date as a day of hope* (`obl` + `case as`).

**In UD.** A predicative noun or adjective on the object is an `xcomp` of the verb (not `obj`: there is no second `obj`, see `core-objects.md`).
