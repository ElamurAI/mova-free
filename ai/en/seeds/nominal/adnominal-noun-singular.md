# A noun modifier stands in the singular

**Gist.** When a noun modifies another noun (*shoe shop, apple tree*), it is usually singular, even if the meaning is plural: *toothbrush* (many teeth), *a three-volume novel, a five-foot rope, a ten-year-old boy, rose cultivation*.
**Conditions and exceptions.** There are fixed exceptions with the plural: *sales manager, sports car, arms race, savings account, systems analyst, customs officer*; with a plural head — *women writers, gentlemen farmers*. A numeral with a measure used as a modifier is also without *-s*: *a 2 year old*, *a two-hour delay*.
**Examples.** *a five-dollar bill*; *a two-hour meeting*; *sales figures* (exception).
**In UD.** The modifier is `compound` before the head. EWT 2.18: a plural `compound` noun 117 times (*weapons, sales, rights, arms*…) versus 5 343 singular. There are too many legitimate exceptions for a rule, so number inside `compound` is only a hint for a human.
**Sources.** Poutsma GLME vol. 3, Ch. XXV §31–35 (pp. 287–292 = book pp. 267–272); Poutsma GLME vol. 1, Ch. IV §8 (p. 292: *iron bedstead*, *Gladstone bag* — not apposition).
